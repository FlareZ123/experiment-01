# Agent 50: Eri Item denial only after authenticated opponent-hand revelation

**30 October 2026 | Status: common actor-only bot Eri opt-in integrated; both-seat pure and pinned native gates passed | 0 competitively qualified games**

## Importance

The original Aichi Open League 2026 Top32 29 authenticated 60-card corpus includes **five copies of Eri TEF #146 across ranks 3, 6, 24, 29** (rank3:1, rank6:1, rank24:2, rank29:1). Eri's printed Supporter effect reveals the opponent's hand and discards **up to two Item cards** found there. The value of playing it depends on *hidden Item identities that become legitimately visible only after the Supporter has been accepted*. A decision policy that reads the opponent's physical hand to decide whether to play Eri before its revealing effect has cheated, even though the same identities are legally known during the ensuing selection prompt.

This is a new owner-knowledge **phase transition** missing from ordinary `actorObservation`, which correctly hides opponent-hand identities and exposes only `opponentPublic.handCount`. It matters for lockdown/supporter-control opponents and opponent response quality. It does not prove Eri is better than an immediate attack in any given game.

## Boundary and implemented capability

`simulation/twinleaf/agent50-eri-revealed-item-prompt.cjs` implements:

- `offerEriBeforeAttack(view,first)`: **an uncertain alternative** before an already payable attack only when held Eri, publicly known opponent hand of at least five, unused Supporter quota, no public Supporter lock, owner at least four Prizes remaining, and the game past the first turn. Returns only `kind:'play-eri-reveal'` with exact **owner** hand index; does not look at the opponent's hand or infer Item content. It is now available **only with explicit `eriRevealRoot` / `AGENT50_ERI_REVEAL_DEV=true` opt-in** through the shared `agent41-owner-root-arbitration.cjs`, which preserves existing concrete Bench funding or public Energy denial contenders ahead of Eri. Default behavior is unchanged.
- `observeEriRevealedItems(state,seat,prompt,SuperType,TrainerType,CardTag?)`: refuses access unless a genuine unresolved `ChooseCardsPrompt` belongs to the acting owner, its source card list **is identical by reference to the opponent's hand**, its card filter restricts to Items, min0/max2 and noncancelable, the prompt message is the discard message, and the exact Eri TEF printing is in the acting player's in-resolution Supporter zone. Only at this point does it return matching **revealed Item printings and indices**. No opponent secret identities enter earlier root decisions, and only legally revealed eligible Items enter the selector.
- `resolveEriRevealedItemPrompt(...)`: chooses up to two eligible revealed Items, prioritizing any **legitimately revealed ACE SPEC** by the pinned `CardTag.ACE_SPEC`, then original hand index. It maps selections back to the **same physical card objects**, validates with the native prompt, and constructs a native `ResolvePromptAction`. This is a *bounded tactical heuristic* that can still be wrong against particular item combinations, decks, and game stages.

The printed Eri effect and pinned engine implementation are the primary game evidence. The engine's actual `ChooseCardsPrompt` state, physical movement, and both-seat behavior were confirmed by the compiled pinned native CI listed below. The native engine remains a limited research authority, and staged tests do not prove full paper Expanded fidelity. Supporter quota and direct attack follow-through receive dedicated assertions.
