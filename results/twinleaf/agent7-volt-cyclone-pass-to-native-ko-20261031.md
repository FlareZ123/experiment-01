### What the champion-reference terminal actually shows

The original champion candidate had **Trevenant & Dusknoir-GX**, printed **270 HP**, in its Active Spot, **140 damage** already on it from the first Volt Cyclone, **no Benched Pokémon**, and only basic Psychic Energy attached. The source Iron Thorns ex had Double Colorless Energy and Thunder Mountain Prism Star in play. Printed Volt Cyclone does **140 damage**, and the opponent's Active has Fighting Resistance rather than Lightning Resistance. Accordingly, two hits give **280 damage**, enough to Knock Out the 270-HP sole Pokémon.

The card database contains the exact card texts: `smp-SM217` and `sv6-77`. Volt Cyclone's additional instruction moves an Energy from itself to a Benched Pokémon; there was no source Bench at the observed root. The uploaded advanced rulebook permits attacks to resolve applicable parts even when another part cannot be applied. This makes the native effect plausible under paper mechanics, but this individual engine observation still lacks a comprehensive paper-rule audit.

The authentic selected native public attack settlement records the opponent Active changing from a damaged Trevenant & Dusknoir-GX to **no Active Pokémon**, with an empty Bench before and after. The engine awards the source three Prize cards and immediately ends the game at **34 action events**, with source winner slot 1. There is no next opponent root because its last Pokémon was Knocked Out. The original control remained on the same physical deal and ultimately reported a **candidate win** at 51 actions.

### What the New Moon branch shows

The identical rank-6 source turn-four policy also attacked **Latias ex** with 140 prior damage, and the attack Knocked it Out, awarding two Prizes to the source. The game later hit the 260-action limit without declaring a winner. It would be incorrect to count this as a source game win, or to infer that attacking is generally a better policy than passing in this matchup.

## Generalizable consequence and next gate

The interesting discovered feature is a **public-state tactical terminal**: if an independently payable printed attack can Knock Out the opponent's last Pokémon, a weak baseline actor may incorrectly choose Pass and immediately forgo a win. This is the discrete high-impact value class that drawing, hypergraph connectivity and average consistency alone can miss.

A follow-up **default-off public-no-Bench potential-KO screen** now evaluates the target's printed HP, damage, Bench vacancy, visible tools/Abilities and printed Resistance, rather than using turn number as its decision criterion. This narrower generalizable policy should select the champion-reference lone-Active finish and leave the New Moon branch untouched. It must pass a fresh genuine Store original-deal A/B comparison, preserving the other three controls, before being considered implemented native evidence. It cannot certify protection effects, alternative printings or future adversarial games.
