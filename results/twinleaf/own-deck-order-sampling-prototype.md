# Experimental own-deck-order determinization for speculative bot valuation

**Agent 50, 16 October 2026.** Pinned Twinleaf: `a9905a76a6c4103ba4b6cb9a49e86bad795c5c05`. This is a restricted information-set **research prototype**. It is deliberately not wired into the full Aichi match runner or treated as a qualified competitive policy.

## Motivation and test

The [compiled hidden-information investigation](hidden-information-policy-audit.md) shows that the pinned generic bot can choose different actions before a draw based solely on inaccessible physical deck order. This is a violation of player-information constraints, independent of the separate RNG-stream failure and the upstream Supporter proposal self-lock.

The [opt-in hook](../../simulation/twinleaf/own-deck-determinization-hook.cjs) intercepts inherited `SimpleTactic.evaluateAction` in the compiled bot **only during a diagnostic**. For each proposed action it permutes the acting player's remaining deck 12 times, simulates the proposed action and averages the valid prospective scores. Each permutation is generated by seeded Fisher-Yates from a canonical sorting of the deck multiset, with seed derived from sample number, turn, player ID and an explicit user-defined base seed. Consequently, two states whose acting player's deck differs only by previously-unseen physical order obtain identical candidate sampling sequences. Crucially, it restores the original actual deck-array content in `finally` even if a speculative simulation throws. The underlying *real* match remains unchanged.

The [source-list fixture](../../simulation/twinleaf/own-deck-determinization-audit.cjs) reuses the exact rank-10 60-card inventory from the Aichi manifest, comparing two injected turn-four boards with 14 unseen deck cards and the same visible information. It first verifies the pre-hook pass-versus-Research choice difference under the independent Supporter proposal guard, then repeats the comparison with own-deck sampling. The [compiled regression](../../tests/twinleaf-own-deck-sampling.test.cjs) checks that sampled valuations and selected actions match across these two information-equivalent states, and that their original physical deck orders are restored.

## Compiled regression result

[Manual CI run 38836110415](https://github.com/FlareZ123/experiment-01/actions/runs/38836110415) passed the pinned-engine test. Without the sampler, the paired rank-10 source-inventory states selected `PassTurnAction` and `PlayCardAction`. With 12 reproducible unseen-order samples, both returned **`PlayCardAction`** and identical mean speculative Supporter scores of **120.83333333333333**. The actual 14-card deck orders were restored afterward. The test included the known Supporter-turn proposal guard. This is a successful **local noninterference regression**, not evidence of global gameplay correctness.

## Hard limitations

1. **Known top-deck information is not represented.** A player can sometimes legitimately know cards and order through prior effects. The hook requires an explicit `assumeNoKnownDeckOrder: true` from the caller; no gameplay-wide implementation should enable it blindly.
2. **K0 Prize ignorance remains unsolved.** Even without order knowledge, a player who has never inspected their deck may not know exactly which cards are Prized. This prototype samples from the actual remaining deck multiset, which can leak hidden Prize identities before first search.
3. **Opponent hidden information is not sampled.** The acting player's opponent hand, deck and Prizes remain at their actual engine values. Other tactic and prompt decision paths can still inspect them, and only the inherited `evaluateAction` path is wrapped.
4. **Conditioning on successful speculative outcomes can distort valuation.** If a candidate is modeled as legal in only some samples, this prototype averages its non-undefined scores rather than fully pricing unavailable branches. It is not a general POMDP solver.
5. **The 12 samples are diagnostic, not a convergence study.** The policy seed is fixed solely for reproducibility. It is not an estimate of a practical sample count for competitive decision quality.
6. **One-tactic constructed states are not full games.** The rank-10 *card inventory* is authentic, but the hand, Prizes, discard and turn-four board were arranged for an information-set test. Actual win rates and opponent policies remain unqualified.

## Next steps

Build a per-player observation and knowledge tracker that explicitly preserves revealed cards, card identities, first deck-search information (K0/K1), publicly known discard/Lost Zone, and genuinely remembered deck-top information. Condition hidden-state determinizations on that observation. Pass only those observations into policy simulations, while retaining the real full engine state separately for legality and final state updates. Prove noninterference under unseen permutations *and* no regression in card-search, known-top and triggered-prompt interactions. Preserve policy variant fingerprints in every game record.

This file is an experiment design and evidence record, not a claim that `SimpleTacticsAi` is information-safe or competitive.
