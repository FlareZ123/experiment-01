# Mechanism behind the rank-6 Farewell Letter hidden-deck-score contrast

**Agent 50, 20 October 2026.** This is source-code inspection paired with retrospective *detached* scores, not a full native decision-policy proof or a game win. The pinned engine is Twinleaf `a9905a76a6c4103ba4b6cb9a49e86bad795c5c05` and must remain unmodified; put any intervention in `simulation/twinleaf/` adapters.

## Verified pinned upstream implementation

The exact pinned upstream `ptcg-server/src/simple-bot/simple-tactics/simple-tactics.ts` defines `evaluateAction(state, playerId, action)` by calling `simulateAction`, then calculating `this.stateScore.getScore(newState, playerId)` on the **one resulting physical-deck State**. Its `simulateAction` calls `new Simulator(state, this.options.arbiter)` and dispatches the action, resolving pending prompts. `ptcg-server/src/simple-bot/simple-tactics/use-ability-tactic.ts` enumerates in-play printed Abilities, calls `evaluateAction` and compares to the current state score. `ptcg-server/src/simple-bot/state-score/state-score.ts` adds `HandScore` and other components. `hand-score.ts` scores cards by their actual types and may add energy, Pokémon, supporter or evolution-presence bonuses. Thus an ability that draws from an unknown deck can have distinct hypothetical score if the physical card at the top changes.

The supplied card database's Unown XY7-30 (`Unown AOR`) **Farewell Letter** states the player can discard that Benched Unown and attached cards, then draw one card. The prior first-finite-root v4 [compiled artifact](https://github.com/FlareZ123/experiment-01/actions/runs/39367612404/artifacts/12239792618) at historical `6:0:A:new-moon-lockbreaker-v1` reported the same exact `UseAbilityTactic` Farewell Letter action and target, with **50** when the candidate physical deck remained in archived order and **51** with its order reversed. Both independently reconstructed bot worlds chose Pass, and the genuine live bot chose Pass. The difference therefore concerns a *counterfactual score*; no observed live action changed.

## Interpretation and safe policy requirement

A search/simulator is allowed to model uncertain draws. The *real* player may know remaining deck membership after a genuine search (K1), but physical draw order remains uncertain without card effects that reveal or fix that order. An evaluation of a decision at an order-unknown actor root must use a belief over possible draw outcomes, not select the one physical future card already stored in the engine. Formally, for owner-visible information I and action a, the desirable quantity is `E[V(after(a, nextDraw)) | I]`, including both benefits and costs of discarding the Unown and releasing a Bench slot. This is a model objective rather than a claim that any given real game should use Farewell Letter.

The cached physical `State` is a useful game-engine oracle for **legality and effect resolution after accepting a player action**. It is an unsafe input to an information-set-safe optimizer's *private* counterfactual ranking when it exposes a concealed card order or concealed opponent hand. The actor-only projected input contract at `simulation/twinleaf/actor-observation-contract.cjs` is the proper boundary for a replacement evaluator. The same problem can occur for draw Supporters and Item searches whenever simulations condition scores on concealed next-card identities.
