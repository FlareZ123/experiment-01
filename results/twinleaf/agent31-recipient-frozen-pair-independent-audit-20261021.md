## First authentic rank-11 connector decision: Fog Crystal chose Duskull while the Perfection donor was in deck

The recipient-flag comparison also preserved a precise **turn-1, source-dealt root decision** relevant to attacking policy. In the native rank-11 New Moon game, candidate physical seat B (actual first player) opened with Duskull Active and Mewtwo & Mew-GX Benched. Root **7** played **Fog Crystal CRE**, and its real deck-search prompt at root **8** selected **Duskull VIV**. The actor's **first legitimate own-deck-search K1 audit**, preserved under `policyAudit.components.fieldBlower.events`, simultaneously recorded exactly **one Gengar & Mimikyu-GX in the own deck**, none in candidate hand, Bench or discard. Candidate root **13** then physically Benched the searched Duskull on that same first turn. Root 8 is an actual native-accepted choice under the pinned Store, corroborated independently by `nativePromptChoices.events`, `candidateOwnDeckSelectionEvidence`, and `actorOnlyDecisionCoverage.detailedComponentAudits.fog.postSearchPhysicalEvents`.

The source-pool `Fog Crystal` (`swsh6-140`) can retrieve either a Psychic Energy or a **Basic Psychic Pokémon**. The pool's **Gengar & Mimikyu-GX `sm9-53`** is Basic, Psychic type and has `Poltergeist` at [Psychic,Psychic] and `Horror House-GX` at [Psychic]. Therefore **Gengar & Mimikyu-GX was an identifiable, eligible search alternative if its live printed card satisfied the native prompt validator**. The combined records did not log a complete list of all validated alternatives, so keep the last validator clause as a bounded caveat. If retrieved, Gengar would still need a legal Bench or discard placement, an Active and energized Mewtwo, usable Perfection and GX quota, and a subsequent genuine attack settlement.

The live search adapter [`agent9-fog-actor-prompt.cjs`](../../simulation/twinleaf/agent9-fog-actor-prompt.cjs) exposes the reason the root choice can be strategically arbitrary: `chooseFogPrinting` takes the `offered` cards **in the underlying shuffled physical deck-array order**, then uses `eligible.find(row=>openBench&&row.basicPsychic)`. It ignores the acting player's available Perfection donor, energy requirements and tactical attack plan. Source inspection establishes deterministic **permutation sensitivity**: with identical eligible multiset [Duskull VIV, Gengar & Mimikyu-GX TEU] and an open Bench, the function picks Duskull if Duskull appears first and picks Gengar if the two physical deck positions are exchanged. The underlying paper game allows the player to inspect the deck's membership during search; it does not provide a strategic reason to prioritize the first arbitrarily ordered Basic Psychic card. Do not mistake this algorithmic position bias for rational donor selection or label it a confirmed rules violation.

This is a materially narrower, authentic **connector-domination opportunity** than the general energy-recipient diagnosis: one available Item search selected an ordinary second Duskull rather than a singleton GX copying payload on an already-established Mewtwo Bench. A future policy comparison should fix only that one owner-search choice on the *same genuine source deal*, then inspect the actual Bench/discard donor, subsequent energy assignment, legal pivot into Mewtwo, chosen copied attack and opponent response. There is currently no native replay of that counterfactual, so its winning value is **unproven**.
