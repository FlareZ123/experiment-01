Printed `Technical Machine: Crisis Punch` (PAF 90) requires exactly one opponent Prize remaining, so none of these attachments was immediately attack-eligible on observed Prize state. The Tool card ordinarily discards at its controller's turn end. Absence of a Crisis Punch declaration in eight games is not a rigorous proof that the attack was never legally available in any state.

**Interpretation:** the generic Tool-awareness opt-in made no recorded decision difference in these source-dealt rank-26 diagnostics. A more promising research question is *when the opponent bot burns its limited Crisis Punch Tools without the printed Prize prerequisite*, and whether preserving them improves genuinely reachable winning lines. This is conditional resource management, adjacent to state-dependent discardability/UDP. No simulation win rates are qualified.
