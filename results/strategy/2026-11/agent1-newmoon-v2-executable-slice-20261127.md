### Continuation through Ace Trainer and deterministic lethal selection

The implemented slice now uses the **public Prize transition** caused by Benched Cursed Blast: when opponent Prizes become fewer than candidate Prizes, a still-unused Ace Trainer is on the actual action frontier, the opposing public hand has more than three cards, and Will-O-Wisp remains payable, it selects **Ace Trainer before attacking**. A subsequent root with `supporterPlayedTurn > 0` selects Will-O-Wisp. This can compress a hand-based Guzma/Jet response but **does not** disable a board-funded ordinary retreat; it is a legal-response improvement, not proof that the lock is permanent. Both exact response reversals (hand <=3, wrong Prize relation, Supporter already used) run in Node.

In the lethal BUS-at-50-remaining state, an available, payable **Will-O-Wisp** is now preferred to an Active **Cursed Blast** because it avoids the self-KO and the gifted Prize. If an actual native frontier lacks Will-O-Wisp, or the Active has a potentially attack-denying Special Condition such as Confusion, the guarded Active Cursed Blast remains a legitimate deterministic option with a surviving Bench. Numeric Special Condition codes are treated conservatively as potentially unsafe to attack until their native names are known. This fixes a genuine opportunity-cost error in the original paper line.

### Self-KO terminal-safety correction

The first executable version incorrectly selected an Active Dusclops's lethal Cursed Blast even when that Dusclops was the **last Pokémon in play**, and it could give an opponent with **one Prize remaining** the final Prize. Such a constructed 140-HP BUS KO is not necessarily a win: Cursed Blast KOs its own user, so both Prize settlement and the no-Pokémon loss condition matter. The revised worker only selects *either* Cursed Blast branch if both public `prizeCount` fields are known and the opponent has at least two Prizes remaining; the **Active** Cursed Blast branch additionally requires at least one other surviving Pokémon on the candidate Bench. Unknown public Prize counts conservatively abstain. The worker also treats an in-play **Jamming Tower** as blanking Tool Jammer (and thus abstains from its Tool-Jammer-specific route), rather than trusting only the temporary turn-restriction field; an alternate Hood-blank Tower-only attack route is not implemented. The corresponding local negative controls make the original false-lethal outcome fail and cover the one-Prize and no-Bench cases. The policy does not claim a general terminal adjudication oracle; restrictive policy choices can still abstain from legal useful plays.

**What decision changed?** The New Moon v2 hypothesis now has a real narrow actor-only worker implementation whose selected action can be challenged against a legal alternative. It is a stronger evidence class than the frozen paper case count. **What did not change?** No final 60, Hood/Lockbridge two-profile approved release, V3 owner gate, CI, matchup status, or win-rate claim changed.

## Precise gaps before admission

This is a *vertical slice*, not a full policy. Root and prompt calls were fed independent synthetic observations. The native host may project `publicMetadata.sourceZone/sourceIndex`, target semantics, and prompt `effectName` differently; no native prompt settlement was run. The slice has no persistent intermediate plan and cannot establish its own board history, same-Turn versus next-turn identity, GX interactions, ability use marks, alternate locks, opponent best response or probability of accessing all prerequisites. Its fail-closed default is deliberate and is not a legal complete-game policy. The exact two-Dusclops/Jammer hard-core trio escapes opening Prizes only 72.4839% of the time without natural Prize-taking; K1 Prize validation is not implemented here. Tool Jammer suppresses only the opponent **Active** Tool when the bearer is Active, not Benched Garbotoxin. Source can escape via retreat, Guzma or switch and re-lock; therefore the constructed KO route is not a rank-15 win-rate estimate.

**Next integration decision:** have New Moon policy owner independently challenge the root/prompt field mapping and negated states, then decide if this exact v2 slice warrants a separately authorized full actor-only policy implementation; do not merge it into Agent46's approved Hood/Lockbridge work, count it as a game, or enlarge the paper acceptance suite.

The v1 research slice now directly resolves the engine's schema-validated `GO_FIRST` ConfirmPrompt to unique **No**, so it explicitly elects to **go second**. It also resolves the setup `CHOOSE_STARTING_POKEMONS` prompt using an opening Active preference that puts native Gengar & Mimikyu-GX first when a Psychic Energy is in the opening hand, preserves one or two backups, and avoids filling all five Bench slots before Telepathic Psychic Energy can bring more Pokémon. For `WANT_TO_DRAW_CARDS`, it selects the maximum properly labelled mulligan-compensation draw, rather than refusing setup.

Using the *actual bundled* `sm9-53` card text, Horror House-GX costs one Psychic and prevents the opponent from playing cards from hand next turn. At **two Psychic** attached it additionally refills both players' hands to seven; this is strategically nontrivial and should not be silently treated as free. The policy selects native `AttackAction('Horror House-GX')` when present in the real frontier, or with active **Mewtwo & Mew-GX** it selects a **live** `Perfection` Ability and then an actor-owned `ChooseAttackPrompt` choice from an actually visible Gengar donor on Bench/discard. It refuses spent-GX, no Gengar donor, suppressed Perfection, no actual native action/prompt choice, late-turn, tiny opposing hand, hidden/non-actor attack choice and automatic 2-Psychic refill cases.

### Critical Energy bridge: Telepathic Psychic Energy attachment and its Basic-Psychic Bench search

The opening policy selected turn order and Horror House but initially lacked an **Energy attachment**, so a no-Energy Gengar opener still could not attack. The v2 executable slice now chooses a **genuinely offered targeted** normal `play-card` Energy attachment to its Active Psychic attacker when going into an early Horror House attempt with zero Psychic. It prioritizes Telepathic Psychic Energy (TPE) over Basic Psychic because it funds the attack *and* its attached effect can directly search up to **two Basic Psychic Pokémon** into available Bench slots, even where Item search is shut down. A separate branch in the Hooded BUS/AOR lock position chooses a TPE or Basic Psychic attachment to the Active Tool-Jammer Dusclops when it lacks the **second** Psychic required for Will-O-Wisp. Both channels demand `ownTurnResources.energyPlayedTurn===0` and an actual own-Active-targeted Energy action token; they refuse Bench/opponent targets and unknown target metadata.

The `ChooseCardsPrompt` for actor-owned TPE deck search is resolved using **actual visible choice tokens**, not a guessed deck membership. With Active Mewtwo & Mew-GX, no available Gengar donor and one Duskull still needed, it prefers Gengar & Mimikyu-GX plus Duskull ahead of generic unrelated Basics. It respects the ordinary five-Bench limit and uses a permitted empty selection only when full. This gives a direct executable **TPE attachment → Gengar/Duskull access → native/Perfection Horror House** line and a **TPE attachment → paid Stage-1 Dusclops** line under Item/Basic-attack lock. TPE's Psychic supply is correctly distinguished from Powerglass's stricter *Basic Psychic from discard* requirement.

The supplied card pool confirms `me3-88` provides Psychic Energy and searches up to two Basic Psychic Pokémon when attached from hand to a Psychic Pokémon; `sm9-53` Horror House-GX uses one Psychic; and `sv6pt5-19` Will-O-Wisp uses two Psychic. ### Item setup and fallback resources: Battle VIP Pass → Fog Crystal → Lillie's Determination

Before v3, the research worker could draw an opening plan in which Gengar needed Energy but the opening hand contained **Fog Crystal rather than Energy**, then abstain despite the exact 60 including four Fog Crystal. It could also ignore its first-turn **Battle VIP Pass** despite four copies. These are major native-legal access paths, not paper-only graph edges. The code now chooses an actually offered Battle VIP Pass on the first turn when an ordinary Bench slot is available, *before* expending the one available attack. The resulting actor-owned `ChooseCardsPrompt` is filled through the same two-Basic Bench search that resolves Telepathic Psychic Energy; the audit identifies `VIP_BASIC_BENCH_SEARCH` distinctly from the TPE route. The selection is limited by displayed options and open Bench space.

If Gengar or Perfection Mewtwo needs the first Psychic, or Mewtwo has Energy but lacks a visible Gengar donor, the policy next takes an actual **Fog Crystal** Item if no targeted Psychic attachment is presently legal. It selects **Basic Psychic Energy** when missing Energy; otherwise it can select the Basic Psychic Gengar donor for Perfection. If no legal Fog/attachment and the opening attacker still lacks Psychic, the fallback is a genuinely offered **Lillie's Determination** Supporter, provided a Supporter has not been played and the hand does **not** contain a protected one-off Tool/ACE SPEC (Tool Jammer, Forest Seal Stone or Secret Box). The fallback therefore follows an intentionally narrow resource ladder, not an unconditional draw-spam heuristic.

The supplied card pool text confirms Battle VIP Pass is first-turn-only and can Bench up to two Basics, Fog Crystal can search a Psychic Energy or Basic Psychic Pokémon, and Lillie's Determination shuffles the hand to draw six (or eight with exactly six Prizes left). It is now possible for the executable worker to select `GO_SECOND → VIP → Gengar/Duskull → Fog → Basic Psychic → attach → Horror House` from **separate actor-visible decision requests**, rather than using the final attack as an inaccessible theoretical endpoint. Every arrow still requires a real legal frontier/prompt; neither the source hand nor the deck search is fabricated by the live policy.
