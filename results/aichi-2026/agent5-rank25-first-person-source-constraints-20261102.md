# Rank-25 Alolan Raichu: first-person matchup and decision constraints (2 November 2026)

## Scope and provenance

The **exact original printed 60** for Aichi Open League final rank 25 remains **unrecovered**. Do not substitute a teammate's top-four list, a later deck, or a generic Raichu Electrode Combo list. The published [Limitless result](https://limitlesstcg.com/decks/282/results) identifies **rank 25: Kazuki Kumegawa, Raichu Electrode Combo**. The [Pokekameshi Aichi Extra Top32 page](https://pokekameshi.com/cl2026aichidec-extra/2/) separately identifies a Top32 **kume** Alolan Raichu player and links a [first-person May 14 note](https://note.com/kume_pokeca/n/n6cfd126f5774). The note says the author qualified from **Swiss place 25**. The alignment is strong, but the English-name/alias crosswalk itself is not independently documented here.

The first-person note has a **public introductory portion** and a **paid deck-construction portion**. This report uses only its publicly displayed text. The public portion does not disclose a complete original 60-card list or a verified deck code. It therefore closes **none** of the missing-list denominator.

## Source-reported matches, not an estimated win rate

In the author's public first-person match record, Swiss rounds were:
- vs Regidrago: rounds 1 W, 2 W, 4 L, 5 W, 7 W: **four wins in five played matches**;
- vs Lightning toolbox: round 3 W;
- vs Roaring Moon: round 6 W;
- vs Arceus/Dragonite (三神カイリュー): round 8 W.

Reported Swiss result **7-1**, Swiss qualifying placement **25**. The author then reports a **Top32 elimination loss vs Mega Absol/Garbodor**. These are *self-reported historical observations*, not reconstructed games, not independent randomized samples, and not reproducible game-win probabilities. The five Regidrago games are especially relevant to opponent-response policy, but they cannot establish a 4/5 true matchup rate.

## Actionable public opponent-policy information

The author describes the archetype's proposed matchup plan against Regidrago as having an answer to both starting seats:
- **Going first:** pressure the opponent's early Basic Pokémon Ability engine with a lock (Klefki considered alongside Wobbuffet and Garbodor paths), reducing access to Latias ex and Tapu Lele-GX and the threat of an early Budew attack.
- **Going second:** manage opponent Path to the Peak, Sudowoodo, or Wobbuffet states while surviving/avoiding Regidrago's next attacking turn; public candidate answers include Marshadow, Escape Rope or Canceling Cologne, and disruption or Horror House-GX.
- The public deck concept is a **two-turn Alolan Raichu one-shot** with an early lock/denial phase. Later attack and Bench/retreat decisions cannot be rated against a generic early-pass source player.

The author weighs different first-turn lock options and says Klefki was preferred in the construction reasoning because it affects Latias ex and Tapu Lele-GX; this **does not establish the final printed card quantity**. The note's descriptions of Dedenne-GX, Carmine and Regidrago's setup are in part *opposing-deck analysis*. They must **not** be imported as alleged cards in rank25's 60. The page also discusses environmental assumptions about Regidrago share (roughly half) as the author's pre-event expectation; they are not observed event proportions.

## Quantified danger of borrowing a different source 60

The **two already authenticated Alolan Raichu original 60s** are rank **4 Shu Hosoda** and rank **17 Yuya Yamano** in [the repository's printed-roster JSON](full-decklists.json). Comparing complete card-name multisets (summing the smaller per-card-name count across the two 60s) yields exactly **40 common physical slots**, hence **20 substitutions out of 60**, affecting **22 card names**. This is a reproducible count derived from the two complete public decks, **not** an assertion about rank25.

Examples: rank4 plays **2 Dedenne-GX, 2 Crobat V, 4 Quick Ball, 3 Sky Field and 1 Forest Seal Stone**; rank17 plays **4 Unown, 4 Battle VIP Pass, 4 Rescue Carrier, 1 PokéStop and 1 Marshadow**, and plays none of those five named rank4 packages (other than cards listed separately). Both lists carry **1 Klefki**, **1 Gengar & Mimikyu-GX**, **3 Electrode-GX**, **2 Alolan Raichu**, and **4 each of Reversal, Counter and Unit Energy LPM**. An action policy built around the rank4 Dedenne/Crobat/Quick Ball/Sky Field engine does **not** automatically transfer to rank17's Unown/VIP/Carrier engine. Rank25's unrecovered list could be either, a hybrid, or another design. Its own author's first-person strategy discussion alone cannot identify its actual printed package.

## Implication for the fixed-60 benchmark

1. Keep rank25 labelled **missing original roster**, even if its tactical outline is available. A top-four Alolan Raichu list is a potential *surrogate policy-development fixture only*, with independent fingerprint and explicit exclusion from the exact rank25 denominator.
2. An eventual rank25 source driver needs separately audited **first/second starter choices**, early ability-lock pressure, a genuine Horror House or comparable denial line if present in its actual printed deck, and an actual Raichu Prize-winning continuation. An unconditional generic passing driver will be adversarially inadequate.
3. The first-person Regidrago matchup history is a useful **qualitative policy falsification target**: a simulator in which comparable source builds cannot take meaningful anti-Regidrago actions should be diagnosed before ranking fixed challengers.
4. Record exact physical target/Zone, lost/supporter restrictions, real Store prompt choices, printed randomness and 25-minute Open League clock witnesses. This report certifies **no** native action and **zero** competitive games.
