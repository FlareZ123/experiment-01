## Conditional ordering principle: observe before expensive irreversible expenditure

Let `I` be a genuine owner-deck search such as a Quick Ball search for Trubbish, already required later in the intended turn. Let `C` be an irreversible expensive commitment such as playing Secret Box and physically discarding three other cards. Let `Z` be the hidden card availability state and `O` the information obtained only while native `I` searches its owner's deck. Suppose all three properties hold in the actual state:

1. `I` can be executed before `C` with its complete paper cost, and `I` will reveal relevant `Z` while the player still has the choice of whether to play `C`.
2. If `C` would succeed, `I → C` preserves at least the legal, realistically fundable tactical line and payoff of `C → I`, including discard choices, Bench space, Tool access, and remaining Supporter/Item usage.
3. If `C` cannot support its intended result, the player has a non-worse option after `I` that preserves `C` and its payment, subject to any value lost by executing `I` first.

Under these **conditional** assumptions, the contingent policy `I → [play C only when the newly known state makes it worthwhile]` weakly dominates an unconditional `C → I` policy for the same initial physical deal. This follows by strategy replication when `C` remains worthwhile and the preserved optional refusal when it does not. Strict improvement requires a positive-probability failure state in which preserving the costly card/resources actually matters.

**The conditions cannot be silently assumed.** Swapping actions changes discard options, searchable Pokémon availability, hand size, native event triggers, shuffled deck order, and possible fallback decisions. `I` may consume the precise fourth discard needed to play `C`; a pure search-access hypergraph would miss that. The owner could also have an alternate productive use for `C` even if Shake is missing. Search timing and opponent-visible Pokémon can change the strategic payoff. This is a formal *testable sufficient condition*, not a universal Pokémon TCG law.

For a single Shake in a randomly shuffled 60, six fixed Prize cards, and `h` already identified distinct non-Prize physical cards known not to be Shake before a first search, a simplified exchangeability model gives

\[P(\text{Shake in Prizes}\mid\text{Shake not among the }h\text{ known cards})=\frac{6}{60-h}.\]

For illustrative `h=8`, this is **11.53846%**. It is an *illustrative prior*, not a measured failure rate, and depends on setup, initial mulligans, conditional held cards and any turn actions. No native root legitimately knows its actual Prize inventory at K0. The original 64 owner-only native deal cohort found 9/64 actual Tool-Garbodor firstturn setups and the separately selected canonical 32 found one K1 missing-Shake failure; differing policies/deals prevent pooling these as a clean probability estimate.

## Follow-up: lower-cost first-search connectors in the actual rank8 paper printed 60

The negative [64 matched-deal native comparison](agent44-original-rank8-preregistered64-prebox-scout-matched-native-20261102.md) now establishes **zero net native setup-completion difference** (five successful firstturn Tool-Garbodor and one failed Box in each arm, 58 non-offers), despite one prospective successful Quick Ball-first search. The sole prospective K1-negative source deal, index461, already had Trubbish in play, so the deliberately narrow trigger requiring a Quick Ball to obtain Trubbish did not apply. The current code should remain default-off. The result motivates investigating a broader but *conditionally useful* class of prior searches that advance the intended board even when Trubbish is already established.

The supplied exact original rank8 printed list contains **two Battle VIP Pass, one Adventure Bag, one Pokémon Communication, two Ultra Ball, and four Quick Ball**, in addition to Secret Box/Shake. The attached card database provides the following concrete printed effect structures:

| Earlier owner search | Source printed effect | Potentially fundable K0→K1 line | Main contention or failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Battle VIP Pass** (FST 225, 2 copies) | On *your first turn*, search up to two Basic Pokémon and put onto Bench | When actual Bench slots are free and a Trubbish or useful Basic is needed, playing Pass before Box both improves the board and reveals whether unique Shake remains in deck | First-owner-turn limitation, Bench space, Item lock; may overfill Bench and compromise other payoff |
| **Adventure Bag** (LOT 167, 1 copy) | Search deck for up to two Pokémon Tools and put into hand | When the Garbodor line already needs a Float Stone or Muscle Band, Bag-before-Box obtains the Tool with no discard payment and reveals Shake K1 | The Tool might be unavailable or already held; spending Bag may forgo another Tool choice, or be dead under lock |
| **Quick Ball** (FST 237, 4 copies) | Discard one other card, search for a Basic Pokémon | This run's paid one-card Trubbish-before-Box line has both a post-selected K1-negative success avoiding Box and one independent K1-positive native line retaining Garbodor setup | It consumes a Basic searcher and a physical discard; in 64 matched independent deals, only one qualified K0 trigger and no net setup gain |
| **Pokémon Communication** (TEU 152, 1 copy) | Shuffle a Pokémon in hand into deck, then search one Pokémon | Can get a missing Trubbish or other planned Pokémon before Box, exposing K1 | It trades away a real held Pokémon and may reintroduce evolution/Bench risk; avoid naively rewarding connectivity |
| **Ultra Ball** (MEG 131, 2 copies) | Discard two other cards, search any Pokémon | Could find Trubbish when Quick Ball absent and expose K1 before Box | Its two-discard cost competes directly with Box's three discards, often eliminating paper payability; far less plausible without disposable duplicate resources |

The *printed texts* support these access windows but do **not** establish a strategically dominant ordering. A proper decision model should value a search action's **information edge** in addition to its immediate Pokémon/Tool effect and its future opportunity cost: whether paying that action before Box preserves future lines on the good branch and avoids an irreversible cost on the bad branch. The observed zero-difference study is a direct warning against treating a plausible K0→K1 improvement as a deck or win-rate gain without new unselected paper-audited tests. For any next test, register exactly which previously necessary search routes are eligible, preserve physical payment, Bench capacity, all initial hidden zones, both actor seats, and match chronology. These conditions are especially important before treating opponent lock matchups as solved.
