## The two causal, fully paid source roots

**Seed 603372370, source seat 0.** The first different accepted public native action occurred at global turn 12, Store action 67: the control declared Tapu Lele-GX's Energy Drive, whereas the intervention played Mysterious Treasure. The native owner cost prompt actually discarded **Salamence ex JTG** from source hand, native own-deck search chose **Regidrago VSTAR SIT**, and the native ShowCards continuation finalized the Item. At turn 14, a second accepted Treasure actually discarded **Dragapult ex TWM**, searched **Latias ex SSP**, and finalized. Thus the intervention placed two useful Dragon attack donors in the **real source discard pile**, with two paid genuine owner-hand costs and two native-retrieved physical Pokémon. It did **not** induce Apex Dragon: the source's actual attacks continued as **Tapu Lele-GX Energy Drive** through its last observed own attack at global turn 26. A Regidrago VSTAR evolved in both arms at turn 14, but the upgraded attacker never occupied the Active position for a source Apex attack. The native winner and final Prize vector matched the control.

**Seed 2295622320, source seat 1.** First changed public action at global turn 8, Store action 44: control Passed, intervention played Mysterious Treasure. The genuine cost discarded the now-dead **Battle VIP Pass FST**, and the owner-deck search selected **Dialga-GX UPR**. The bot immediately put Dialga-GX on the Bench at turn 8. The control went on to declare four Apex Dragons at turns 18, 20, 22 and 24, all without native copied-choice selections. The intervention declared **zero Apex Dragon**, and continued with Pass or other board actions, also with no native copied-choice selections. Source was seat 1 and retained all six Prizes in the identical final `[0,6]` vector; the native opponent won both arms. The changed subsequent action sequence illustrates a nonmonotonic root/Bench interaction, although four unsuccessful baseline declarations are not four successful attacks. The retrieved Dialga was not automatically placed into the discard as an Apex payload.

These are **three fully paid Item -> hand-discard -> owner-deck-selected target -> physical receipt chains** across two fixed source deals, with zero rejected native continuation replies. They establish genuine source-access mechanics; none demonstrates copied-attack effects or improved competitive strength.

## What the result changes

The earlier [34 declaration / zero discarded Dragon finding](agent8-rank13-apex-dragon-public-discard-zero-payload-20261103.md) already established that selected attacks were blocked by missing Dragon payloads. This paired transport demonstrates a second boundary: **successfully supplying payloads can still fail to produce a copied attack when the correct attacker remains on the Bench**. When a search brings a new Pokémon to hand and the bot then plays it to the Bench, it can also consume later positioning and allocation opportunities. In the other positive deal, the Treasure search retrieved a would-be attack source *into hand*, where Apex Dragon cannot copy it.

A search optimizer that scores the three paid searches as three strategic wins, or scores fewer Apex declarations as a deterioration, will be misleading. The correct payoff chain requires actual owner-zone custody, Regidrago VSTAR Active and fueled, genuine `ChooseAttackPrompt` native selection, settled damage/effect, Prize transition, and consequential opponent reply. The current generic source/opponent actors do not meet that standard.
