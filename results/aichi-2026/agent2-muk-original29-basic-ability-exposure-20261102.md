## Exact source-level result

The supplied paper-Expanded card database and the frozen [29 published original Aichi printed sixties](full-decklists.json) were joined by English display set code, physical collector number and card name. All published source Pokémon printings were resolved **including four promo entries** whose tournament display aliases `SMP 217`, `XYP 67`, `XYP 49` correspond to the supplied catalog's `PR-SM / SM217`, `PR-XY / XY67`, `PR-XY / XY49` identifiers. No unresolved source Pokémon printings remain after those specific, traceable alias mappings. The source manifest remains 29/32; missing ranks 23, 25 and 28 were **never imputed**.

The [reusable exact-print census script](../../simulation/evaluation/agent2-muk-field-basic-abilities.cjs) and [static regression](../../tests/agent2-muk-field-basic-abilities.test.cjs) completed successfully in manual [global field-gate CI run 41536875857](https://github.com/FlareZ123/experiment-01/actions/runs/41536875857), head `d0419ce35ff7cb9310eba7412b9238994a7a13c2`, pinned source inventory blob `ae773f6201d7aa69d31914dbfee66f3fba692200`.

- **29/29** authenticated 60-card opponents include **at least one printed Basic Pokémon Ability**.
- Across all 29, there are **208 physical Basic Pokémon cards with printed Abilities**, with **43 distinct card/Ability printings**.
- **2 different Basic Ability printings explicitly work from their owner's discard pile:** Exeggcute PLF4's *Propagation* (two copies in original rank 26 Meloetta Donk), and Ho-Oh-EX DRX22's *Rebirth* (one copy in original rank 19 Raging Bolt).
- The previously published five-name Klefki census found only **22/29 decks containing one of its selected five utility carriers**. The broader **29/29** result counts *all* Basic Abilities and the extra out-of-play activation window. The denominator and question are different; 29/29 is a card-list incidence fact, not actual effective lock coverage.

The cited 208 is a sum of physical copies of **Basic Pokémon with an Ability printed on that exact card**, even if a Pokémon has multiple ability objects; it is not an expected Ability activation count.

## Strategic coverage and possible counterexamples for Apex Alchemy

**Iron Thorns ranks 6, 24, 29:** every original source 60 contains exactly four Basic Iron Thorns ex with *Initialization* (12 physical copies across the cluster). Stage-1 Alolan Muk itself lacks a Rule Box and is not an Initialization target. Printed suppression of Thorns' own Basic Ability is thus a credible early counterlock *hypothesis*, subject to timing, Bench/evolution reach, Item denial, existing lock precedence and actual native Store settlement. The important question is whether Muk can take effect before the opponent's damage/positioning plan wins.

**Stall ranks 3 and 7:** rank 3 carries three Basic Snorlax with *Block* along with other Basic Abilities; rank 7 has two Basic Snorlax with Block, three Wobbuffet with *Bide Barricade* and three Mimikyu with *Safeguard*. Muk's broad Basic lock has potential to turn off meaningful stall pieces. **Do not assert that Muk automatically defeats Active Wobbuffet**: its overlapping continuous Ability suppression and any Stadium/Tool interactions require an exact order/priority ruling and live board witness. The candidate's own Bench constraints and four-Retreat-Cost Muk are also hazards under gust.

**Discard-pile-specific advantage relative to an Active-only Klefki model:** source rank 26 holds two Exeggcute with *Propagation*, allowing recovery from the discard under its printed condition. Rank 19 holds one Ho-Oh-EX *Rebirth* with a discard activation window. Alolan Muk SUM58 says Basic Pokémon **in play, in each hand and in each discard pile** lose Abilities, so the out-of-play part of its printed effect has a potential target in these two exact original lists. Whether either ability would be used *after* Muk is established is a further gameplay question. This is especially time-sensitive for rank 26's early aggressive plan.

**Vileplume ranks 2 and 15:** both have Basic support Abilities in their physical lists, but their evolved Vileplume Item-locking mechanism is not itself Basic. Incidence of support targets does **not** mean the main evolved lock is neutralized.

**Self-lock cost:** both the unchanged fifth [Apex Alchemy v0](agent43-apex-alchemy-structural-candidate-20261102.md) and the [Shake4/Quick2 sixth challenger](agent2-apex-alchemy-shake4-quick2-falsification-20261102.md) include one Basic Tapu Lele-GX with *Wonder Tag* and one Basic Latias ex with *Skyliner*. Deploying Muk before using these Abilities can remove a legal Supporter search or retreat-cost reduction line. A policy must model the resource/turn order and potential ability-lock reversal. Counts alone cannot assign whether Muk helps or hurts any matchup.

## Limits and next discriminating test

A card being in a source 60 **does not imply** it was accessible, deployed, effective, desired or timed to matter. The source field includes active-only, bench-entry, bench-activated, continuous and discard abilities that have different strategic windows and different self-interference. It is impossible to derive matchup wins by counting affected card copies. The mapping does not decide whether opponent counterlocks, Active/Bench placement, paper rules, subsequent attacks or Prize trajectories work as intended.

Prioritize a **genuine original-source rank6 Thorns versus live elected Muk** same-deal two-player fixture and a **rank7 Wobbuffet-overlap lock** state with exact Ability outcome. In parallel, test whether rank26's source *Propagation* genuinely activates before and fails after Muk under a legal native state without overriding effects, then continue to a consequential opponent turn. Each test requires real selected roots, settled prompts, original-owner physical custody, non-passive opponent policy and the paper clock to be competitive evidence. Zero qualified games remain and 99.5% is wholly unproven.

**Evidence class:** printed-card deterministic census plus bounded strategic hypotheses; no win-rate inference.
