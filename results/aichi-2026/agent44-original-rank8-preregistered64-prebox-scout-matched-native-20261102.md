### Fresh scout-success witness, source index451

The only firstturn pre-Box scout in the 64 fresh pairs was **index451**, source seat1. Its source owner paid a genuine native **Quick Ball** cost (discarding `VS Seeker`), searched its own deck for a Trubbish, and then learned through that native owner-deck prompt that **Boost Shake remained available**. The treatment continued with its real native Box and the three distinct physical source hand→discard payment cards, then acquired Shake and evolved an equipped Garbodor with a subsequent opponent public decision root. The matched baseline also completed this strict native setup. The treatment thus **preserved** the previously working sequence on this one new K1-positive example. Other effects or longer-term strategic quality from spending Quick Ball earlier were not proven equivalent.

### Negative boundary: source index461

At **index461**, the source originally had Trubbish in play, so the existing narrow Quick Ball-first trigger requiring a held Quick Ball as the *needed means to obtain Trubbish* was inapplicable. Both policies committed Secret Box before owner K1 search, discovering Shake missing and failing the intended firstturn lock. This is an observed limit on the conditional heuristic and indicates that an early K1 information path needs to be assessed under its own real discard/tempo cost when there is **no independently necessary Basic search**. Automatically forcing a Quick Ball search only for deck inspection may be strategically wasteful; no evidence here justifies it.

The separately preserved **historically selected positive** completed native source Tool-Garbodor in both arms and is excluded from all fresh counts. The previously post-selected index383 repair ([information-first case study](agent44-k0-k1-information-first-search-sequencing-20261102.md)) shows a K1-negative example where the Quick Ball-first line can avoid Box spending, but combining it with this 64-deal independent fresh experiment as a randomized success would be invalid.

## Interpretation and next research

The independent matched 64 original source positions **falsify any claim of demonstrated setup-completion improvement** under the tested narrow treatment: 5 strict native success versus 5, one failed Box versus one, and no firstturn native errors. The mechanism is feasible in at least one new physical deal and avoids cost in one earlier post-selected failure. With a single positive scout among 64, this experiment cannot establish a general effect on game wins, expected cards preserved, payoff from saving Box, or matchup. A fair policy optimization must value the Quick Ball resource spent to acquire K1, support alternate Box uses when Shake is unavailable, and model later opponent pressure. Further tests could compare additional **already-required search connectors** that reveal own K1 before expensive play, using a new independently preregistered seed set and preservation of all actor decisions.

The major program remains **29/32 authenticated original Aichi Top32 printed source sixties (ranks23,25,28 missing), zero competitively qualified games, no fixed60 selection and no demonstrated 99.5% field win rate**. All 64 native results are developmental. Neither exact SHA parity nor source TWM paper-payment custody certifies other game phases, strategic bot competence, the missing paper clock or real-world matchup outcomes.
