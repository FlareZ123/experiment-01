# Agent 10: correction to Aichi Open League qualifier/final-rank provenance

**7 November 2026 | Source-method correction.** This note corrects the event-stage interpretation used in the earlier Agent 10 rank-25/rank-28 provenance notes and in the Agent 44 rank-28 identity-join audit.

## Official format applicable to this opponent field

The benchmark is the **Open League / Extra Regulation** event on 10 May 2026. The official Pokémon Card Game event page explicitly separates this from the Master League format:

- The Open League was held on 10 May only.
- Its qualifying stage was one Swiss-draw event, with the top 32 advancing.
- Its Top 32 main stage was a single-elimination tournament.

See the official event overview: https://www.pokemon-card.com/info/005445.html (Open League schedule and format, especially the Open League section). The same page's earlier Master League section describes a different three-stage structure (Day-1 qualifier, Day-2 Main 1 Swiss, Day-2 Main 2 bracket); that Master League structure must not be imported into the Open League.

Limitless reports the final Top 32 placement table for the CL Aichi Open League here: https://limitlesstcg.com/tournaments/566.

## Consequence for the social-source leads

The public Japanese accounts describe **qualifying Swiss ordinal**, not a separately named final bracket placement:

- Rank-25 lead: the kume first-person article says `予選25位抜け` and reports a first-round knockout loss. The Pokekameshi recap associates kume with a Best32 Eleki Rain / Alolan Raichu one-shot entry. The Limitless final Top 32 table names Kazuki Kumegawa at 25th and classifies the entry as Raichu Electrode Combo.
- Rank-28 lead: the Replica / @nnaacc5_uec post says `予選28位` and a first-round knockout loss. The Pokekameshi recap associates Replica with a Best32 Regidrago entry. The Limitless final Top 32 table names Jo Kato at 28th and classifies the entry as Regidrago.

The Open League stage structure makes these **plausible same-event, same-archetype leads**. It does not, by itself, establish that a qualifying Swiss ordinal is the exact final Limitless placement. A first-round bracket loss places a player among the first-round losers; the official format page does not specify the ordering rule for assigning final ordinals within that group. Nor do the public posts directly bridge either alias to the official Limitless player name.

Therefore, equality of `Swiss 25` with final `25th`, or `Swiss 28` with final `28th`, is not an identity proof. A direct player-account/name bridge, official bracket record, or other authoritative final-placement mapping is still required before attaching either photo/list to the named final-rank opponent.
