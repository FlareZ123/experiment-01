## Player-reported strategic design intent worth testing

The public article says the player favored a first-player-compatible **Alolan Raichu one-shot** design as a response to expected Regidrago prevalence. His stated required matchup plans include:

1. Suppress Regidrago's **Latias ex and Tapu Lele-GX Basic Abilities** to make its mobility/search less reliable; he identifies **Klefki** as an economical one-card Ability-lock option. The card's self-lock/opening consequences matter.
2. Plan around early **Budew Item denial**, opposing **Path to the Peak**, **Sudowoodo Bench-space restriction**, and **Wobbuffet/Klefki Active lock** with differentiated access routes. Discussed *possible* answers include drawing without Items, Pokémon Ranger, Marshadow, Escape Rope/Canceling Cologne, and opposing ability-lock counterplay. These are **design options the author considered**, **not proof that each card was registered**.
3. Counter an early attacking Regidrago via **Energy denial, Horror House-GX, a disabling attack, or a gusted one-shot**. Again these are prospective strategic axes from the player, not a decklist.
4. The player regarded **T1 Garbodor via Boost Shake** as an alternative with high slot cost (approximately six slots in his framing) and stated he did **not** choose it. He also discussed Wobbuffet versus Klefki and Iron Thorns-only as tradeoffs, while emphasizing first-player access. None of this proves full printed counts.

These signals differ from the common rank-4/17 Raichu decklist: the author says that a **Klefki-based** route mattered for his own selection, so treating the rank-4 Raichu 60 as rank-25's source would be an unsupported identity error. Conversely, the text does **not** establish whether his exact submitted 60 contains Klefki, in what count, or which one-shot support package was chosen.
