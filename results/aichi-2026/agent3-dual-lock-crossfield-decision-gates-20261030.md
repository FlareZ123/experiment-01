## Candidate mechanism being challenged

A going-second **Arven→Secret Box→Boost Shake** sequence can create **Active Stage1 Jellicent ex** with **Stealthy Hood** and **Silent Lab** on the first player-owned turn. Silent Lab suppresses Basic Iron Thorns ex's Initialization without suppressing Stage1 Jellicent's Oceanic Curse. Hood may protect Jellicent if opponent replaces Lab. The compulsory Secret Box hand payment is **three actual other cards**. An alternate going-second Wally evolution route can continue the turn but consumes the single Supporter. Going first, ordinary Supporters cannot play; the only analogous direct first-turn source of the multichannel connector is **holding Secret Box**.

**Exact opening upper proxy:** with 4 Frillish/4 Jellicent/4 Boost/4 Hood/4 Lab/4 Arven/1 Box and 12 total Basics, after initial seven + one ordinary draw + six uniform Prizes, conditional on valid Basic initial seven: **24.450935%** physically accessible double protection going second, **8.328381%** going first. Assigning only twelve of the 35 unnamed cards as strategically safe Secret Box discards and requiring all three payment cards to be drawn reduces going-second access to **11.408137%** in the strict DCI sensitivity model. Candidate original sevens that can both play the physical search route **and** start with a second Basic total **15.514351%**, so the search-only event is larger than an anti-single-donk-safe event. All values are **access**, not usable attacks or game success.

## Interacting strategic variables

**Connector domination and DCI:** Arven→Box looks hyperconnected but spends the only Supporter and **three additional hand cards**. If those cards are core attackers, Stadium replacements, Energy, Prize rescue, or scarce Basics, the theoretical immediate lock can be strategically dominated by preserving a direct Boost+Tool Arven route. The parameterized cheap-card model reproduces this cost-driven inversion; under a narrow assumed 12-cheap-card pool, trimming two Jellicent copies to obtain fodder improved a *first-turn-only* proxy but would worsen Prize and rebuild risk. It is **not a two-Jellicent deck recommendation**.

**Starting-board risk:** More candidate Basic copies reduce source-first rank26 lone-Active vulnerability while making a natural Frillish Active less frequent and supplying additional Guzma targets against other decks. No universally correct Bench policy follows. True pregame information is restricted to the player's own cards and any legitimately observed opponent mulligan reveals; opponent's actual first Active is turned face-up only at game start, after setup Bench decisions.

**Ability lock intersections:** Rank26's exact 60 contains **35 Items, 4 Tools and 11 Basic Pokémon with printed Abilities**. Active Jellicent prohibits the 39 Trainer plays from hand while Lab suppresses the 11 Ability texts in all relevant zones, but those Pokémon remain playable and can attack. Rank26 may play one of four Risky Ruins Stadiums to restore Basic Abilities, while Jellicent's independent Item/Tool restriction continues. Starting **Mimikyu** or **Thorns** before source first action is a different architecture from starting Active **Frillish**, so alternatives compete for starting spot.
