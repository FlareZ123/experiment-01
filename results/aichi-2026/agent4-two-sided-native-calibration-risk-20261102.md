# Why raw native setup scores can rank decks backwards: a matched two-sided calibration witness

**Agent 4, 2 November 2026. Cross-experiment methodological synthesis grounded in actual native Store receipts.**

The shared optimizer's target is a **fixed legal paper Expanded 60-card list** competitive across all 32 original Aichi 2026 opponent lists. Two distinct classes of native-simulation failure have now been physically observed in bounded tests, on the same pinned Twinleaf revision a9905a76a6c4103ba4b6cb9a49e86bad795c5c05:

## Upward bias: granting impossible multi-axis resources

Original source-printed **Secret Box TWM** requires **three other physical cards discarded**. An actual native Store **CHOOSE_CARD_TO_DISCARD min3/max3** prompt has validate([])=false, yet Store accepts the submitted empty payment. It then legally processes the separate category-search prompt, **retrieving one Item, Tool, Supporter and Stadium into hand without any mandatory cost paid**, both actual player seats. [Real native payment fixture + report](agent4-secret-box-twm-native-three-card-paper-cost-20261102.md), [green native CI 41431566095](https://github.com/FlareZ123/experiment-01/actions/runs/41431566095). The same underlying empty payment acceptance exists at **Quick Ball FST**, albeit with no Basic selected after zero payment; [full physical native receipt](agent4-quickball-fst-mandatory-discard-native-20261102.md). A deck with many expensive connectors may appear far more consistent if the engine silently credits their payoff after rejected compulsory costs.

## Downward bias: omitting a payable one-card attack resource

The actual original Aichi **rank5 Mew/Genesect 60** contains four Double Colorless Energy SUM and Mew VMAX Cross Fusion Strike requiring two Colorless units. In a **pre-registered exact-full-deal four-pair two-seat native source experiment**, with actor Mew evolution/draw/mobility policy fixed, a single added **two-seat Special Energy root** changed genuine original-source DCE hand-attachment count from **0/4 control to 4/4 treatment**. One matched New Moon opponent pair changed from **0 source attacks and a 260-action cap** to **4 genuinely settled Cross Fusion Strike attacks and an engine-native six-Prize termination**. [Frozen raw all-eight archive](https://github.com/FlareZ123/experiment-01/actions/runs/41432524058/artifacts/13135660347), [full report and preregistration](agent4-rank5-special-energy-root-prospective-20261102.md). This demonstrates that a weak owner policy can understate a genuine opponent's line. It does not establish a paper-legal victory or a win probability.

The same experiment reveals a residual problem: other starting states choose the DCE for **Active Lillie's Clefairy ex** while Mew VMAX is on the Bench, producing many turns of a stranded support Pokémon with zero attacks. A policy that merely checks whether Energy attachment happened will miss this positional contention.

## Practical ranking implication

**An optimizer can be wrong in both directions simultaneously.** If the candidate receives illegally free Secret Box category connectors while the original opponent fails to attach a readily playable DCE, native setup or outcome comparisons systematically favor the candidate for reasons unrelated to real deck strength. These biases cannot be corrected by increasing simulated sample size; repeated games merely estimate the wrong two-player decision and rules process more precisely.

Required minimum confidence layers before win-rate claims:

1. Exact source 60, source printing, physical Prize/deal provenance, paper legality, game-clock and exclusion census.
2. Physical payment audits that enforce every compulsory source-printed cost (not just prompt class and theoretical validator) and preserve search effects that are truly optional.
3. Both-owner action policies capable of attainable archetype-specific powerup/evolution/attack lines, tested by actual Store acceptance, paid attacks and subsequent opponent replies. Current Mew source proves both successful and unsuccessful branches on identical starting deals.
4. Same-deal matched field comparisons with **all 32 original opponent decks**, source per-matchup dispositions, confidence and failure modes. No claims for missing exact original rank23/25/28.

This synthesis is based on real Store prepared board and separately registered native games. **No game here is competitively qualified; 29/32 authenticated opponent printed sixties; the 99.5% target remains an aspiration.** Treat both upward and downward calibration errors as launch blockers for any deck being marketed as hyper-optimized.
