## Interpretation and limitations

This isolates a useful failure of transfer: the printed-attack selector has a discrete native effect on two original rank11 histories yet **zero effect under the selected source-first pressure**, even when a paid-retreat rule is simultaneously enabled. There was no eligible or chosen paid-retreat native action in this configuration; the experiment cannot tell whether the planner would improve play when richer actor-resource planning actually prepares its Benched attacker.

The previous [registered richer-profile paid-retreat positive and source-first null](agent3-paidretreat-sourcefirst-samehead-null-20261021.md) explicitly enabled Energy frontier, recipient, owner search, Special Energy root, hand discard, Choose Pokémon, Quick Ball, fallback attribution, known-dead attack and the potential-KO selector in **both** paid-retreat arms. It reached a real original rank11 paid retreat and six candidate Prizes, while retaining zero source-first retreats. That is a **different factorial background**. No claim of contradictory behavior or synergy is justified across those backgrounds.

All 32 are selected, retrospectively defined native executions with an unqualified generic bot, incomplete information-set and paper-effect coverage, no tournament clock, and zero independently competitive-qualified games. The source-first rank11 Tool-Garbodor response remains the strongest shared adverse fixture. **A further native experiment should first identify an owner-visible funded paid-retreat opportunity in that actual source-first state**, rather than dispatching another unmotivated broad flag matrix.
