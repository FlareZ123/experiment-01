## Why there was no treatment exposure

A read-only audit of all archived source-owner root snapshots found **eight of the 24 physical games ever had an Active VSTAR**, and **six had a same-root Active VSTAR with nominal printed Apex Energy readiness**. Six games had a same-root funded Active VSTAR *and* a Dragon already in public discard, so the added donor-discard play was unnecessary. **Exactly one of the 24 games**, source seat1 seed `3588534981`, offered a funded Active VSTAR **without** a Dragon in public discard (four owner-root snapshots). It had an owner-held evolved donor and Battle Compressor, **but no Quick Ball in hand at those decision roots**, so the newly defined Quick Ball-only branch correctly remained inactive. That game later reached six copied Apex selections in both arms by the original unchanged paths.

This explains the null outcome with an intersection of necessary board, Energy, public discard, and hand resources. Observing these conditions **at different times in a single game** would give a misleading apparent opportunity. It also shows that availability of a powerful discard connector in hand is insufficient: the relevant exact connector must be present when the tactical line can be paid.
