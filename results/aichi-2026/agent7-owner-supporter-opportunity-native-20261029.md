# Agent 7: native both-seat owner Supporter opportunities and policy non-use

**29 October 2026. Evidence class: read-only original-Store developmental policy audit, zero competitively qualified games.**

## Motivation and exact scope

The independently archived 29 October, 32-arm source-policy census found 339 candidate PlayCardAction roots and zero accepted candidate Supporters, but had no independent owner-hand opportunity denominator. The [29 October Critic priority notice](../../communications/broadcast/20261029T1850Z_critic_two_player_playcard_policy_gate.md) requests separation of held, legally usable, policy proposed, native accepted, and effect settled states for both actual players. This study establishes weak structural layers and actual policy proposals for four old historical native games. It **does not establish paper legality or a missed beneficial Supporter**.

The new code integrates into the **existing canonical bilateral actor composition**, simulation/evaluation/agent7-canonical-actor-composition.cjs, with reversible, default-off flag AGENT7_OWNER_SUPPORTER_AUDIT_DEV=true, implemented by simulation/twinleaf/agent7-owner-supporter-opportunity.cjs. It uses the detached own-hand plus public-state contract and native TrainerType.SUPPORTER. The observer is installed as the last decoding hook immediately around the genuine synchronous Store workload and delegates the chosen action unchanged. It watches both authenticated acting seats only on real live Store owner-root turns. Opponent-private hand identities cannot enter the decision observer.

At each first owner root of each normal turn, it records exact held Supporter printing identities, the unused normal Supporter quota, publicly visible Supporter-play restrictions, and a first-player-first-turn screen (ordinary Supporters restricted; known Carmine exception). This count is called **structurally open**, which is *not* verified legally playable or strategically profitable. The optional null-target native canPlay screen was **disabled** in this native experiment; some cards require physical target checks that such a probe cannot certify. The audit additionally records proposed PlayCardAction Supporters, successful native Store dispatch, movement from hand and advancement of quota, and final Pass roots with an open structural window. Bounded seat/turn-level owner examples are preserved. The upstream decision algorithm, source/candidate decklists and printed card instances were unchanged.

These numbers establish an **actual owner-state structural bottleneck** for the observed two-player policy. They **do not** prove that any of the 60 Passes was mistaken. Some held Supporters may be conditionally unusable, costlier than passing, disrupted or competing with another tactical plan. Printed card-specific applicability and native target availability must be recorded before a legal-missed-opportunity claim; genuine prompt selection, native effect settlement and source responses must be verified before a competitive claim.
