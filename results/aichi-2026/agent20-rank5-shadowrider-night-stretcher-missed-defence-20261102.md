# Agent 20: rank5 Mew native terminal depends on a missed Night Stretcher defensive option

2 November 2026. Retrospective inspection of immutable original-deck Agent13 integrated v3.1 native artifact 13115652707, CI 41387484608. This is an owner-hand and public-board evidence audit, not an additional simulated match.

In original rank5 Mew/Genesect versus rank1 Shadow Rider champion-reference, source physical seat1 used Cross Fusion Strike to KO candidate Dialga-GX on global turn19 (two Prizes) and Latias ex on turn23 (two). The candidate's actual native Store turn20 and turn24 responses were accepted PassTurnAction roots. At global turn24, the after-decision owner-hand receipt for physical candidate seat0 includes **Night Stretcher ASC**. The public board at that pass shows its Active **Girafarig** and **no Benched Pokémon**. No opponent Item lock is visible: the attacking Active is Mew VMAX and the observed opposing Bench contains Mew VMAX, Genesect V and Dedenne-GX.

Night Stretcher ASC 196 is an Item returning a Pokémon or Basic Energy from the user's discard pile to hand. Both previously Knocked Out Dialga-GX and Latias ex are Basic Pokémon; neither player took a subsequent documented action recovering those cards between their KOs and the candidate's turn24. Under normal paper rules they are placed in the discard pile when KO'd. The candidate therefore had a concrete paper-legal sequence: **play Night Stretcher, retrieve an eligible Basic, Bench that Basic before ending turn24**. The observed bot instead passed with no Bench and was eliminated when source turn27 KO'd Girafarig, with source still having one Prize left.

This establishes a material **opponent decision-competence veto** against interpreting that particular native terminal as evidence of matchup strength. It does not prove which subsequent action the source would take on a different board, nor that the candidate ultimately wins. Exact same-state native alternative replay and paper Item availability testing remain unperformed.
