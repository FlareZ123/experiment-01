## Source-described strategic operating constraints

The player describes selecting Alolan Raichu one-shot primarily to contest Regidrago while retaining play against other archetypes. The report's central *intended opening threat profile* is position-dependent:

1. **Going first against a second-turn Regidrago player:** establish a Basic-Pokémon Ability-locking Active. In particular, suppress Latias ex and Tapu Lele-GX access so the opponent has less freedom to construct a turn-one Budew Item-lock position. This strategic intent is relevant to any candidate bot that assumes those support Pokémon always activate.
2. **Going second against a first-turn Regidrago player:** the list needs answers to a Path to the Peak, Sudowoodo or Wobbuffet opening and must survive the opponent's subsequent Regidrago VSTAR turn. This requires a different reply policy from the going-first lock line.
3. **Construction choice:** the author considered Klefki, Wobbuffet, and Boost Shake/Garbodor alternatives, citing their divergent suppressions and startup risks, and states that the eventual Alolan Raichu build deliberately used **Klefki**. The report explains that Klefki can obstruct important Basic Pokémon Abilities but can also obstruct the owner's own start; Garbodor required an evolution, Tool, and additional slots. These are **author's strategic claims**, not a proof that every such interaction applies under every printed-card state.
4. **Specific named contingency options in the player's design analysis** include Pokémon Ranger or other non-Item avenues against Budew, Marshadow against Path to the Peak, Escape Rope or Canceling Cologne against certain opening locks, and energy denial or early disruption against turn-two Regidrago. Merely mentioning them as contemplated options **does not establish tournament inclusion or quantities**.
