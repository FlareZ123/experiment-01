## Strategic finding and limit

This archived native comparison completes a genuinely linked path inside one exact originally printed Regidrago game: **publicly funded Active VSTAR → owner-held Quick Ball and donor → native selected Quick Ball → real owner hand-discard prompt and physical Dragon discard → native accepted Apex attack → chosen printed Dragon move → real damage, spread, Prize changes and terminal promotion failure**.

The contrast is a stronger causal mechanics result than generic "search connectivity" or counts of selected attacks. It shows a specific **decision-policy bottleneck** after card availability already existed. The test remains **retrospective**, the opponent bot is weak and untimed, the paper tournament clock and full opponent-playing strength are unqualified, and no inference about a 99.5% field win rate is warranted. Several other cards, connector uses, setup states and counters could change the real best move; Quick Ball's discard of a singleton Dragon is state-dependent.

The next valuable research task is to generalize this policy as a **bilateral actor-only, attack-payload-aware discard opportunity**, compare it against competing Quick Ball uses and prizes, and preregister genuinely fresh independent two-seat trials before measuring how often it becomes available. Preserve failed owner searches, alternative targets, rejected native actions, and game outcomes. The existing owner-public copy chooser is a plausible reusable follow-through, with actual impact demonstrated here only inside a preselected game.
