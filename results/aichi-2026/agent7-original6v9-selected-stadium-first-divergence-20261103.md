## Original rank6: concrete attack and Prize consequence

Immediately before the natural turn-10 Pass, the actual source actor had:

- Active **Iron Thorns ex TWM**; three attached physical Energy cards: `Speed Lightning Energy RCL`, `Capture Energy RCL`, `Speed Lightning Energy RCL`.
- Existing own `Wondrous Labyrinth Prism Star TEU`; physical `Thunder Mountain Prism Star LOT` in own hand at slot7; opponent had no Stadium; Stadium quota last used turn2; no Stadium play lock.
- Opponent Active **Unown AOR** with a single `Prism Energy ASC`, opponent holding six Prize cards; actor had six Prizes.

The provided card database says Iron Thorns ex `sv6-77` `Volt Cyclone` requires Lightning + two Colorless (base three units) and does 140 damage, moving one attached Energy to the actor's Bench. `Wondrous Labyrinth` `sm9-158` adds one Colorless for a non-Fairy attacker; with three attached single-unit Energy cards, the nominal requirement of **four** cannot be met. `Thunder Mountain` `sm8-191` reduces Lightning attacks by one Lightning symbol; the two remaining Colorless symbols can be met using the available three attached Energy cards. The card-rule paper explanation is consistent with the observed native activation, subject to any other effects not included in this local comparison.

**Native control:** on global turns 10, 12 and 14 the rank6 source bot selected Pass, and **zero attack actions** occurred anywhere in the complete 90-action control. No Prize changed hands.

**Source treatment:** native Store accepted `Thunder Mountain` and moved displaced Wondrous into the own Lost Zone. The next native owner root was a genuine **`AttackAction('Volt Cyclone')` on turn10**. During attack prompt settlement, Iron Thorns moved one `Speed Lightning Energy` to its Bench and the opponent's Active left play. The actor took a Prize, **six to five**. The opponent's actual following turn11 responded with **Rescue Carrier**, `Unown` to the board and a selected Pass. The actor then used **Volt Cyclone again on turn12**, moved `Capture Energy` away to its Bench, and claimed another Prize, **five to four**, with genuine prompt/K.O. resolution. The opposing rank9 source subsequently played more cards including `Prism Energy` and `Rescue Carrier` on turn13. This is a genuine selected source attack with a consequential opponent reply, **within the limited native simulator**.

The two source Prize receipts show tactical improvement over the fixed native Pass comparison at this particular historical state. The original source baseline and the treatment each remain action-capped; the opponent bot is not competitively validated, and no official paper clock was modeled. This must **not** be counted as a win or a 99.5%-field contribution.

On original source global turn8, the rank9 owner first used its already-in-play `PokéStop PGO` Stadium ability, then native bot chose Pass. At that Pass it held `Silent Lab PRC` in physical slot8, and native enumerator omitted it. It is evidence **against** promoting generic all-Stadium-replace-on-Pass behavior as globally stronger.

## Release lesson

One source-owned, paper-eligible omitted Stadium action can produce an authentic attack/Prize divergence despite the native enumerator and default bot both missing it. Another nearly identical structural omission offers no immediate payoff. The next shared **one selected, symmetric** policy should evaluate *what the replacement makes possible now* (funded attacks, denial, competing Stadium actions) rather than always replacing a Stadium. Preserve paper name/quota/lock gates, physical owner-hand provenance, Prism Star destination, and a per-action first-divergence + opponent-reply trace; retain negative cases and both seats. These results remain developmental.
