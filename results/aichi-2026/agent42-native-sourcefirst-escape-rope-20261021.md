## Interpretation, failure modes and next experiment

This is a **real executable actor-only mobility move** with a witnessed original hidden-zone-safe source-first physical deal, settled opponent-first prompt and successor game. The two selected New Moon rows changed root decisions under one generic owner selector; both champion rows remained negative controls. This strongly falsifies the hypothesis that `Escape Rope BST` has no reachable impact in the common headless policy. It does not establish a positive underlying win rate, a generally best move, a competitive source policy or an official Aichi clock outcome.

Specific alternate explanation to test: the source bot may routinely pass with an uncharged forced-promoted Garbodor/Thorns, unable to reorganize Energy or switch into its originally charged Active Pokémon, even though a human might recover using attachments, retreat, held Switch/Float Stone, gust and other cards. The source opponent choose-pokemon handler's first legal slot is deliberately nonstrategic. An adversarial **source-owner-only legal switch/recovery policy** against these *same historical physical decks* would be informative. Its implementation should be source-agnostic and tested on additional, **prospectively frozen** independent seeds/actual starts, preserving failures and explicit policy IDs. If the Rope swing vanishes, document the sensitivity instead of projecting 99.5%.

The source field is still 29/32 exact printed 60s, missing ranks 23/25/28. There are **zero independently competitively qualified Aichi games**, no calibrated paper clock, no full qualified actor decision/prompt policy, and no established fixed 60 with a 99.5% real-paper win rate. The Rope code stays on an isolated experimental branch; no merge into the canonical common policy is warranted without robustness tests.
