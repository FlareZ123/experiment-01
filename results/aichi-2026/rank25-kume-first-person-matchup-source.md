## Public strategic rationale, clearly distinguished from tested mechanics

The author says he had little pre-event direct matchup practice, instead relying mainly on environment analysis, deck construction and solo test sequences. He *anticipated* Regidrago at about half the field; that was a prior assumption. He describes choosing an off-Regidrago deck only if it could address Regidrago and diverse other decks; his hoped-for 60–70%+ Regidrago performance was a **design threshold**, not a measured rate.

His public decision framework highlights:

- Regidrago starts: going first can rely on early draw/bench setup and opposing Ability obstruction; going second can use Budew Item lock.
- Against Regidrago going second: prepare for Path to the Peak, Sudowoodo, Wobbuffet and a possible second-turn Regidrago attack.
- Ability-lock choices: Klefki, Wobbuffet and a Boost Shake/Garbodor line compete in bench-slot and consistency demands. He says the six-slot Garbodor package was unattractive and preferred a deck compatible with Klefki.
