## Research consequences

A comparison that only records access to a Pokémon, an attack count, or an Energy removal cannot tell whether its cost dominated a stronger actual alternative. The two confirmed *physical hand payments* expose hidden strategic loss (both Plumeria for attack; G&H/Capture for denial; two Speed Lightning for other denial). The source rank6 LO objective is control/deckout, so damage without a Prize may be low-value. A generic after-Pass Plumeria policy should include a **conservation veto** when its only two payable hand cards fund next-turn attacks, or when G&H is an indispensable connector. A proper counterfactual should preserve the actual first-turn Energy and opponent same-turn actions, and the new test does this.

**Limitations:** action-capped developmental native games, original policies not established competent, only two published original match opponents used, printed hand discard and Prize-failure hindsight known when seeds selected, no paper-clock model. **Zero competitively qualified games. Full field 29/32 exact opponent 60s (23/25/28 missing), no demonstrated 99.5% fixed deck.**
