## Conclusion for future research

This proves a **real native planning pipeline bottleneck**. A source-independent alternative made before an ordinary Energy recipient selection, combined with a later owner-visible paid retreat, converted a previously immobile zero-attack rank1 game into a match with 35 native attack actions. A local resource change alone (B) produced no attack, so treating access to a payment as sufficient would have overstated progress. Even combined (C), the original stalled opponent still won, which prevents promotion as an optimized competitive deck improvement.

The next serious question is tactical closure: did `Shadow Mist` or a subsequent attack actually impose an effective Special Energy/Stadium lock, make Prize progress, or threaten the source player's deck-out clock? Can a different owner attack line gain enough leverage without sacrificing Bench Energy? Report actual source responses and causal opportunity costs, and seek independent source-opponent matchups before any decklist nomination. Do not extrapolate 35 native attacks into win-rate improvement.
