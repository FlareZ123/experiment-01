# New Moon vs Snorlax Stall: Bench width becomes a gust-and-Block liability

**Agent 13 · 27 November 2026**  
**Decision affected:** retained New Moon policy competence against authenticated Snorlax Stall ranks 3 and 7.  
**Scope:** exact source-list/card-text decision analysis on the unchanged fixed 60. No game, CI, or win-rate claim.

## Finding

New Moon's normal setup instincts invert against Snorlax Stall.

Both authenticated stall families use **Snorlax PGO 55** with Block:

> while Snorlax is Active, the opponent's Active Pokémon cannot retreat.

Rank 3 also carries Guzma and two Counter Catcher; rank 7 carries Guzma and three Counter Catcher. Their control shells have Energy denial and recycling/disruption.

The retained New Moon 60 has many low-impact Basic support Pokémon and only **one Escape Rope** as a direct own-Active switching card. Counter Catcher changes the opponent's Active, not New Moon's. Therefore:

> **Every unnecessary support Basic placed on the Bench is a potential future trap target.**

A policy that maximizes TPE/Battle VIP Pass/Fog Crystal Bench development can make the matchup worse even while increasing raw search/setup metrics.

## Why the trap is different from ordinary retreat pressure

Snorlax's Block says the opposing Active **cannot retreat**. Paying Retreat Cost does not solve it.

If stall gusts an Unown, Duskull, Solrock, Lunatone, Klefki or another support Basic Active while Snorlax remains Active, New Moon needs one of three broad outs:

1. a card effect that switches its own Active, chiefly the singleton Escape Rope;
2. Knock Out / remove the Active Snorlax with the trapped Pokémon;
3. otherwise alter the board so Block no longer applies.

New Moon cannot assume that “attach enough Energy and retreat later” works.

## Bench-target quality matters

The candidate's Basics are not equally dangerous to expose.

### Higher trap liability

- **Unown AOR 30:** Hidden Power is only 10 damage for one Psychic. Into 150-HP Snorlax this is not a credible quick escape.
- **Duskull VIV 69:** its attack does not deal damage. It may evolve into Dusclops, but that requires the evolution and two Psychic Energy for 50-damage Will-O-Wisp; the stall shell can attack those resources.
- **Klefki SVI 96:** Joust deals only 10 and its Basic-only Ability lock is not itself an escape from Block.

These are not “never bench” cards; they must have a concrete payoff that exceeds their future gust liability.

### More self-rescuing targets

- a powered attacker already capable of taking the Snorlax KO;
- a Duskull that can immediately evolve and be funded as part of a planned Dusclops route;
- a support Pokémon whose attack/effect creates a credible board change before the stall loop takes over.

The policy should score **how the Pokémon escapes if gusted**, not only what it does while safely Benched.

## TPE target count again must be optional

Telepathic Psychic Energy searches for **up to two** Basic Psychic Pokémon.

Against Regidrago, taking fewer targets can avoid Phantom Dive spread Prizes.

Against Snorlax, taking fewer targets can avoid future gust/Block prisoners.

The same mechanical choice has two opponent-specific strategic reasons. That makes “always take two” especially indefensible.

A Snorlax-aware TPE policy should:

1. identify the minimum board actually required for the current attack/control line;
2. leave unnecessary support Basics in the deck/hand rather than filling Bench slots for generic value;
3. prefer targets that remain functional if promoted;
4. preserve one open Bench slot when doing so improves Escape Rope or future board management.

## Escape Rope is a scarce, discrete resource

The candidate has exactly one Escape Rope.

Do not spend it merely for minor positioning if:
- the opponent still has repeated gust capacity;
- a future support-Pokémon trap would otherwise be terminal or highly resource-negative.

Conversely, do not preserve it forever if the current Block state prevents the only viable attack line.

This is a classic discrete tactical resource: its value is concentrated in the exact state where Block has trapped the wrong Active.

Secret Box can search Escape Rope if it remains in the deck, but Secret Box itself requires three acceptable discards and is an Item; it is not a free second Rope.

## Do not over-bench the Solrock/Lunatone package automatically

The Solrock/Lunatone pair is highly valuable against Stadium-based Ability suppression, but Snorlax Stall is not primarily won by maximizing Stadium immunity.

If the current stall source does not present a Stadium effect that makes the pair necessary for the selected line, benching both can create two extra gust targets.

The policy should therefore treat the pair as **matchup/line-dependent**, not as a permanent bench-reservation invariant.

This directly challenges the old narrow bench-reservation adapter that reserved slots for both partners whenever the Mewtwo/Gengar core existed. That adapter is useful as a local experiment, not a whole-deck policy.

## Stall held-out states

1. **TPE with two optional support targets:** Active attacker is already functional; legal TPE targets include Unown and Duskull. Worker should be allowed to take zero or one rather than maximizing to two.
2. **Singleton Escape Rope in hand, no current trap:** opponent still has public/source-known gust capacity. Worker should price Rope as future Block insurance before spending it for marginal positioning.
3. **Unown trapped Active vs 150-HP Snorlax:** worker must recognize that one-Psychic 10-damage Hidden Power is not a quick escape and should search for the switching/board solution rather than assume eventual retreat.
4. **Duskull trapped with Dusclops + Energy actually available:** worker should recognize the evolution/attack escape line rather than hardcode Escape Rope.
5. **Solrock/Lunatone generic setup:** no relevant Stadium-control need is present; worker should not reserve/fill two Bench slots merely because the pair exists in the deck.
6. **Immediate Snorlax KO available:** if the current Active can remove Snorlax and end Block, take that board release rather than preserve Escape Rope reflexively.

## Disposition

Snorlax Stall does not force a card change yet, but it is a serious policy qualification.

The unchanged New Moon 60 can remain the retained non-Shadow challenger **only if its setup policy is opponent-aware enough to keep its Bench narrow when support bodies are liabilities**.

This further weakens any optimizer that rewards maximum Bench/search connectivity without pricing board-position risk.

No qualified matchup or 99.5% claim is created.
