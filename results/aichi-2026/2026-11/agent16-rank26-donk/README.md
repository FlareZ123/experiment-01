## Attacker subproblem solved

Rank26's 12 Basics, 4 Damage Pump, 2 Exeggcute PLF4 (30 HP), 4 Risky Ruins, 2 Rescue Carrier, Koko Prism Star, 2 Lightning, DCE, 2 Crisis Punch, and Dowsing Machine permit the following conditional sequence. Risky Ruins places two counters on a Basic non-Darkness Pokémon put onto the Bench during its owner's turn (including from deck; not setup). Crobat V is Darkness and receives none. Damage Pump can move up to two counters from one Pokemon to another Pokemon in any way; allocate one to each of two Eggs with two counters each: both KO, opponent takes two Prizes. Propagation can recover both Eggs from discard and rebench them; repeat with a second two-counter donor. Rebench both once more and use one counter from Egg A to Egg B: B KO, A survives at one counter. Thus **3 Pumps, 4 external counters, 2 accessible Eggs** suffice for exactly five Prizes; the sixth is prohibited. Rulebook E-18 supports reuse after leaving/reentering the zone, subject to suppression. Two Rescue Carriers can replace the two pair recoveries under suppressed Propagation if otherwise legal. One accessible Egg needs five Pump uses including legal Dowsing recovery, five external counters, repeated recovery.

Before starting the irreversible sequence, require known legal lethal Crisis Punch against opposing sole Pokemon, Meloetta Active with effective Tool and three Colorless energy units, effective Debut Performance if attacking on first turn going first, usable Items, Risky Ruins active and sufficient resources. Koko must fund Meloetta while Benched before pivoting Active. Correction from TPCi Team Up FAQ (31 Jan 2019): Koko itself can be the second recipient and one Lightning in discard is sufficient; see addendum below. Wobbuffet PHF suppresses both Koko and Exeggcute abilities while Active; Cologne can suppress opposing Active's Ability for the turn if legal. Carrier is no substitute for Koko. Do not assume the donation loop is always strategically correct: opponent with a backup is not eliminated by this single-target attack, and giving five Prizes creates extreme loss risk. A complete policy must account for all KO/Prize triggers, actual native receipts, target damage/protection, prompt tokens and counter allocations.

[donk_pump_plan.cjs](donk_pump_plan.cjs) is a bounded actor-known BFS for that narrow allocation problem, not a full-game policy. ## Defender: structural exposure to a first-turn game loss

Both fixed profiles have 14 Basics; original rank26 has 12. The [exact rational calculator](setup_exposure.py) uses seven-card hands, independent shuffles for each mulligan, canceling simultaneous failures, and maximum bonus draws after random Prize placement. Let p and q be the candidate and rank26 probabilities of no Basic in seven. For net bonus K, P(K=0)=(1-q)/(1-pq) and P(K=k)=(1-p)(1-q)q^k/(1-pq) for k>0. Given exactly one Basic in the valid opening seven, the marginal bonus draw misses remaining Basics with probability C(54-B,k)/C(53,k), capped at 47 available cards. Multiplying by P(exactly one Basic|valid opening) gives forced singleton exposure assuming every available backup is placed. This is **not** a loss rate or a prediction of combo success.

Under an equal-weight 32-opponent mean, a 99.5% target allows at most 16 percentage points of loss at any one rank even if the other 31 are perfect. If rank26 chooses first and our candidate always chooses second, rank26 goes first regardless of coin. The unavoidable-singleton subset alone exceeds the entire budget if conditional conversion exceeds **42.658936%**. These are conditional sensitivity thresholds, not measurements of conversion. Increasing Basic count is not free; even 16 Basics and first preference leaves almost no field loss budget under worst-case conversion. No recommendation to change frozen 60s.

## One accessible Egg is not necessarily a five-Pump bottleneck (correction)

The earlier five-Pump count applies to repeated Egg-only knockouts, not all legal prize-donation plans. One Egg plus a non-Darkness 60-HP Unown benched under Risky Ruins can donate five Prizes with **four** Damage Pumps: each moves one counter to the Egg (KO from 20 damage) and one to the Unown. Rebench the propagated Egg before each Pump. After the fourth split, the Unown has 60 damage and both are KO'd. This consumes eight external donor counters, e.g. Meloetta, Squawkabilly, Dedenne, and a second Unown each supply two. It fits five Bench slots: Squawkabilly, Dedenne, donor Unown, recipient Unown, Egg. Requires live Propagation, no premature victim Farewell Letter, and the terminal attack already secured. With `allowUnownKO:true` the resource planner now explicitly searches such allocations and tracks Unown KOs as one Prize; legacy default remains Egg-only. Local actual Node tests 39/39, including one-Egg/four-Pump success and one-Egg/three-Pump fresh-board failure. Not a native game or win-rate estimate.

## 28 November: one physical Dowsing across power and donation

The power/mobility planner and donation planner are independently sufficient *subproblem* solvers, **not independently spendable card inventories**. The new [shared-turn resource gate](shared_turn_costs.cjs) and [direct](shared_turn_costs.test.cjs) / [cross-planner](shared_turn_costs.integration.test.cjs) regressions reconcile one physical inventory: two Crisis Punch Tools, one Float Stone, one Escape Rope, one Canceling Cologne, four Damage Pumps, two Rescue Carriers, one Dowsing Machine, and explicit Dowsing discard fodder. No new source cards are assumed. The returned audit lists exactly which Trainer each action consumed, including a single explicit or implied Dowsing retrieval.

**Decision-changing counterexample:** with Meloetta initially Active, Koko and Squawkabilly in hand, one Lightning already in discard, DCE in hand, Rope and TM in hand and two discard fodder, `planPowerMobility` constructs an independently valid two-Rope route by using **Dowsing Machine to retrieve Rope**. After that route, Meloetta is the powered Active with TM and Squawkabilly is on the Bench with two Risky Ruins counters. With one accessible Egg, two Unown, and five abstract Pump uses, `planDonationResources` independently constructs a five-Prize donation. But the source owns only four Damage Pumps: the apparent fifth requires that **same Dowsing Machine**. `reconciledTurnCosts` rejects the composed trace with `DOWSING_ALREADY_SPENT`. The two independently successful plans do not constitute a legal combined line.

**Executable contrast:** with Squawkabilly initially Active and a Float Stone available, `planPowerMobility` establishes the same powered Meloetta Active and two-counter Squawkabilly Bench **without spending Dowsing**. The same five-Pump donation trace then passes the shared physical-resource gate because the fourth spent Pump can be recovered once with Dowsing. That is a *resource-feasibility* certificate only; every native action, hidden-zone access and terminal target still requires the existing source worker, card text and full-game validation.

The gate uses only explicitly supplied known hand/discard/tool data, rejects unexplained future acquisitions, and accepts only a power-then-donation ordering. It intentionally does not claim there is no other legal interleaving when it reports infeasible/unsupported. The single shared Dowsing is the key constraint; avoid reporting independent planner successes as joint source-play reachability.
