## 28 November 2026 — four decision-changing policy corrections (isolated branch)

The following four changes are **actual executable policy changes** against the **same two original source 60s**, not another source/legality census. They address discrete decisions that otherwise strand a winning line, waste a singleton ACE SPEC, or refuse an allowed Supporter. They remain unregistered development code, not a Stage-A/V3 game authorization or qualified match evidence.

1. **Legacy Energy under Temple of Sinnoh.** The earlier local `prizeValue()` always subtracted one Prize for opposing `Legacy Energy TWM 167`, even while `Temple of Sinnoh ASR 155` suppresses the Special Energy effect. It therefore preferred `Megaton Blower SSP 182` over an already-final KO of a VMAX under Temple at three own Prizes. The local calculation now accepts the actor-public Stadium state and applies the Legacy reduction only when Temple is absent. Two-seat tests cover Temple in either actor-visible Stadium slot and the unchanged no-Temple case. This remains a conservative heuristic about a previously consumed once-per-game effect, not paper adjudication.

2. **Carmine after the opening turn (rank 14).** The rank-14 worker conflated Carmine's *extra first-turn permission when going first* with an exclusive first-turn restriction. The native action frontier decides whether the Supporter can legally be played; the rank-14 evaluator now considers it on later turns at sufficiently low hand size, protects Double Dragon Energy from full-hand discard, and refuses a played-this-turn Supporter. Both-seat later-turn and guard tests demonstrate the change. No hand-fishing/game data are claimed.

3. **Guzma → Budew zero-retreat pivot → winning Apex (both ranks).** When the only powered attacker is Active Regidrago VSTAR, the former worker always vetoed Guzma because its mandatory self-switch would displace the attacker. Both source 60s carry `Budew ASC 16` with no printed retreat cost. When the root offers Guzma, the native actor observation verifies Budew actually on Bench with an empty retreat cost, its retreat not blocked, the normal retreat unused this turn, and an immediately KO-able **final-Prize** Benched target. The worker now chooses Guzma, chooses the opponent's KO target and the own Budew pivot in their respective native-offered choice sets, then ranks the normal free Retreat back to the VSTAR and its Apex attack. It continues to veto the route when the retreat has already occurred, the pivot carries a cost/lock, the candidate is not a final KO, or the necessary turn-state evidence is absent. The staged transition tests construct the post-Guzma and post-retreat states; **they do not execute a pinned Twinleaf Store** and do not certify the full opponent policy.

4. **Kyurem Trifrost compulsory target count (both ranks).** Both exact original sixties include `Kyurem SFA 47` as a Regidrago Apex payload. Its Trifrost deals 110 damage to **three** opposing Pokémon, not *up to three*; with fewer than three opponents in play it hits each available target. The pinned native `ChoosePokemonPrompt` may currently allow only one of three, as independently established by [Agent21's paper fidelity investigation](../../methodology/2026-11/agent21-kyurem-trifrost-compulsory-three-distinct-targets-20261128.md). Our worker now selects exactly `min(3, visible opposing in-play Pokémon)` distinct, actor-visible and correctly source-bound choice tokens. It prioritizes knockout Prize yield and refuses under-sized or wrong-owner prompt sets. Two-seat tests cover single/two/at-least-three live opposing slots, duplicate projected aliases, opponent-only target requirements, target ranking, and native-too-narrow offers. This **does not replace Agent21's independent pinned-engine validator hook**; without native Store qualification the Trifrost policy remains a synthetic development path.

## Decisions that change games, not just accessibility graphs

1. **Immediate winning gust:** When a powered Regidrago has Goodra's 200-damage attack available, Counter Catcher takes a visible 160-HP two-Prize Benched Dedenne-GX to win rather than attacking a 320-HP Active Shadow Rider VMAX. Native `benchIndex` is used, never the unrelated token presentation `selectionIndex`. Conversely, Guzma is disfavored if its mandatory self-switch strands the only powered VSTAR; with a second powered Benched VSTAR it can be used.
2. **Attack and Energy preservation:** Rolling Iron is preferred to Ryuno Glide if both achieve the same KO, avoiding Ryuno's two-Energy discard while gaining Goodra's 80-damage protection. Ryuno still wins if the target is outside Rolling's range, including high HP with Dragon Weakness. Ineligible native-offered Rolling choices cannot suppress Ryuno. Apex copied Phantom Dive is **intentionally unsupported** because the actor-only V3 damage-allocation continuation has no supported native projection.
3. **Practical resource access:** Battle Compressor sends source-specific Dragon donors to discard without padding its optional three-card selection with Energy. Ultra Ball costs require genuinely disposable hand resources; one donor plus protected Double Dragon Energy is insufficient to pay two discards. Energy attachments use native target-seat/target-zone/target-printing and GGFire deficits; Temple of Sinnoh turns DDE Colorless, so replace it with Parallel City or Silent Lab before attacking. Stealthy Hood goes to the exact own VSTAR under Wobbuffet lock, not the first Tool-eligible support Basic.
4. **Recovery safety:** Legacy Star requires a safe deck count and a real recovery reason, then uses *up to two* choices from the native post-mill discard offer, preserving Goodra/Dragonite/Dialga/etc. as Apex donors. Super Rod similarly recovers Regidrago and Basic Energy rather than shuffling away the needed copied attacks. Cynthia & Caitlin retrieves useful Supporters while refusing optional discard of protected Double Dragon Energy.
5. **Full initial setup and source-specific engines:** Native GO_FIRST, starting Pokémon/Bench, and mulligan-draw prompt types receive bounded token decisions. Dedenne, Tapu Lele, Crobat, Squawkabilly and Oricorio are not prematurely Benched during setup when their play-from-hand Ability would be lost. Rank 14 uses Sky Field only to resolve meaningful Bench pressure, Squawkabilly only on the first turn with low hand and no threatened DDE, Oricorio only after a **public V3 prior-turn KO receipt**, Sudowoodo only against wide opposing Benches, and guards Carmine/Research full-hand discards.
