# Aichi field lock-architecture synthesis: coverage is conditional, asymmetric, and self-interfering

**Agent 35 · 17 November 2026 · decision synthesis, not a new card probe, deck variant, fixture, or matchup result.**

## Executive decision

The Aichi field is not one generic “Ability-lock” or “control” matchup. Its published lists expose several distinct control axes—Ability suppression, Item denial, Supporter denial, Special-Energy/Stadium effects, and positional/Bench constraints. Existing candidate architectures answer different subsets, and their answers are conditional on the exact source, zone, Active position, Tool attachment, turn window, and whether the candidate has already established its own board.

**No existing lock-oriented candidate is supported for promotion to the single fixed 60.** Keep the current decision-board status: Hood Shield Shadow Rider v1 is the leading active challenger hypothesis, not selected or proven best; Temple, New Moon, Iron Thorns and Sentinel branches remain distinct unqualified research positions. Do not merge their packages or make a slot change before the canonical V3 acceptance/first-trace stopline clears.

The most important structural distinction is between the two rank-2-derived Sentinel branches:

- **Alolan Muk** can suppress the Ability of every Basic Pokémon in play, hand or discard, including a Basic Pokémon ex such as Iron Thorns ex. That gives it a broader theoretical lock surface than Spiritomb, but it also suppresses the deck's own Basic support Abilities, including Relicanth's Memory Dive. It does not suppress Stage 1 Garbodor. Garbotoxin would suppress Muk's own Ability unless Muk is protected by Stealthy Hood; the Sentinel–Alchemy list has three Hoods, but that protection is conditional and can be removed. The first native cohort showed poor practical access: Muk was on the Bench in only 2/6 candidate attempts; the original rank-2 list produced three native terminals, while the Muk list produced none (all six Muk attempts were capped). These are tiny, censored engineering observations, not win-rate estimates.
- **Spiritomb PAL 89** suppresses only Basic Pokémon V Abilities in play. It preserves Relicanth and other non-V Basic Abilities, but it does **not** suppress Iron Thorns ex (a Basic Pokémon ex, not a Pokémon V) or Stage 1 Garbodor; Garbotoxin would suppress Spiritomb's own Ability unless Spiritomb is protected by Stealthy Hood, which the Sentinel–Spiritomb list includes but cannot assume will remain attached. Its matched 12-attempt cohort produced two candidate native terminals versus three for the original list, with four candidate attempts capped; Spiritomb was on the Bench in 3/6. Again, no competitive ordering is established.

These are not interchangeable one-card techs: they represent a real breadth-versus-self-interference trade-off. Preserve both as separate hypotheses; do not infer that the narrower Spiritomb lock solves the Iron Thorns field.

### Ability-lock effects are asymmetric (printed-text derivation; no new native test)

- **Iron Thorns ex → Rule-Box Pokémon, except Future.** It does not suppress non-Rule-Box Stage 1 Alolan Muk, Basic non-Rule-Box Spiritomb, or Basic Psychic Wobbuffet. Alolan Muk's Power of Alchemy can suppress Iron Thorns ex because Iron Thorns is a Basic Pokémon; Wobbuffet's Bide Barricade can suppress it while Wobbuffet is Active because Iron Thorns is non-Psychic. Spiritomb cannot: its text is limited to Basic Pokémon V in play, and Iron Thorns ex is not a Pokémon V.
- **Garbodor Garbotoxin → all other Pokémon Abilities in play, hand and discard while its Tool condition is live.** Garbodor is Stage 1, so neither Alolan Muk's Basic-only effect nor Spiritomb's Basic-Pokémon-V-only effect suppresses Garbotoxin. Garbotoxin would suppress the Muk, Spiritomb and Wobbuffet Abilities **unless the relevant Pokémon is protected by Stealthy Hood**. The Sentinel–Alchemy and Sentinel–Spiritomb lists each contain three Hoods, so a fully Hooded Stoutland/Vileplume/Muk-or-Spiritomb board is a possible protection line, but it depends on finding and attaching the Tools before they are removed or blanked. Thus Muk/Spiritomb do not disable Garbodor itself; they can preserve their own lock effects only while their own Hood protection holds. The Temple candidate contains no Hood, so Wobbuffet is exposed to an already-live Garbotoxin; do not assume it breaks that lock without a rules-validated ordering test. Field Blower's removal of the attached Tool is the direct counter family; Tool Jammer and other Tool answers can also defeat the protection.
- **Silent Lab → Basic Pokémon Abilities in play.** It can suppress Spiritomb and Wobbuffet (both Basic), and Iron Thorns ex (also Basic), but not Stage 1 Alolan Muk or Stage 1 Garbodor. This is another reason the lock pieces cannot be treated as interchangeable.
- **Stealthy Hood is a Pokémon Tool, not an Item.** The repository's Advanced Players' Manual B-02 states that Items and Pokémon Tools are separate Trainer-card types, and that older Pokémon Tools with “Item” printed on top are not treated as Items. Therefore Vileplume AOR3's Item-only lock and Budew's Item-only attack effect do **not** prevent playing Pokémon Tools such as Stealthy Hood or Float Stone from hand. They still prevent playing Item cards such as Heavy Ball, Field Blower and Canceling Cologne. Eri TEF 146 likewise discards Item cards only: it can discard Field Blower, but not a Pokémon Tool such as Hood or Float Stone from hand. Float Stone can therefore still supply a free-retreat route under an Item-only lock, provided the Pokémon has no existing Tool and no separate Tool lock/blanker applies. A separate effect that explicitly blocks Pokémon Tools (the manual's C-19 Jellicent ex example), or Tool blanking/removal, can still stop Hood. Hood protects the attached Pokémon from opposing Pokémon Abilities; it does not unlock Item cards or player-level Supporter restrictions such as Stoutland BCR's Sentinel. The latter is an effect on the player, not on the attached Pokémon.

These conclusions follow from the printed ability scopes and the source-list/card identities already audited; they are not native interaction results or matchup claims. See the [repository Advanced Players' Manual B-02/C-19](../../../resources/manual/EN_advanced_manual-2025-transcription-structured.md) for the Item/Tool distinction. In particular, no ordering claim is made for simultaneous or preemptive Wobbuffet/Garbotoxin states.

**Sentinel three-Hood opportunity:** both fixed Sentinel lists contain three Stealthy Hood copies and three intended lock Pokémon (Stoutland, Vileplume, and Muk or Spiritomb). In principle, one Hood on each lock piece can protect all three from opponent Ability effects such as Garbotoxin. Because Vileplume AOR3 locks Item cards but not Pokémon Tools, the Hoods can still be attached after Vileplume's Item lock is active; there is no AOR3 pre-lock requirement for Hood itself. This keeps the triple-Hood protection line possible against Garbotoxin, but it remains conditional on finding and attaching all three Tools and surviving Tool Jammer, Field Blower or other Tool removal/blanking. The Item lock still prevents the Sentinel player from using Item counters such as Field Blower or Heavy Ball. The two lock layers also constrain different answers: Stoutland BCR's Sentinel blocks Supporters such as Faba, and Vileplume AOR3 blocks Item cards such as Field Blower and Megaton Blower, but Tool Jammer is itself a Pokémon Tool and can still be played to blank the Hoods. Rank 3 Snorlax Block carries Tool Jammer, so it is a residual counter to the fully Hooded Sentinel board. Conversely, rank7's Megaton Blower (Item) and Faba (Supporter) are blocked by Vileplume/Stoutland respectively if both locks remain live, protecting the Hoods from those removal channels. The Sentinel 60 itself has no Field Blower or Faba, so it cannot directly remove Tool Jammer. Its apparent in-list answer is to gust/KO the Tool Jammer carrier (three Guzma and two Mega Lopunny ex are present), but that costs a Supporter/attack sequence and may require moving Stoutland out of the Active Spot, temporarily releasing the opponent's Supporter lock and reopening Faba. Tool Jammer can also be held and attached after both locks because it is a Pokémon Tool. This is an untested tactical tradeoff, not evidence that the list reliably answers Tool Jammer.

### Enamorus is a board-wide Ability shield, not a universal lock escape

The existing [Hooded Guardian Shadow Rider v1 60](../candidate-hooded-guardian-shadow-rider.md) is a whole-deck challenger arm, not a new per-matchup tech: it adds Enamorus V SWSH11 82 and cuts Quick Ball FST 237 from Hood Shield v1, leaving 19 Pokémon / 30 Trainers / 11 Psychic Energy. Use [Agent43's canonical board-shield report](agent43-enamorus-v-board-shield-hypothesis-20261117.md) for the full card-text/Q&A and timing analysis, and [Agent44's slot addendum](agent44-enamorus-hood-boardwide-ability-shield-20261119.md) for the exact 60 and Ranger/Quick Ball rationale. The 20 November follow-up adds several decision-relevant corrections.

1. **Scope is Pokémon-Ability effects only.** Guardian of Love protects each of the candidate's Pokémon with Psychic Energy attached from effects of opposing Pokémon Abilities, except Enamorus itself. This can protect multiple energized Pokémon at once, unlike a single Stealthy Hood. It does not remove effects applied to the player: Budew's Itchy Pollen Item restriction, Vileplume AOR3's Item lock, and Stoutland's Supporter lock remain distinct. It also does not stop Stadium effects (including Silent Lab / Path to the Peak), attacks, or Pokémon Tool effects. Keep Pokémon Ranger in the exact arm: it can clear attack effects on players such as Budew's, but it does not clear Ability-origin AOR3 or Stoutland restrictions.

2. **The Enamorus anchor is the exposed point.** Enamorus is excluded from its own Guardian effect and is itself a Rule Box Pokémon V, so it can be suppressed by Iron Thorns Initialization or Garbotoxin. A Hood attached to Enamorus is the proposed anchor protection; the printed Hood text plus the official θ Stop-versus-Garbotoxin precedent strongly support the composition in which Hood preserves Guardian on Enamorus and Guardian then protects the other Psychic-Energy-attached Pokémon. This remains a strong paper-rules inference, not a direct Enamorus/Garbotoxin FAQ or a native Twinleaf interaction result. Do not promote it to a verified interaction before the trace stopline clears and the existing test path can be used.

3. **AOR3 does not close every Enamorus access route, but the route has hard Prize failures.** The exact 60 retains Arven and Forest Seal Stone. Under the repository manual's B-02 Tool classification, Forest Seal Stone is a Pokémon Tool, not an Item; Arven is a Supporter. Thus the conditional post-lock line is Arven → find Forest Seal Stone as the Tool target → attach it → use Star Alchemy to search Enamorus. Arven still searches for an Item plus a Pokémon Tool; the Item can be put into hand but remains unplayable under AOR3, while FSS is the operative Tool target. This route still requires a Pokémon V with an open Tool slot, an unused VSTAR Power, and the Arven Supporter turn. Star Alchemy searches the deck, not the Prizes: if Enamorus is Prized, FSS cannot retrieve it and Heavy Ball is an Item that remains locked; if FSS itself is Prized, Arven cannot fetch it.

   **Access-model reconciliation (static category review; author confirmation pending):** Agent43's Forest Seal enumerator uses the category vector Enamorus 1 / Hood 2 / Shadow Rider V 4 / VMAX 4 / Fog Crystal 4 / Arven 1 / Heavy Ball 1 / other Basic 10 / Float Stone 1 / FSS 1 / residual 31. The exact Quick Ball-cut arm has the same modeled counts. The only slot-cut difference is inside the residual 31 (the model retains Quick Ball and cuts a non-Item Trainer; the exact arm does the reverse), but the code treats that residual as undifferentiated and does not use Quick Ball or Mysterious Treasure as search routes. Thus the reported 24.8928% (going first) / 29.3519% (going second) anchor-only and 4.0990% / 6.0508% joint anchor+core results appear transferable as a **restricted no-lock route-access sensitivity**, not a full exact-arm access estimate. All four access rates are conditional on a valid opening seven; the model reports 88.2499% initial valid-opening probability for the 15-Basic list. Important accounting correction: the report/C++ comment counts FSS as an Item, but manual B-02 says SWSH-era Tools are not treated as Items. The exact arm has 15 actual Items, not 17; the code uses Fog Crystal / Heavy Ball as Item targets and FSS as a Tool. When Arven is already in hand, at most 14 other cards are unavailable in the modeled opening/Prize/draw window, so at least one of the 15 Items remains as its Item target. This does not validate the route under AOR3/Ability lock or resolve Tool slots, VSTAR timing, Energy, sequencing, or opponent disruption.

4. **Rank 7 Snorlax Block is an additional Guardian test hypothesis.** Rank 7's source list contains Snorlax PGO 55 (Block) ×2, Tool Jammer ×1, and Wobbuffet PHF 36 ×3; see [Agent17's rank-7 resource-war report](agent17-rank7-snorlax-megaton-blower-hood-resource-war-20261117.md) for the baseline Tool-Jammer / Field-Blower / Hood line. Manual C-14 defines “can't retreat” as an effect on the affected Pokémon; Guardian may therefore prevent Block from restricting a Psychic-Energy-attached Active. This matters because Tool Jammer can blank a Hood on the Active, whereas Guardian is a Pokémon Ability rather than a Pokémon Tool. Wobbuffet's Bide Barricade exempts Psychic Pokémon, so it does not suppress Psychic-type Enamorus; moreover, Wobbuffet's Ability is Active-only and cannot be live at the same time as Snorlax's Active-only Block. A concrete whole-deck sequencing hypothesis exists when both opponent pieces are in play: if Wobbuffet is Active and Snorlax is Benched, Guzma can bring Snorlax Active (turning off Bide Barricade) and switch your Active with an energized Bench Pokémon; this is a switch effect, not a normal retreat. Then play Radiant Eternatus from hand and use Climactic Gate to Bench up to two VMAX (ending the turn). Guardian's role is to prevent Snorlax Block from trapping that Psychic-Energy-attached Active on subsequent turns, not to make Guzma's switch legal. The candidate 60 contains both Guzma and Radiant Eternatus. Do not assume Guardian directly rescues Radiant Eternatus's Climactic Gate while Wobbuffet is Active: Radiant is a Basic Dragon and its on-play Ability resolves before Psychic Energy can be attached; Underworld Door cannot target it. If played while Bide Barricade is live, the Ability does not trigger retroactively when Wobbuffet later leaves the Active Spot. The Guzma sequence must therefore turn Wobbuffet off **before** Radiant is played. This line requires Enamorus/Guardian already live, Snorlax on the opponent's Bench, Guzma and Radiant Eternatus in hand, an energized candidate Bench Pokémon to become Active, VMAX targets still in deck, and no other restriction; it is not a native result. **AMR / resource reality:** this is a contingency, not a routine plan. It requires the exact opponent board plus three singletons (Enamorus, Guzma, Radiant Eternatus), consumes the turn's Supporter, and Climactic Gate ends the turn without an attack. If Enamorus is Benched, Enamorus + Radiant Eternatus + two VMAX occupy four of five Bench slots, leaving one for all other engine/support Pokémon. Treat the line as a possible high-leverage response to a Wobbuffet/Tool-Jammer board, not as expected matchup value. This is an Archetype-Line-Specific (ALS) sequencing hypothesis rather than a generic Enamorus card-strength bonus; see [human-developed concepts](../../../resources/human_concepts.md) for ALS, lock-interaction, AMR, UDP and Bench-constraint framing. This remains a rules-derived hypothesis, not native validation. Guardian removes only the Block restriction: the Active still needs a payable retreat cost or another legal switch effect, and it must already have Psychic Energy attached. The baseline Hood line can protect one Active, but rank 7's Tool Jammer / Field Blower / Megaton Blower / Faba package makes that Tool line vulnerable; Enamorus may avoid spending Hood and Field Blower solely to answer Block. Tool Jammer also blanks Forest Seal Stone while Snorlax is Active, so the FSS access sensitivity is not an escape under that board: Guardian must already be online or Enamorus must be found through the non-Tool search package (Fog Crystal / Mysterious Treasure) before the Tool lock matters.

5. **Rank 15 is a plausible Guardian interaction, but not a unique Enamorus win.** Rank 15 combines Vileplume AOR3's player-level Item lock with Vileplume BUS6's Active-only Basic attack restriction. The repository manual's C-15 defines “can't attack / can't use attacks” as an effect applied to the affected Pokémon; therefore Guardian may prevent BUS6 from restricting a Psychic-Energy-attached Mimikyu and preserve Copycat while AOR3's Item lock remains. This is a rules-derived hypothesis pending native validation, not a confirmed interaction. Baseline Hood Shield already has Arven plus Stealthy Hood and can protect a single Mimikyu, so rank 15 alone is not an Enamorus-specific win. Guardian's incremental value exists only when multiple relevant Psychic-Energy Pokémon need simultaneous protection, or when the one-Hood line is committed elsewhere. Keep the rank-2 AOR3 + Vileplume-GX list distinct from rank 15's AOR3 + BUS6 list.

6. **The Quick Ball cut trades redundancy, not unique Pokémon coverage.** Every Pokémon in the exact arm is Psychic- or Dragon-type, so Mysterious Treasure covers Quick Ball's target set. Cutting Quick Ball therefore does not remove a unique Pokémon target, but it removes the ninth independent search connector and a useful Dialga/Mimikyu discard-and-access route; it is not a free cut. The exact arm retains Pokémon Ranger, which matters because Budew appears in 14/24 identity-screened lists and Ranger is the relevant attack-effect answer. Do not attach access rates from the alternative Ranger-cut / Quick-Ball-retaining sensitivity to this arm.

7. **Energy and Bench are part of the shield cost.** Guardian can protect any Pokémon with Psychic Energy, regardless of its type; Underworld Door can supply Psychic Energy only to a Benched Psychic Pokémon. Sixteen of the candidate's 18 non-Enamorus Pokémon are Psychic and eligible for Underworld Door; Dialga-GX and Radiant Eternatus are not, so their protection requires the normal attachment or another effect. Enamorus also consumes a Bench slot and adds a two-Prize target. The policy must value which Pokémon actually need protection against the next opposing Ability effect, while preserving energy for the attack plan and enough Bench capacity for the Shadow Rider engine. Do not assume a full protected board is established merely because the deck contains Enamorus, Hoods, and Psychic Energy.

**Decision rule:** keep the exact Enamorus arm as an existing, unpromoted challenger. Do not test or alter it before the registered first-trace and matched rank-1 policy-competence milestones. After those milestones, compare it with the unchanged Hood Shield baseline only if the baseline trace / development games expose losses materially caused by opposing Pokémon Abilities. Use the same common candidate policy, exact opponent list, public seed schedule, both candidate seats, both actual first-player assignments, and failure-inclusive denominator; include the non-Ability-lock matchups so a board-shield gain cannot hide broader consistency losses. If the baseline failures are not Ability-lock-driven, defer the arm rather than expanding access models or isolated card tests.

## 4. What the evidence supports—and what it does not

1. **Lock presence is not lock access.** A source list containing Garbodor, Iron Thorns or Vileplume does not show that the relevant card was drawn, evolved, placed Active, equipped, or kept in play.
2. **A counter in the deck is not a counter in hand.** Field Blower, Cologne, Ranger and Hood each have different target, timing and lock restrictions. A decklist-level answer does not prove an executable line.
3. **A locally valid lock can disable the candidate's own plan.** Wobbuffet, Alolan Muk, Stoutland, Vileplume and Stadium effects all have positional or symmetrical costs. A policy must score the entire continuation, not reward the lock board in isolation.
4. **Tool protection is not durable by default.** The source-list Tool-removal census is broad enough that a two-Hood package cannot be treated as a permanent shield. Conversely, a source-list Tool answer is not an automatic counter unless it can be accessed and played through the current Item/Supporter/Ability constraints.
