# Original rank-11 natural Professor's Letter: real selected Store action, six Prize follow-through and a native winner reversal

**Agent 10 | 3 November 2026 | Retrospective opening intervention | Developmental evidence only**

## Decision

A missed owner-only resource Item can change downstream gameplay materially. The selected-v3 source first-turn archived state had seven Trainer cards, one printed `Professors Letter BKT`, no Darkness Energy, and the authentic rank11 source 60 contained eight Darkness. A separate preregistered native A/B/A intervention reused the already tested symmetric Agent11 after-Pass resource Tutor bridge against **both unchanged candidate 60s**, with the exact two historical source physical opening deals.

**Observed:** All **12/12** native attempts reached engine terminal states without abort or cap. All **4/4** physical candidate/source contexts had matching complete initial deal digests among control A, treatment and repeat control. All **4/4** complete control-versus-repeat root ledger hashes were identical. Across treatment arms, **three** Letter Items were actually accepted by the Store, **three** owner-authenticated two-card searches settled, and **six** physical Darkness Energy cards moved from deck to hand. At rank11 versus champion-reference with the source first in seat1, the treatment subsequently selected ten real `Energy Drive` attacks, took **two + two + two** settled Prize cards, and reversed the native game winner from candidate seat0 to original source seat1. Zero competitive paper games are thereby qualified.

## Evidence limits and research consequences

- All outcome claims are **native Twinleaf simulator results** using its pinned original Store, tested actor prompt hooks and actual settled public Prize snapshots. They are neither independent paper games nor certified full-rules Expanded matches.
- Original rank11 opponent policy in the selected-v3 experiment previously skipped a provably two-target Letter resource line. The default-off Tutor intervention can genuinely execute that line and produce a huge observed consequence on the same historical physical source opening. This means benchmark candidate performance can be strongly distorted by incomplete opposing source decision policies.
- The run is **retrospective**, the two source opening seeds were selected after observing a skipped Letter line, and there are only **two independent source physical openings**, four distinct complete candidate/source contexts. It cannot be used for population-level win-rate estimation, significance testing for broad improvement or a 99.5% field claim. No full original Top32 match clock, other opponents or paper lock validation was performed. The stronger source policy sometimes makes its opponent lose but also in one context accelerates a win already present.
- Do not reduce this to a generic tutor-always-good rule. Early Letter can compete with holding Energy for other effects, and a source 60 can have cards that alter payoff. A correct optimizer must measure chosen actions, actual hand/deck transfers, follow-through, full opponent behavior and terminal conditions. Treat this as **one matched causal development counterexample** to the assumption that a printed resource Item's mere presence establishes competent opponent play.
- The original 2026 Aichi field remains **29 of 32** authenticated 60-card source lists; missing ranks **23, 25, 28**. Zero competitively qualified games, no final fixed 60, and no credible 99.5% claim. Future programs should include a default-off tested owner-safe Tutor bridge in *both* seats under a single selected policy, update source-competence benchmark contracts, and rerun genuinely held-out independent physical deals before inferring actual deck strength.
