The actual settled Max Geist source board immediately before attack had **eight Basic Psychic Energy cards**: Active VMAX three, Benched Float-Stone VMAX three (two before retreat plus one subsequent Underworld Door attachment), and Benched Girafarig two. A **different, plausible printed legal route** at source turn34 would skip Float Stone, pay the VMAX's printed Retreat Cost of **two Colorless** by discarding its two attached Basic Psychic Energy, and promote the already-funded other three-Psychic VMAX. Even with no later attachment, the source would retain five Basic Psychic across its board, yielding **160 Max Geist base damage**; if the second Underworld Door still attaches a Basic Psychic to an eligible Benched Psychic Pokémon, six would give **190 damage**. The actual opponent's printed Lunatone OBF 92 has **90 HP** and had **10 damage** already at the attack snapshot, no Tool or Stadium; either hypothetical base damage exceeds its remaining 80 HP. This strictly identifies a **resource-preserving Tool use**, not proven immediate knockout necessity. In the same printed no-modifier state, preserving the two Psychic increases Max Geist's damage by **60** (250 versus 190 with a later Underworld Door). Immediate Knock Out necessity from that difference would arise only for a target with **191–250 remaining HP**, barring Weakness, Resistance or other effects; the actual Lunatone had 80 remaining HP. The paid-retreat branch has **not** been executed as a native controlled intervention, so its occurrence under the actual decision policy, all interaction effects and future Energy economy remain open. This strengthens the reason to evaluate opportunity costs and actual selected roots instead of attributing an entire win to a theoretical connector.
