## Terminal outcome and tactical diagnosis

- Original A/C matched identical original physical deals, first seats, source printing sixties; A/A-prime entire native roots and final boards reproduced exactly.
- Rank1 in C dealt settled 10 damage repeatedly across 35 attacks, took **exactly two real native Prize cards at turn56** via Rotom V Knock Out, and had a source Articuno-GX at 160 damage at turn88. A/B took none.
- The C game ended at native turn90: source physical seat0 had zero deck cards, six remaining Prizes; rank1 physical seat1 had zero deck cards and four remaining Prizes. The native winner remained source rank3 physical seat0 through the owner's next-turn deck-out drawing rule. A ended at turn88 with owner zero deck cards and six remaining Prizes, same native winner. This is real native progress but **does not flip the outcome**.
- The complete C A/B pairing establishes a two-step policy dependency: moving one Basic Energy into an otherwise stranded Active does not automatically retreat; the later typed native paid-retreat action enables settled attack effects. The policy remains conservative: it chooses a funded positive attack even when the resulting 10 damage per turn cannot reliably overcome the deck-out clock. A more informed policy needs to compare attack damage and tactical lock benefits to the opponent's remaining HP, remaining turn resources and available Bench attackers, with owner-only public and legally known information.

## Limits and followthrough

Thirty-five settlements belong to **one** correlated historical game, not 35 statistically independent matches. It is a two-original-rank source development experiment against an incomplete opponent policy and pinned simulator, with no independent opponent response competence, no 25-minute paper match clock, and no audited full card/effect parity. The 29/32 authentic Top32 published original sixties still lack ranks23,25,28. This contributes **zero competitively qualified games**, no selected fixed 60-card winner and no proof of the 99.5% target. Next evaluate a stronger source reply and owner-funded attack opportunity with genuine post-prompt Store effects before considering a fixed-deck change.
