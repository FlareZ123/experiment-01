# Agent47 — rank-13 Regidrago VS Seeker → Crispin → funded Apex/Shred endgame

## What the live source-bound worker does wrong

Unchanged original Aichi rank13 (Hiromu Sasaki) contains VS Seeker ×3, Guzma ×2, Crispin ×1, Dialga-GX UPR100 ×1, Regidrago VSTAR SIT136 ×3, 4 Basic Grass and 3 Basic Fire. The Agent24 rank13 worker's existing VS Seeker choice ranks **Guzma 240** whenever any opposing Bench Pokémon exists, and **Crispin 135** unconditionally; it does not account for missing Energy on the active Regidrago. The existing policy also scores each Crispin deck-search choice independently, assigning Grass 210 and Fire 200, so with two Grass and one Fire available it can select two Grass even though Crispin requires *different* Basic Energy types. These are concrete actor-only policy behavior defects, not estimates of their real-game frequency.

Constructed **actor-visible final-Prize opportunity** (both seats): own Regidrago VSTAR active with Basic Grass + Basic Fire, own discard includes Dialga-GX, Crispin and Guzma, one Prize left, unused Supporter, opposing Active Mimikyu GRI58 (70 remaining HP), opposing Bench nonempty. VS Seeker offers both Supporters. The unmodified policy selects Guzma. If **both different Basic Energy types are still in the deck**, recovery of Crispin, selection of one Grass plus one Fire, and attaching Grass to the active Regidrago yields its printed [Grass,Grass,Fire] Apex Dragon requirement. Copying discard Dialga-GX's 80-damage **Shred** can Knock Out that unprotected 70-HP Active and take the last Prize. This is a **conditional tactical opportunity**, not a game result; the constructed view does not attest concealed deck content or opponent best-response probability. Another visible gust-to-Bench line may sometimes be stronger; do not generalize from this one terminal geometry.

## Actor information limits applied in the actual selector

The latest branch uses the exact source Basic Energy multiset (Grass ×4, Fire ×3) and **declines the Crispin branch when fewer than two cards remain in own deck, or all copies of either type are already accounted for in actor-visible hand/board/discard/Lost Zone**, with positive and negative controls. It also uses `ownerK1.knownPrintings` **only when exactMembershipKnown=true and invalidationReason=null** to reject a search when the authenticated owner's deck has no Grass or no Fire; stale K1 does not veto. This avoids pretending that a theoretically accessible Supporter can conjure unavailable Basic Energy.

**Important native turn-counter correction:** `ownTurnResources.supporterPlayedTurn` counts Supporters already played **this turn**; it is **not a global-turn timestamp**. A nonzero value vetoes recovery, even at global turn 5. The first implementation incorrectly compared the count with the global turn; corrected before handoff/promotion, with a one-Supporter-used negative added. Both improvements are grounded in existing `simulation/twinleaf/actor-k1-search-plan-veto.cjs` and current actor protocol; no extra private deck access.

## Executable root decision improvement: don't lose the winning line before VS Seeker

The original rank13 worker scores **Legacy Star 245** when VSTAR power unused versus **VS Seeker 125** even if VS Seeker can recover Crispin for an immediate one-Prize Shred knockout. Legacy Star **does not end the turn**, so that priority alone does not prove a lost game. However, the pinned attack/Ability source explicitly says Legacy Star discards the **top seven cards of the deck** and spends the VSTAR Power before selecting two discard cards; those intermediate discards can remove the scarce Basic Fire Energy from the deck needed for Crispin. Merely fixing the VS Seeker prompt is therefore insufficient to preserve this already-certified endgame opportunity when another root action can disrupt its prerequisites. The new `chooseRank13VsSeekerFundingRoot` in `agent47-rank13-vsseeker-funding-line.cjs` is a stricter root-only selector: it requires the same physical one-Prize funding geometry, **certified K0 or consistent fresh-K1 Grass+Fire in deck**, Crispin in own discard, VS Seeker in own hand, exactly one offered native `play-card` VS Seeker token, no playable immediate attack in the enumerated frontier, untouched Supporter allowance and no relevant locks. It then overrides other setup action scores with an actor-visible `select-action` for VS Seeker. Nonmatching states delegate to unchanged Agent24 worker rather than commanding a speculative line.

`tests/2026-11/agent47-rank13-crispin-root-priority.test.cjs` compares **both physical seats** in the same synthetic root with an executable-looking Legacy Star competitor: **unchanged Agent24 chooses Legacy Star**; overlay v2 selects VS Seeker. Eleven negative mutations (used Supporter, no Crispin/VS Seeker, depleted deck, exhausted Fire, non-Prize terminal, existing attack, duplicate VS Seeker action, etc.) verify delegation to the original policy. Another group verifies role/seat/state/frontier spoof rejection. These are policy/typed-frontier executions, not native games.

This is not just a cosmetic score change. Under an explicitly **uniform 18-card remaining deck** (the synthetic root's deck count), there are either **one or two Fire** in the deck in the exact one-Prize geometry. If the player uses Legacy Star to discard seven *before* searching with Crispin, the probability that **no Fire remains in deck** is `7/18 ≈ 38.9%` with one Fire, or `C(7,2)/C(18,2)=21/153≈13.7%` with two. These are exact conditional hypergeometric calculations, **not simulation wins**; they ignore any subsequent recovery or alternative attack and do not assign probabilities to the hidden Prize identity. They explain why the tempo/ordering of an otherwise strong Ability can materially erase a previously certain 1-Prize search line. The conservative root override selects the immediate established win path, not a statistically averaged card.

The manual staged native smoke adds `AGENT47_STRICT_ROOTS=1 AGENT47_LEGACY_STAR_COMPETITOR=1`, which makes Legacy Star legally available and requires that the **actual native enumerator** offers it and the baseline chooses it while the overlay chooses VS Seeker on that exact same physical actor state. This comparator was V8 **syntax-compiled only**, **not run** against the pinned Store; any native failure must be treated as a genuine blocking result, not evidence of success.

## Stronger exact K0 result: one Prize mathematically certifies both Basic types

The new `assessRank13CrispinEnergyPair` helper distinguishes `certified-k0-lower-bound`, `certified-fresh-k1`, `possible-k0`, `blocked`, and `invalid`. This is not a deck search and never examines physical Prize identities. With the source rank13's **4 Grass/3 Fire**, one Grass and one Fire attached to the Active Regidrago, no other copies in owner-visible zones, and **one Prize remaining**, there must be **at least 2 Grass and 1 Fire in the owner's remaining deck**: after subtracting observed attachments, 3 Grass + 2 Fire remain unaccounted and at most one card of either type can be in the single Prize. Thus the worked final-Prize `VS Seeker → Crispin` setup is not merely a speculative search; that specific Energy pair is **guaranteed by copy conservation**, independent of randomness or the hidden Prize identity.

For general source-visible states, let `g=4-visibleGrass`, `f=3-visibleFire`, and `p=ownPrizesRemaining`. The certified per-type lower bounds are `max(0,g-p)` and `max(0,f-p)`. A fresh actor-owned K1 deck membership takes precedence only when consistent with original copy counts and combined remaining Prize capacity. Inconsistencies fail closed; stale K1 cannot veto. A K0 proof certifies **Energy availability only**, not card/attack legality, the use of Crispin, or a real game win. The prompt decision still may choose a possible but unproven K0 pair where the alternative Guzma pivot is visibly unfunded. The attachment continuation now refuses to co-opt already-funded Regidrago states and only attaches Grass to Active when it completes the actual `{Grass,Grass,Fire}` Apex cost.

## Cross-rank Regidrago consequence: same source text, different last-Fire certainty

The branch exports `assessSourceCountCrispinEnergyPair(view, {grass, fire})` as a conservative, source-list-parameterized proof utility while the rank13 policy wrapper retains authenticated rank13 copies `{grass:4, fire:3}`. The full Aichi manifest shows **rank14 and rank20 both run Crispin SCR, Grass ×4, Fire ×2**. On the same hypothetical one-Prize state with one Grass and one Fire attached and no other visible Basic copies, all three ranks have a K0 lower bound of **at least 2 Grass in deck**, but ranks14/20 have **no guaranteed Fire** because their one unaccounted Fire might be the sole Prize. Rank13's extra Fire copy is what creates the deterministic two-type certificate. The **source-exact native Crispin prompt projector** could be reused for all three family lists because the printed Crispin effect and Grass/Fire types match; the rank13 **win-line selector and K0 proof must not be silently transplanted**. This difference may be relevant to deck-slot tradeoffs but is not a recommendation to add a Fire copy without full matchup evaluation.
