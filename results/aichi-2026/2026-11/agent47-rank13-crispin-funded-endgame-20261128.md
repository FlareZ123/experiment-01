## What the live source-bound worker does wrong

Unchanged original Aichi rank13 (Hiromu Sasaki) contains VS Seeker ×3, Guzma ×2, Crispin ×1, Dialga-GX UPR100 ×1, Regidrago VSTAR SIT136 ×3, 4 Basic Grass and 3 Basic Fire. The Agent24 rank13 worker's existing VS Seeker choice ranks **Guzma 240** whenever any opposing Bench Pokémon exists, and **Crispin 135** unconditionally; it does not account for missing Energy on the active Regidrago. The existing policy also scores each Crispin deck-search choice independently, assigning Grass 210 and Fire 200, so with two Grass and one Fire available it can select two Grass even though Crispin requires *different* Basic Energy types. These are concrete actor-only policy behavior defects, not estimates of their real-game frequency.

Constructed **actor-visible final-Prize opportunity** (both seats): own Regidrago VSTAR active with Basic Grass + Basic Fire, own discard includes Dialga-GX, Crispin and Guzma, one Prize left, unused Supporter, opposing Active Mimikyu GRI58 (70 remaining HP), opposing Bench nonempty. VS Seeker offers both Supporters. The unmodified policy selects Guzma. If **both different Basic Energy types are still in the deck**, recovery of Crispin, selection of one Grass plus one Fire, and attaching Grass to the active Regidrago yields its printed [Grass,Grass,Fire] Apex Dragon requirement. Copying discard Dialga-GX's 80-damage **Shred** can Knock Out that unprotected 70-HP Active and take the last Prize. This is a **conditional tactical opportunity**, not a game result; the constructed view does not attest concealed deck content or opponent best-response probability. Another visible gust-to-Bench line may sometimes be stronger; do not generalize from this one terminal geometry.
