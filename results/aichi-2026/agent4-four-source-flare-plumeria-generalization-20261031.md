## Mechanistic public Energy supply threshold

The **preregistered treatment threshold was one attached Active Energy**, so it explores actual one-Energy scenarios intentionally excluded from the earlier rank6 experiment.

A post-run, source-visible count on the native first chosen Team Flare root partitions the fifteen registered positive cases **without altering their treatment, seeds or outcomes**:

| Opposing Active Energy cards when first Team Flare was selected | Selected and native-settled first Flare cases | Later Plumeria fully paid and second Energy removed |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| **Exactly one** | **11** | **0** |
| **At least two** | **4** | **4** |
| **All** | **15** | **4** |

Among the four multi-Energy first roots, the public opposing Active initially held **two, two, three or five Energy cards** respectively. Each completed the extra two-turn sequence: genuine future source `Plumeria BUS` played, two genuine owner-hand cards selected and accepted, and a second opposing **Psychic Energy MEE** actually removed. For each the control already discarded two own hand cards for Plumeria at the first turn and then passed at its next owner turn. In the four alternative branches, the future Plumeria paid **the same two printed cards** as the original early control payment, now on the next owner turn. The first Team Flare therefore gained an extra Energy-discard effect along these observed paths without an extra two-card hand-payment, while consuming a second Supporter turn and retaining temporal disruption risk.

The exact verified four late Plumeria payment printings were:

| --- | --- | --- |
| Rank3 `3:0:B:champion-reference` | 47 -> 49 | Arven OBF + Lusamine CIN |
| Rank24 `24:1:A:champion-reference` | 29 -> 31 | Double Colorless Energy SUM + Enhanced Hammer TWM |

**One** additional real opponent first action changed, on rank29 `29:1:B:champion-reference`: after the second source Plumeria, opposing rank1 bot's original `Shadow Mist` attack at global turn38 became `PassTurnAction` in the Team Flare treatment. Other next-opponent actions across these four were unchanged, or in the rank24 turn72 case absent before the native action cap.

**Interpretation:** the exact supplied native evidence supports a strong conditional prediction: *a two-Energy public Active Spot was sufficient for the second distinct denial along these particular sampled trajectories; one Energy was not sufficient in their continuation*. The count alone is **not a universal paper rule**: a single-Energy opponent can manually reattach, have another Energy on their Bench, switch, or use recovery by the next turn. The observed division is also unrandomized with respect to Active Energy count, source deck, opponent and game turn; it is a mechanistic explanation **and a hypothesis**, not a causal estimate of the effect of changing the threshold itself. A static rule favoring first Flare at one Energy risks producing a next-turn dead Plumeria.

Combining the earlier **independently preregistered rank6 study** (5/16 selected first Team Flare under the **two-Energy** protocol, all 5/5 later full two-card Plumeria + second opposing Energy settlement) with these four source ranks yields **20 genuinely native-accepted first Flare choices** across **48 matched game pairs, 96 original Store attempted games**, and **9 fully settled two-turn Energy denial paths**. They represent at most **15 distinct positive first-source-deal fingerprints** (rank6 four, four others eleven). The protocols intentionally have **different minimum public Active Energy thresholds** and are not a randomized one-versus-two threshold comparison. All game-level winners remain unchanged and **zero outcomes are competitively qualified**.
