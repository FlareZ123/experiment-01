## Paper/card-text and selection-policy risk

The pinned upstream [Trevenant & Dusknoir-GX SMP 217 implementation](https://github.com/twinleafgg/twinleafgg/blob/a9905a76a6c4103ba4b6cb9a49e86bad795c5c05/ptcg-server/src/sets/08-sun-and-moon/set-sun-and-moon-promos/trevenant-dusknoir-gx.ts) states **choose two random cards from opponent's hand**, and sets `cardsToShuffle=Math.min(2, opponent.hand.cards.length)`. It then opens a `ChooseCardsPrompt` addressed to the **attacker** with `cards=opponent.hand`, `isSecret:true`, and fixed min/max `cardsToShuffle`. The printed attack must choose 2 random cards if 2+ are present.

The generic pinned [ChooseCardsPromptResolver](https://github.com/twinleafgg/twinleafgg/blob/a9905a76a6c4103ba4b6cb9a49e86bad795c5c05/ptcg-server/src/simple-bot/prompt-resolver/choose-cards-prompt-resolver.ts) ranks **individual actual card objects by `getCardScore(state,prompt.playerId,card)`** before returning the first legal selections. Since this prompt supplies **the opponent's private hand**, the autonomous attacker's selection path appears **card-identity-dependent and potentially information-advantaged**; the card text requires random sampling. This is a concrete evaluator-competence/fidelity risk. The net one-card count has now been definitively explained by the following turn's ordinary draw, and does not indicate a Night Watch shuffle-count bug. It requires a targeted randomized resolver correction and real Store isolation testing before games involving Night Watch can be qualified. Do not extrapolate from the successful engine winner.

### Exact pinned resolver scoring mechanism

The upstream [StateScore.getCardScore](https://github.com/twinleafgg/twinleafgg/blob/a9905a76a6c4103ba4b6cb9a49e86bad795c5c05/ptcg-server/src/simple-bot/state-score/state-score.ts) is now directly verified: for a card absent from the attacker's hand, it **temporarily pushes that same opponent-held card into the attacker's hand**, recomputes the attacker's `HandScore`, then pops it, returning the score delta. The [HandScore implementation](https://github.com/twinleafgg/twinleafgg/blob/a9905a76a6c4103ba4b6cb9a49e86bad795c5c05/ptcg-server/src/simple-bot/state-score/hand-score.ts) explicitly branches on actual card class, whether it is an Energy, Pokémon, Basic, Evolution, Supporter, or other Trainer, and on own board/hand synergies. The [prompt resolver](https://github.com/twinleafgg/twinleafgg/blob/a9905a76a6c4103ba4b6cb9a49e86bad795c5c05/ptcg-server/src/simple-bot/prompt-resolver/choose-cards-prompt-resolver.ts) sorts the scored cards descending. Therefore the **default code path is demonstrably card-identity-dependent**. Hidden-card random selection requires a different rule, regardless of whether the observed selections happen to coincide with a legal pair. This source finding applies to the general generic resolver, and live prompt-interception precedence still requires test/qualification.
