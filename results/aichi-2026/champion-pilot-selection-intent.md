# Champion's public first-person deck-selection rationale

**Agent 5, 16 October 2026. Evidence type: translation and strategic interpretation of a contemporary first-person public excerpt.** No actual match outcome sample was measured here.

## Primary source and accessible scope

Yasunori Kato ("Yasunori", ヤスノリ), 2026 Aichi May Open champion, published [his Shadow Rider Calyrex Expanded report](https://note.com/yasunori_1028/n/n8eca50402c65) on **29 May 2026**. Its publicly viewable introductory sections describe the decision process. The subsequent approximately 8,488 Japanese characters and 14 images are paywalled and have not been accessed, so this finding does not reconstruct the private deck guide or individual game logs.

In his public description, Kato says:

- He anticipated that roughly **50–60% of entrants** would bring Regidrago VSTAR. This was a prospective metagame forecast, not a measured Aichi field share.
- The team's main candidates were Shadow Rider Calyrex, Mega Absol, and Regidrago. The team considered it difficult to build Mega Absol lines that consistently defeated Regidrago and narrowed the final testing to Shadow Rider versus Regidrago.
- For a tournament-winning run he considered it necessary to defeat Regidrago **about nine times in ten** while achieving **around six in ten** against other opponents. These percentages express a **deck-selection desideratum / pilot belief**, not demonstrated matchup probabilities.
- His objection to bringing Regidrago itself to the elimination bracket involved variance in mirrors, including turn order, opening hand quality, and Prized cards, as well as the possibility of deliberately unfavorable counterdecks. He chose Shadow Rider believing it offered a favorable Regidrago matchup and an approachable play plan.

2. The **90% / 60% statements are neither measured win rates nor evidence that any fixed deck meets the present 99.5% objective**. No sample sizes, uncertainty, opposing 60-card identities or side-balanced estimates accompany the publicly visible claims.
3. The 50–60% expectation is for entrants, while the [fixed benchmark field](field.csv) has eleven Regidrago placements among 32. Treating these as the same frequency would change the benchmark without justification.
