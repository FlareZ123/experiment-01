Among the 16 exact registered trials, source attack action counts dropped in **5** matched comparisons, rose in **2**, and remained equal in **9**. The native source attack root total fell by **16**. Native terminal-to-censor transitions were **two source-terminal to censored**, while **one candidate-terminal became a source-terminal**. Both arms remained censored in two trials. Neither action totals nor native terminal categories constitute a valid competitive win-rate estimator.

The most important development counterexample is `1:1:B:champion-reference`. The source's control arm recorded **seven** AttackAction roots and a native source terminal. Treatment used **eight** real original-source Underworld Door roots and recorded **zero** original-source attacks before the **260-action** censor. The parallel `1:1:B:new-moon-lockbreaker-v1` source fell from **eight** AttackAction roots and native source terminal to **one** source AttackAction and censorship, despite six paid source Underworld roots. Conversely `1:0:B:champion-reference` increased from **two** source attacks to **six**, shifting from candidate-native-terminal to source-native-terminal. These heterogeneous outcomes show why raw draw and payment counts are insufficient to decide policy quality.

One original control source terminal under `1:3:A:new-moon-lockbreaker-v1` remained a candidate-native-terminal after treatment, with source attacks falling four to one. The individual cases are evidence of **native decision-sequence differences**, with potentially confounded combat/policy consequences; they are unsuitable as proof of a particular tactical cause without further same-state public action and board reconstruction.

## Methodological conclusion

A reusable optimizer should treat **real owner root → validated prompt/payment → physical transition → legally enabled attack → opponent response → Prize and native terminal** as one connected decision process. Correctly executing extra Underworld Door actions solves a concrete mechanical and prompt-planning problem; two additional 260-root censored runs illustrate that the effect can coexist with poor attack follow-through in this particular native policy.

Next investigate why the source misses attacks in those two same-deal trial pairs. A promising *hypothesis*, requiring exact public state and native action tests, is priority/contention between low-cost Ability use, Energy recipient selection, and active attacker funding. Other explanations include changed opponent reactions, card draw sequencing, a reached engine action cap, or unrelated unmodeled effects. A newly preregistered held-out replay should follow any fixed policy improvements.
