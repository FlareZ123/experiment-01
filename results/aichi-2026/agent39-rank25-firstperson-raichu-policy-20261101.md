## Player-sourced opponent-model requirements

The author identifies asymmetric first/second threats and responses. These are direct statements of *intended mechanisms*; all native execution remains untested.

1. **Going first against Regidrago:** early Basic-Ability suppression is valuable for keeping Regidrago's `Latias ex` and `Tapu Lele-GX` unavailable and reducing the risk of a going-second `Budew` Item lock. The author considered `Klefki`, `Wobbuffet` and `Boost Shake` + `Garbodor`, and explicitly preferred a **Klefki**-capable construction after weighing slot burden and self-lock behavior. **It is not evidence that Klefki was actually active in every recorded game**.
2. **Going second:** the author requires outs to `Path to the Peak`, opposing `Sudowoodo` and Active `Wobbuffet/Klefki` before a prospective Regidrago VSTAR second-turn assault. They list `Marshadow` as a Stadium answer; `Wobbuffet/Klefki/Empoleon V` as Sudowoodo responses; and `Escape Rope/Canceling Cologne` as Active Wobbuffet/Klefki answers. Whether any answer exists in the unreleased rank25 sixty remains unknown.
3. **Preventing a Regidrago VSTAR second-turn attack:** listed possible answers include Energy disruption, `Horror House-GX`, an unspecified cry/lock attack and gust-plus-one-hit KO. This is a **possibility set, not claimed simultaneous inclusion**. A bot scoring only next-turn damage and ignoring this disruption/positioning channel cannot be assumed to represent human anti-Regidrago play.
4. **Regidrago source-policy strength matters:** the author describes Regidrago setting a Benched V with Energy, using Dedenne-GX/Squawkabilly ex draw, and presenting Active Wobbuffet when first, or Active Budew when second. Its VSTAR attack copying Dragon attacks and disruptive bonus-turn lines then condition later turns. These are opponent strategy descriptions, not proof that any particular source sixty consistently executes them.

The article explicitly contrasts first-turn permanent Ability-denial candidates with their own start-lock costs. A **policy that always favors a nominal Ability lock can self-lock the owner's essential setup**; a policy that always burns an Item search into draw could lose access to the relevant lock answer. Evaluate actual owner hand, opponent Active, Bench and lock state, fixed Supporter/Item contention and legal prompt settlement.
