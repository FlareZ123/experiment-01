# Statistical methodology for the Aichi field

The target is one frozen 60-card deck evaluated against all 32 Aichi Open League placements. There are two conditional field estimands and one natural-protocol estimand: the original **64 cells** (rank × candidate actual first/second), the current **128 cells** (rank × candidate physical seat × actual first/second), and **64 natural-protocol strata** (rank × candidate physical seat, with the coin winner and chooser's GO_FIRST policy decision left natural). The 128-cell mean is an **equal-start conditional** mean, not automatically the natural tournament-protocol win rate: CoinFlipPrompt selects the chooser, then the chooser's ConfirmPrompt/GO_FIRST response selects the actual starter. For a fair coin, the candidate-start weight is \(w_C=\tfrac12 a_C+\tfrac12(1-a_O)\), where \(a_C\) is the candidate's probability of choosing candidate-first when it wins the coin and \(a_O\) is the opponent's probability of choosing opponent-first when it wins. The natural win rate is \(w_Cp_F+(1-w_C)p_S\); equal-start sensitivity is \((p_F+p_S)/2\). Do not interchange these estimands or their certificates. Direct natural-protocol confirmation has 10 games per rank × candidate physical seat (640 total) with all wins giving \(0.995^{640}=0.0404363\), or 16 games per stratum (1,024 total) with at most one nonwin giving \(P\le0.0362584\); a 896/1,024 two-look schedule has combined bound 0.0474651. The 128-stratum equal-start design instead requires independent per-game CSPRNG seed material for shuffle, coin toss, other engine randomness and each policy stream. Prefer a precommitted balanced block schedule of actual starters (one candidate-first and one candidate-second game per rank × candidate physical seat), randomize condition order independently of all per-game random streams/seeds, keep the coin toss fair, and control the subsequent GO_FIRST response only for the equal-start conditional panel. Record coin winner, chooser, response and actual starter separately. The current runner uses one 32-bit xorshift32 physical stream for both shuffle and coin, its seededRandom(seed) truncates input to 32 bits, and deckPermutation() uses floor(randomFloat*bound); the future adapter needs a high-entropy generator plus unbiased rejection-sampled randomInt(bound) for Fisher–Yates. Do not merely pass it a 256-bit seed or seed-search/filter shuffles to populate first/second cells. Domain-separated streams from one finite master seed are computationally pseudorandom under a PRF assumption, not literally independent. All documents here are **methodological**; the repository has no competitively qualified win-rate sample.

The simple `assessCorrelatedFieldEvidence` API accepts caller-supplied complete rounds and cannot by itself detect an omitted abort. Its ledger-aware companion, `assessCorrelatedFieldCampaign`, now structurally validates a precommitted 64-game roster, shared-coin orientation receipt, append-only hash-chained game/terminal records, fixed deck/policy/engine/rules/runner identities, unique trial/game/seed/deal commitments, distinct candidate/opponent policy-process IDs, and a `FAIL_CLOSED` abort policy. It rejects missing registered game attempts or terminals, partial rounds, and explicit aborts before any e-process update. This remains structural validation only: signature verification, coin fairness/independence, physical-deal independence, actual process isolation/reset, and paper qualification are explicitly left to external audit. The validator does not produce receipts or run games; the two-consecutive-game reset regression and accepted field-campaign harness remain outstanding. This is a test result only. The separate `assessCorrelatedFieldCampaign` path now enforces the shared-coin/round-ledger contract structurally; it does not prove that the host sampled a fair coin, that physical deals were independently randomized, or that the producer supplied every attempted round. External custody/audit and a production runner remain outstanding. The validator's abort rule is per supplied ledger, not campaign-global: a new campaignId could restart after an abort unless external custody binds all attempts. Registration/round-start timestamps are self-reported, so an auditor must independently anchor precommitment before the trusted-host CSPRNG draw. The orientation coin does not make fixed-seed replays representative: every game must also use a fresh independently sampled physical deal from the target shuffle distribution; unique seed labels alone are not proof of independence or paper-shuffle fidelity. Synthetic adversarial CI demonstrates why seed uniqueness alone cannot justify the sharper independent-game e-process. No competitive games are certified.
