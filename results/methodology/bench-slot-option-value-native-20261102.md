# Bench-slot option value in genuine original-deal headless games

**Agent 5 | 2 November 2026 | methodological synthesis of two independently preserved native Store episodes.** This analysis sharpens the existing state-dependent Bench-space AMR idea from `resources/human_concepts.md`. It has **no competitively qualified games, fixed-deck nomination, game-win estimate or demonstrated 99.5% target**.

## Why the Bench decision should be valued as a constrained action

The generic legal action “put a Basic Pokémon from hand onto the Bench” may immediately protect against the opponent Knocking Out the player's only Pokémon. It also occupies a future slot that could be required by an unplayed primary attacker, search recipient or supporting Basic. The value changes sharply when the Bench is almost full, a core Pokémon is still absent, an opponent threatens a sole-Pokémon KO, or the deck's plan requires a lock to remain Active.

A candidate Bench choice can be analyzed as a *conditional owner-information opportunity cost*:

1. Immediate tactical reward of the actual Basic played: support Ability, evolving line, anti-KO backup, recipient, target, and current lock position.
2. Displaced future action space: any Basic subsequently drawn/searched that might require the last free slot, current optional connector uses, forced promotions and available Bench-clearing effects.
3. The opponent's current real threat: active attack funding, public KOs on next turn, gust, lock topology and clock pressure.
4. Uncertainty: where the stronger alternatives actually are, what the opponent can do from its concealed hand, and whether the present Basic itself is worth preserving.

A raw “number of legal bench connections” or “never bench an extra Basic” policy does not account for these competing constraints. This is a research decision framework, **not a demonstrated optimal decision rule**.

## Evidence A: opposite risk from preserving the sole Active Snorlax

[Agent19's prospective native source-rank3/rank7 12-pair, 24-attempt Snorlax experiment](../aichi-2026/agent19-snorlax-bench-preservation-native-results-20261021.md), CI [39611175734](https://github.com/FlareZ123/experiment-01/actions/runs/39611175734), authentic original source-deal matches 12/12. Its prospective optional-bench suppression diverged at **three solo-Snorlax original rank3 deals**. Two showed premature source native loss after the opponent's Full Moon Rondo because source had *no backup Pokémon*; ordinary Benching or subsequent supported access prolonged survival. Specific frozen source seeds: 4248970588, 2238730718. The third seed 4276588378 preserved the nominal setup but a Capture Energy effect still created Bench Pokémon later.

This is an **actual paired intervention**: unconditional “preserve empty Bench” can make a vulnerable only-Active board worse. It does not prove an optimal general Bench count or Snorlax performance, and all games are untimed, bot-dependent engine records.

## Evidence B: opposite risk from consuming the last New Moon Bench slot

[Agent5's native original rank9 Absol/Electrode versus unchanged New Moon source-policy contrast](../aichi-2026/agent5-rank9-known-dead-same-closure-native-20261102.md), treatment [41399309154](https://github.com/FlareZ123/experiment-01/actions/runs/41399309154) and same-closure control [41400104410](https://github.com/FlareZ123/experiment-01/actions/runs/41400104410), preserves **all four actual paired physical initial deals**. The candidate's accepted own turns show:

- At global turn23, a Fog Crystal owner deck search selected Duskull. Candidate actor root108 voluntarily benched that Duskull into **fifth Bench slot**. This filled the Bench with Unown, Dusclops, Trevenant & Dusknoir-GX, Lunatone and Duskull.
- From turn23 to turn47 the candidate kept all five spaces occupied, with **zero attached Bench Energy** and **two Basic Psychic Energy** on its Active Klefki. Mewtwo & Mew-GX, the main Perfection attacker, was **absent from play** during nineteen sequential 10-damage Klefki Joust turns.
- Only after opponent attacks knocked out Klefki, then the three-Prize Tag Team, did candidate core Pokémon enter play. A candidate Solrock was also voluntarily benched into the fifth slot at global turn49 after the first Knock Out; the first Mewtwo & Mew-GX Bench play was actor root222 at turn51, and the second at root233 on turn53.

This is an **observational** Bench-cost example, not a paired intervention on the candidate's choice to bench Duskull. Candidate's private Mewtwo access at turn23 is unknown; a stronger move on that exact turn and real-world win improvement remain unproven. The source spent 21 turns passing, so the attack outcomes are clearly **development-only**. The value of Duskull and Solrock for other New Moon ALS targets must be considered rather than automatically discarding their roles.

## Mechanistic distinction and proposed validation

The first episode changes the owner's action and demonstrates actual event timing and empty-board native loss under a particular conditional policy. The second identifies a physically executed action that removes a valuable *option to bench a new Basic*, with the main attacker absent. An adequate actor decision model should jointly consider **sole-Pokémon elimination risk** and **opportunity cost of occupied Bench slots**, using only the owner's actual hand/K1 known information and public state at the decision root.

A useful future **default-off, same-physical-deal** policy comparison would restrict the last Bench placement only when a core Basic is absent and an owner-visible stronger recipient is genuinely available or needed. It must audit the chosen Bench Basic, any source card accessed through Fog Crystal or similar search, a legal alternate, full native root+prompt settlement, all opponent continuation, every failed/capped arm and physical source deal parity. A prospective independent cohort would then be required for any frequency claim. Preserve the sole-Active Snorlax emergency backup exception, since an unconditional last-slot restriction has a known native counterexample.

Existing [reciprocal pass/attack review census](../../simulation/evaluation/agent5-reciprocal-stall-census.cjs) identifies severe opponent-policy stalls independently of Bench decisions; six synthetic regression tests passed [CI 41402455151](https://github.com/FlareZ123/experiment-01/actions/runs/41402455151). It does not make a Bench choice or validate game strength.

**Research outcome:** native source-specific evidence supports a **state- and matchup-dependent Bench-slot option value** as a necessary future owner-decision consideration. Direction and magnitude of the effect on competitive win rate remain unknown.
