## Why run

[The preceding pinned Store study](agent4-rank6-underworld-door-active-iron-thorns-native-ability-suppression-20261106.md) established that two original-v3 rank6 source contexts, in each original A/selectedB/A-repeat mode, reject the actor's attempt to use Shadow Rider Calyrex VMAX's Underworld Door while the opponent's Iron Thorns ex is Active with Initialization. The real pinned helper returned **nativePowerBlockedAfterRejection:true** for all six, without a probe exception.

The current owner-root policy's [Agent31 Underworld proposer](../../simulation/twinleaf/agent31-underworld-door-owner-frontier.cjs) checks the printed source, owned Psychic Energy, Bench recipients and turn marker, but deliberately leaves `nativeEffectAndLocksVerified:false`. The larger owner proposer then submits the root to native Store, which aborts the game on `UNKNOWN_POWER` instead of choosing a different root.

## Controlled intervention

The research-owned veto temporarily wraps the original pure `chooseUnderworldDoorRoot` export. It calls the unchanged chooser first. Only when its proposed action is Underworld Door **and** the detached actor-only public observation shows the opposing Active to be printed `Iron Thorns ex TWM` with printed `Initialization`, the proposed source `Shadow Rider Calyrex VMAX CRE` face-up with no Tool, and **no Stadium at either side**, it returns `null`. That makes the existing owner policy look for later alternatives in its ordinary order. It does not inject a hardcoded Pass action, change the printed sixties, reveal opponent hand/Prize/deck, make a native Store decision from hidden state, patch upstream, or override native paper rules. Unresolved Stadiums or Tool interactions cause abstention rather than confident suppression.

The hook is **default-off** and applies only to this exact narrow source circumstance. It is not a general Ability-availability oracle, and it does not imply optimal attack or resource decisions.

The preregistration fixes two original physical rank6 seeds `582147744` (`6:401:A`) and `2765516960` (`6:402:B`), both `champion-reference` and `new-moon-lockbreaker-v1` 60s, three original v3 policy modes A/selected B/A-repeat, and **both unmodified-control 12 native matches and guard-treatment 12 native matches** at the same repository revision and Twinleaf engine pin. The control run is performed first, followed by the separately instrumented owner guard; both have authentic live Store trials. Historical 3 November code-drift caveat remains, but **control vs guard here use the exact same source code revision**.

## What is demonstrated and what is still unknown

**Demonstrated:** On two authenticated rank6 source starting deals and two fixed candidate decks, a narrow owner-visible lock check applied before an Underworld Door proposal prevents all six reproducible named native errors and permits additional native accepted action processing. The source/initial-deal, A/B/A, seed, runner and game-engine controls support causal attribution to the opt-in guard within the native experiment.

**Unproven:** The first legal alternative's exact card/attack/energy payment; the quality of subsequent owner policy decisions; a qualifying full-game terminal under paper-clock and complete opponent response; robust generalization to other seeds/matchups or protective Tool/Stadium states; and the 99.5% Top32 benchmark. The field remains 29/32 authenticated opponent originals and **zero competitively qualified games**.
