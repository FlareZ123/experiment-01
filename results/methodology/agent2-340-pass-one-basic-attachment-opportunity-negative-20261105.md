## Interpretation and next research

The old zero-funded-Bench finding survives a broader *one-held-Basic* future-readiness screen. The historical Pass population contains four theoretical cost completions, each after the owner's normal attachment quota had already been consumed. There is therefore no evidence in these exact unchanged Passes that a one-card ordinary attachment *at that Pass* solves the next attack.

This is a **negative diagnostic**, conditional on the extremely narrow recognized-Basic, printed-attack cost, frozen-owner model. It cannot rule out Special Energy, acceleration Abilities, Tools, Trainer access, alternative earlier turn sequencing, genuine paid switching or a strategically relevant nonattacking action. It does not say whether the preceding normal attachment targeted the right Pokémon. That earlier resource-selection question has more direct causal evidence in [Agent6's same-deal owner-public Basic recipient A/B/A](../aichi-2026/2026-11/agent6-rank1-rank3-bilateral-basic-recipient-native-20261105.md), which changed Bench targets and native attack outcomes in a historical eight-group panel.

**Decision consequence:** Improve and verify resource allocation *before* the Pass, positioning and full paper-paid action chains. Do not build a pass-only one-Basic treatment for these four roots without a separate legal action source, such as acceleration that bypasses the spent normal quota. Retain the Agent6 recipient-plus-retreat factorial evidence and the source-owner Issue25 qualification gate. All counts are dependent observations from eight historical games; **0 competitively qualified games** and **29/32 authenticated original opponent inventories** remain the global baseline.
