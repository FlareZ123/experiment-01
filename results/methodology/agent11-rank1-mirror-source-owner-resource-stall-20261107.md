## Two falsifiable lines for shared policy evaluation

1. **Potential Dimension Valley enabling attack at source turn16.** Actual Pass root at global turn16 had owner-held `Dimension Valley PHF`, zero owner Stadium placements in this game, Active `Tapu Lele-GX GRI` with one attached Psychic Energy. The printed Energy Drive costs two Colorless; Dimension Valley reduces Psychic Pokémon attack costs by one Colorless. In ordinary unrestricted circumstances, playing the held Stadium could make Energy Drive payable from the existing Psychic. This audit does **not** record every global Stadium/Ability lock and has **not executed the card or a subsequent attack in a native Store**. Next test should use an independently copied *exact* real source pre-Pass state to check actual held Stadium PlayCardAction, effect, paid attack cost, settlement, damage and opponent reply, with unchanged original 110-root control and source return. Do not label it a proved profitable counterline until that test.
2. **Previously tested Lillie's Determination hand refresh at source turns20–24.** The source held `Lillie's Determination M1L` for the final three Passes with unused Supporter count and `supporterPlayLocked=false`; it still had all six Prizes. The supplied Advanced Player's Rulebook describes this Supporter as shuffling its hand into deck and drawing eight when exactly six Prizes remain (otherwise six). Its strategic value depends on what cards are protected and the accessible deck, so this is a **candidate owner-safe resource line**, not a demonstrated improvement. Agent15 has already tested a physical source-turn20 Lillie fork with an A/B/A design and observed two native Night Watch attacks before the 128-root cap; the printed random-target discrepancy and unknown opponent competence prevent paper-qualified conclusions. Future work must integrate a general owner-safe resource recovery into the **single symmetric common two-seat policy** and judge real reciprocal payoff rather than repeat a source-only Lillie fork.

These observations operationalize **state-dependent DCI, Bench contention, and realistic availability (AMR)** without assigning arbitrary values or relying on opponent concealed information. In particular, high hand size was not accompanied by hand Energy or a viable attacker; a purely hand-size-based Supporter selector can therefore miss decisive recovery windows.
