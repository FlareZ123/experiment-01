## Additional realistic decision: nonterminal three-or-more Prize pressure

The original worker could construct a terminal Rain from an actual paid Active Raichu or one self-KO, but **declined a three-Prize knockout if it did not end the game**. With only terminal chains, a midgame ability-to-attack route could not complete through its mandatory host prompts, even though it may be strategically favorable.

The new explicitly bounded *nonterminal* path accepts an **already funded Active Raichu** that can KO at least a three-Prize public target, and resolves Energy discard plus every 30-damage packet with no unnecessary Electrode sacrifice. The direct paid attack is prioritized ahead of any nonterminal Bomb. A separate narrowly permitted route starts from **Active Electrode + known Benched Raichu** with **no attached Energy or Tool on Electrode**, no Stadium/opponent Tools, fully visible eligible public targets, **opponent at least five Prizes remaining**, and own at least four. Only when one Bomb and a freshly reevaluated funded Rain take at least three opponent Prizes with an immediate net swing of at least one is the self-KO initiated. It resolves real CES transfer, mandatory Raichu promotion, actual paid Rain root, physical Energy discard, and damage-packet settlement. The card-source worker still does not claim this heuristic always dominates all long-game alternatives.

Constructed nonterminal example: an Active Electrode transfers four Reversal PAL plus one Counter CIN from discard onto a Benched Raichu; self-KO takes opponent from six to four Prizes, making the Special Energy multiplier live; after promotion, Raichu discards **three Reversal cards**, counted as **nine Lightning Energy**, to allocate **nine 30-damage packets to KO one 270-remaining-HP Shadow Rider VMAX**. A second opponent VMAX remains, so this is a three-Prize *advantage*, **not a win**. A separately tested already-funded Raichu uses the same actual paid three-Prize attack with zero Bombs and preserves the Electrodes. Tests veto the nonterminal self-KO when the opponent has only four Prizes remaining or when the departing Electrode has attached Energy/Tool resources; terminal wins keep their existing precedence.

## Terminal/Prize-value correction: opponent's face-up Legacy Energy

**New safety defect caught in the same executable worker:** `r17VisibleRainTargets` originally assigned only the printed Pokémon prize value. But a Pokémon carrying **Legacy Energy TWM 167** yields **one fewer Prize** if KO'd by opposing attack damage, if the ACE SPEC's once-per-game effect remains available. Electro Rain is attack damage in the correction hook. The previous worker did not read target `energies`, so it could initiate a very expensive double-Electrode self-KO expecting six Prizes when only five were available and the remaining Bench could not be Knocked Out.

**Change:** Each visible target with one attached exact `Legacy Energy TWM` (also recognizes the numbered `TWM 167` identity) now contributes `max(0, printedPrizes - 1)` to the dynamic target planner, uniformly affecting direct, one-Bomb, two-Bomb and comparator projections. The actor view does **not certify whether Legacy's once-per-game reduction was consumed earlier**, so this pessimistic discount may forgo a valid win in that case. This is preferable to falsely announcing or sacrificing to a guaranteed terminal win. It does not alter the actual native damage or Prize settlement.

**Two root counterexamples, two seats:** (a) An already-funded Raichu could KO an opposing Regidrago VSTAR SIT worth nominally two Prizes, but Regidrago carries face-up Legacy Energy, another opposing Unown survives, and only one Prize is conservatively guaranteed; the new **two-Prize nonterminal pressure** root correctly abstains. Removing Legacy from otherwise identical public state restores the funded Rain decision. (b) Active Raichu, two Benched Electrode and eight discard Energy could produce 20 Lightning units and KO two opposing 270-HP-remaining Shadow Rider VMAX in a three-Pokémon opposing board, but one VMAX carries Legacy. A third 90-HP Solrock needs three more damage packets; killing all three needs 21 packets, which exceeds the 20-unit maximum. Two VMAX KOs take 5 Prizes, not 6, so with own6 Prizes the dual self-KO is **not guaranteed to win** and is now refused. Removing Legacy restores the original six-Prize terminal two-Bomb choice. The 90-HP decoy is essential: a 60-HP decoy could be KO'd with the twentieth packet, which would turn the attack terminal despite Legacy.
