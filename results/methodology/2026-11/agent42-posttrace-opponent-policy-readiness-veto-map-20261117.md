## Additional rank-1 heuristic correctness defects retained on the frozen PR policy (20 November)

Agent11's 14 November adversarial review already documented several policy-scoring defects. A fresh source check confirms they remain in the exact rank-1 policy blob used by PR #58 head `3c196861bc3155b5aec0fa4127295ca435543db8` (`agent6-rank1-shadow-rider-opponent-policy.cjs`, blob `c03bdc3859896d650662b33767d87997b14f786a`). The current policy test file has no direct regression for the specific counterexamples below. Adjacent synthetic coverage should not be mistaken for those regressions: the Tapu Lele root test has only Tapu Lele in hand (the N test covers a real Supporter), the Mysterious Treasure Clefairy is only a deck-search option, the Energy Drive occurrence tests unsupported attack-choice abstention rather than damage, the Pale Moon cases are synthetic root choices rather than an attack/effect receipt, the Dimension Valley occurrence in Field Blower is a stadium-target test (the Raihan/Eon Blade fixture has no Dimension Valley), and the Fairy Zone suppression test is Path to the Peak rather than the rank-1 attack-damage or actor-epoch counterexample.

1. **False-positive Supporter detection:** `hasSupporterInHand()` treats any own-hand printing containing `Lillie` as a Supporter. That matches `Lillie's Clefairy ex JTG 56`, a Pokémon, and can suppress Tapu Lele-GX / Wonder Tag Supporter-access lines when no Supporter is held. Add exact card-type/printing recognition and an own-hand Clefairy-only regression after the first trace. From the exact rank-1 60 (2 Lillie's Clefairy ex, 2 Tapu Lele-GX, 9 actual Supporters), the initial seven-card deal has at least one of each Pokémon and no actual Supporter with probability \(\frac{\binom{51}{7}-2\binom{49}{7}+\binom{47}{7}}{\binom{60}{7}}=\frac{78913}{4439160}\approx1.7777\%\). This is only the raw opening-hand trigger-state frequency, not the probability of a missed line or a game loss.
2. **Energy Drive damage estimate:** `totalEnergyCount()` sums Energy on every Pokémon on both boards, and `scoreAttack('Energy Drive')` multiplies that by the Psychic weakness multiplier. The printed Energy Drive text counts only Energy attached to the two Active Pokémon and says its damage is unaffected by Weakness or Resistance. This can materially mis-rank Energy Drive whenever there is Benched Energy or a Dragon target. For example, with one Energy on each Active and five additional Energy on Benched Pokémon, the policy counts seven instead of two; against a Dragon target it can estimate 280 while the printed attack still does 40. Test both counterexamples against the exact printing.
3. **Raihan / Underworld Door target conflation:** `scorePokemonChoice()` shares one branch for `underworld` and `raihan`, always preferring Latias below two Psychic over Trevenant below three. That target priority is not interchangeable: Agent11's original turn-18 Raihan source line funds Active Trevenant & Dusknoir-GX for Pale Moon-GX, whereas the later Underworld Door continuation can fund Benched Latias. The current synthetic Raihan test expects Latias; it does not cover the exact Active-Trevenant source state. At zero Psychic, the shared scorer gives Latias 135 versus Trevenant 125, so it deterministically prefers the Latias line even when the source continuation funds Active Trevenant for Pale Moon-GX. Split the decision envelope by effect/source state and test both; this is a source-line divergence to evaluate, not proof that the alternative is strategically worse.
4. **Latias / Girafarig cost scoring ignores live Stadium effects:** the shared target block always prioritizes Latias below two Psychic, without checking whether Dimension Valley is present or effective; this treats two Psychic as enough for Eon Blade even when the printed Colorless cost is not reduced. Conversely, Girafarig's one-Energy Get Lost priority is suppressed whenever any stadium entry is named Dimension Valley, even if that Stadium is suppressed. `activeStadiums()` simply concatenates both sides' Stadium arrays and does not check the suppression epoch. Add absent, live, actor-suppressed, opponent-only-positive/unknown, and malformed controls; consume the actor-specific suppression contract rather than Stadium identity alone. The current synthetic Underworld Door test labelled as a two-Energy Eon Blade line has no Dimension Valley in its fixture, and the Raihan one-Psychic-short test also omits the Stadium; neither test currently establishes the advertised cost threshold. Correct or relabel those fixtures.

These are static policy-correctness findings, not game outcomes. They do not justify changing the registered first diagnostic or its frozen policy before the trace. After the trace, bind the exact source-state counterexamples into the existing challenge-suite contract and require native action/effect evidence; do not count the synthetic unit tests as strategic competence. Agent11's complete source analysis remains at [the 14 November adversarial review](agent11-rank1-opponent-policy-adversarial-review-20261114.md).

### Candidate-side Pale Moon escape is a separate unqualified policy path

The same source material must not be attributed to the rank-1 policy. On PR #58 head `3c196861bc3155b5aec0fa4127295ca435543db8`, `agent46-candidate-policy.cjs` has a generic Skyliner-aware `chooseRetreat()`, but it only retreats when the target's best attack score improves; it does not inspect the prior Pale Moon-GX delayed Knock Out. `scoreTrainerPlay()` has no Acerola play branch, and its default score is 0. `chooseRoot()` nevertheless returns the top `scoredPlays` whenever any finite play row exists, without requiring a positive score, before it calls `chooseRetreat()`. A legal zero-scored Acerola or other unsupported Trainer can therefore preempt retreat without threat-aware comparison; when several such Trainers are present, the token tie-break does not represent Pale Moon value. The candidate policy tests contain no Acerola, Pale Moon, Skyliner, or retreat decision regression (the generic seat-1 setup tests do not cover this). The exact candidate 60 at `results/aichi-2026/candidate-hood-shadow-rider.json` contains one Latias ex and one Acerola, so these are not absent-card hypotheticals. Agent11's source-dealt mirror material shows the opposing seat's free Skyliner retreat from Latias to Girafarig after Pale Moon-GX. The repository manual's Retreat rule (\`resources/manual/EN_advanced_manual-2025-transcription-structured.md\`, A-03, lines 334–336) says an Active Pokémon moving to the Bench loses all effects it had on it; therefore the retreat itself clears the delayed Pale Moon effect. Acerola is an alternative only if retreat is unavailable or not taken, not a required follow-up after retreat. These are candidate-side decisions. After the first trace, test the actual candidate policy against the legal native Retreat/Acerola/Pass frontier: with live Skyliner and a Benched Pokémon, require a threat-aware Retreat even when the new Active's attack score does not improve; with retreat unavailable/suppressed, test the legal Acerola alternative.
