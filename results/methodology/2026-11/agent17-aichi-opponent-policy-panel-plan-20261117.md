### Rank-14 source-specific policy profile (specification only; not an implementation)

Rank 14 is an exact 60-card Regidrago list, not a generic rank-10/12/13 clone. Its source binding is rank 14 in [the authenticated full-decklist file](../../aichi-2026/full-decklists.json), source JSON blob `ae773f6201d7aa69d31914dbfee66f3fba692200`. Its distinctive package is 4 Regidrago V / 3 Regidrago VSTAR, 4 Double Dragon Energy / 4 Grass / 2 Fire, 4 Quick Ball, 4 Mysterious Treasure, 3 VS Seeker, one Ultra Ball, one Battle Compressor, plus one each of Mega Dragonite ex, Dragapult ex, Noivern ex, Hisuian Goodra VSTAR, Kyurem, Dialga-GX, Latias ex, Sudowoodo, Budew and Marshadow. The first four named Dragon evolutions have no corresponding pre-evolution in this 60 (no Dragonite, Dreepy/Drakloak, Noibat or Hisuian Goodra V); they are Apex Dragon discard payloads, not ordinary board-development targets. Rank-specific search/discard logic must distinguish them from playable Basic Dragon Pokémon and the one-of support Pokémon.

The Apex-eligible Dragon payload menu is a state-dependent portfolio, not a fixed damage ranking: Jet Headbutt (70), Phantom Dive (200 plus six distributed damage counters), Ryuno Glide (330 but discards two Energy), Covert Flight (70 plus protection from Basic-Pokémon attacks), Dominating Echo (140 plus a Special-Energy/Stadium-from-hand restriction), Rolling Iron (200 plus 80 less incoming attack damage), Trifrost (110 to three Pokémon but discards all Energy), Timeless-GX (150 plus an extra turn), and the Regidrago V attacks all have different prize, lock, energy and follow-up consequences. Latias ex is Psychic-type, so its Eon Blade (200 with a next-turn attack restriction) is a direct attack only and cannot be selected by Apex Dragon. Both current candidates contain one Sky Field, one Dimension Valley and only Basic Psychic Energy: Dominating Echo's meaningful lock here is therefore Stadium-from-hand denial (its Special-Energy clause has no current target), especially after Marshadow or Megaton Blower clears the Stadium already in play. Covert Flight can prevent damage from Basic attackers—including Mimikyu's Copycat attack—during the next turn, but it does not cancel Timeless-GX's extra-turn effect; score it as damage mitigation, not as a Copycat shutdown. In both current candidates, all four Shadow Rider Calyrex VMAX (CRE 75) are 320 HP and neither list contains Big Charm; rank14's one Mega Dragonite ex offers Ryuno Glide for 330 (350 with one of the source's two Muscle Bands), a clean three-prize active knockout before other modifiers. The attack discards two Energy from the Regidrago using Apex Dragon, so this is a high-value but resource-expensive missing choice; compare the KO/prize swing against reload cost and the live Copycat counter-ALS before defaulting to it. The list's four Quick Ball / four Mysterious Treasure / one Ultra Ball / one Battle Compressor package makes the human [DCI/AMR concepts](../../../resources/human_concepts.md) directly relevant: Dragon payloads are conditionally discardable, while Dialga, Latias, Sudowoodo, Budew and the singleton ACE SPEC/supporters can have distinct on-board or future-turn value. Do not assign a constant discard score to a card independent of the opponent and intended attack line.

**Highest-priority held-out counter-ALS (rules-supported):** both exact candidate profiles—[Hood Shield](../../aichi-2026/candidate-hood-shadow-rider.json) and [Lockbridge](../../aichi-2026/2026-11/agent31-lockbridge-shadow-rider-v1.json)—retain one Mimikyu GRI 58 and one Dialga-GX UPR 100. Mimikyu's Copycat copies a non-GX attack used by the opponent last turn; Apex Dragon is itself a non-GX attack, and its effect chooses an attack from a Dragon Pokémon in the user's discard. Rulebook C-18's "use it as this attack" treatment means the named attack remains Apex Dragon while the chosen payload supplies its effects/damage, so choosing Timeless-GX as the payload does not make the source's previous-turn attack GX for Copycat's eligibility check. If the candidate's Dialga-GX is in its discard, Mimikyu is attack-ready, and the candidate has not yet used its GX attack, the candidate can copy Apex Dragon and select its own Timeless-GX. This is not just speculative: the TPCi Rules Team ruling reproduced in [PokéBeach's Sun & Moon rulings](https://www.pokebeach.com/2017/01/sun-moon-card-rulings) says copying a GX attack through an attack effect counts as the player's one GX attack, and it cannot be chosen if that player has already used a GX attack; the [official rulebook](https://assets.pokemon.com/assets/cms2/pdf/trading-card-game/rulebook/swsh3_rulebook_en.pdf) states the once-per-game GX limit. The ruling is for Clefairy's Metronome, so applying it to this nested Copycat → Apex Dragon → Timeless-GX sequence is a strong rules-supported inference, not a card-specific ruling. [Mimikyu's Copycat text](https://limitlesstcg.com/cards/en/GRI/58), [Regidrago VSTAR's Apex Dragon text](https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokemon-news/another-peek-at-new-cards-from-pokemon-tcg-sword-shield-silver-tempest), and [Dialga-GX's Timeless-GX text](https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokemon-tcg/pokemon-cards/series/sm5/164) ground the chain. This is **already established repository evidence, not a new discovery**: Agent46's [corrected two-seat native evidence](../../../results/methodology/agent46-copycat-real-apex-history-paper-hook-two-seat-20261107.md) executes the genuine preceding Apex Dragon, then Mimikyu Copycat → the candidate's discarded Dialga-GX Timeless-GX through two opt-in, reversible hooks; the fresh-GX case resolves 150 damage, `usedGX:true` and the extra turn. ## Strategic decision model for competent policies

A rank policy should model *realistic lines*, not merely card connectivity or whether an action is syntactically legal. The supplied [human-developed concepts](../../../resources/human_concepts.md) are useful hypotheses for this layer, but remain non-authoritative and should be checked against the local rules manual, pinned source, and actual native traces.

- **State-dependent discardability (DCI):** estimate whether each held card should be preserved, discarded, or actively sent to the discard pile under the current matchup, turn, Prize knowledge, and intended line. A card's value changes after a one-use ability is spent, an attack payload becomes accessible, or a key resource becomes Lost-Zone vulnerable. Do not hard-code a card as always disposable or always protected.
- **Active Move Realism (AMR):** evaluate whether a theoretical line can actually be paid for now. Include Supporter-per-turn contention, discard costs, searcher consumption, Bench occupancy, retreat/attachment requirements, lock timing, and whether a card is in a playable zone. A high-connectivity path is not an available line if its discard or Supporter costs cannot be paid.
- **State-dependent UDP:** treat important singleton/ACE SPEC/support Pokémon as protected only while the game plan still needs them; once their function is exhausted, their discard value may change.
- **K0/K1 Prize knowledge:** distinguish decisions made before the first deck search from decisions after the player has searched and can infer the exact Prize composition. The policy must not use hidden Prize information before it has legitimately learned it.
- **Archetype-Line-Specifics (ALS):** encode the source deck's actual multi-card sequence, not just its unordered card counts. For example, a Tag Call → Guzma & Hala → Special Energy/Tool/TM: Evolution line is a different policy problem from a generic "play Supporters and search" heuristic.
- **Discrete tactical value:** preserve the value of conditional but decisive effects such as gust, disruption, lock, and prize denial. Do not let a setup-probability, draw-volume, or graph-connectivity proxy delete a card whose value is concentrated in winning states.

These concepts suggest a concrete policy review question: for each important decision, can the policy explain the intended line, its realistic costs, the cards it is willing to spend, and the information it is allowed to use? A high legal-action rate without those properties is not independent opponent competence.
