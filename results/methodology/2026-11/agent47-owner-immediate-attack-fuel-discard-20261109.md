# Agent 47: owner-public immediate-attack fuel preservation for mandatory discard

**9 November 2026. Developmental opt-in policy, no qualified paper games.**

## Decision-changing failure being addressed

[Agent37's actual archived original rank5 Mew/Genesect native witness](agent37-mew-ultraball-protected-payment-retrospective-20261109.md) exposed a concrete opportunity-cost error. At native Ultra Ball prompt 54, generic discard selected **Battle VIP Pass FST + Double Colorless Energy SUM**. With the same complete physical deal and prompt, source-only payment instead chose **Battle VIP Pass FST + Hisuian Heavy Ball ASR**, leaving DCE. The latter physically funded a later Mew VMAX Cross Fusion Strike for 210 Active damage; winner did not change. Its retrospective selection and limited paper status remain unchanged.

The generic helper in `simulation/twinleaf/agent47-actor-hand-discard.cjs` previously optimized only the number of distinct hand printings erased. A two-card choice can therefore destroy the only immediately attack-funding Energy even when another Store-validated payment preserves it. Agent37 has a separate source-specific protected list; this project implements a narrower **reusable board-dependent decision**, without registering a deck-specific protected-card roster.

## Implemented policy and contract

New pure module: [`agent47-attack-fuel-discard-frontier.cjs`](../../../simulation/twinleaf/2026-11/agent47-attack-fuel-discard-frontier.cjs).

New isolated test suite: [`attack-fuel-discard.test.cjs`](../../../tests/2026-11/agent47/attack-fuel-discard.test.cjs).

The actor-owned mandatory discard observer already supplies the exhaustive exact-printing multisets accepted by the native `ChooseCardsPrompt.validate`; the new strategy only **chooses from that menu**. It consults only detached owner hand, face-up owner Active attacks and attached Energy, public turn resource counts, and public Stadiums. For printed Basic Energy and Double Colorless Energy SUM, it computes whether *one remaining manual attachment* could satisfy a currently unfunded printed Active attack. Temple of Sinnoh is modeled as reducing DCE to a single Colorless unit.

The chooser changes its generic payment **only when all conditions hold**:

1. The current Active has an unfunded, permission-compatible printed attack which becomes payable after exactly one known Energy card attachment.
2. The generic discard would consume **all** copies of every such attack-funding Energy remaining in hand.
3. A different multiset in the exact native validated prompt menu retains at least one such Energy.

Among native-valid preserving alternatives, it reuses the existing generic preservation score. Two identical Energy cards are evaluated by remaining multiplicity; spending one copy may leave a viable funded attack. If no alternative exists, the original payment stands. Unknown attached Special Energy, already satisfied printed attack costs, blocked or spent manual attachments, asleep/paralyzed Active, lock turns, and first-turn attack restrictions conservatively abstain. This is a local line-of-play heuristic, not an assertion that nominal attack payment overrules all other strategic costs.

## Reversible integration

`withActorHandDiscard(execute,deps,options)` accepts **`attackFuelDiscard:true`**; the default remains false. It lazily obtains the existing printed public card catalog, or accepts an injected catalog for tests. If the separate, explicitly opted-in directly ready Apex donor selector has already changed the payment, that selector retains priority; the fuel proposer is otherwise evaluated. The adapter still resolves selected printings back to **distinct real owner hand card objects**, validates `ResolvePromptAction`, dispatches through native Store, and records rejected/accepted settlement. New audit fields identify eligible changed alternatives, selected alternatives, reasons, and the opt-in state. No upstream Twinleaf, fixed 60, native rules, other development policy or broad CI trigger was modified.

## Executed checks and limits

Local Node 22 pure contract tests: **8/8 passed**. Cases include a sole DCE protecting a two-Colorless Active attack from generic payment, second-copy survival, mandatory all-Energy payments, Temple of Sinnoh, prior attachment/lock/status exclusions, already funded attacks, color-specific costs, unknown Special Energy and independence from opponent concealed cards. A ninth **mock native-prompt integration contract** is committed alongside them for CI use; it was not executed in the local isolated test setup, so its pass status is pending. Existing baseline `agent47-actor-hand-discard` behavior is unchanged with the new option off.

This is **not** a replay of the original Mew physical deal. The source's DCE may have been saved in the original archived state by Agent37's deck-specific safeguard; the generic owner-Active frontier is not asserted to fire in that exact situation. Crucially, the printed one-attachment calculation is not an independent paper legality/attack-success oracle, and no native competitive or field win is inferred.

**Next decision test:** register fresh, physically matched source games and compare opt-in off/on after identifying actual eligible live payment frontiers. Capture original-source exact sixty, Store prompt/card payment, affected later Energy attachment, settled attack, opposing reply, real physical deal matching, both seats, cap/error outcomes, and all unobserved cases. If no live candidate reaches this condition, report zero activation rather than crediting the hypothetical rescue.

**Global benchmark remains:** 29/32 original 60-card source inventories; ranks 23/25/28 missing, no competitively qualified games, no selected best fixed sixty and no demonstrated 99.5% field game win rate.

## Extension: publicly available Perfection attack costs (v2)

After [Agent36's Nov9 independently executed Perfection native comeback](agent36-gengar-v3-50703-physical-repair-20261109.md), a printed-Active-attacks-only safeguard was incomplete: a Mewtwo & Mew-GX can pay a copied Pokémon-GX attack using **Perfection**, initiated natively as `UseAbilityAction` rather than `AttackAction`. The v2 module now imports the **existing verified two-printing donor catalog** from `agent30-known-perfection-options.cjs`, already exposed as the actor's public `catalog.perfectionDonors`. It also reuses that module's public Perfection veto (printed Active identity/Ability, Path, Initialization, Garbotoxin and other supported locks). Only donors actually visible on the owner's Bench or in public discard are eligible; an unknown deck/Prize donor is excluded. A spent GX attack is excluded while a non-GX Poltergeist remains a candidate.

For example, if a Mewtwo & Mew-GX has **one Basic Psychic attached**, a public Gengar & Mimikyu-GX donor offers two-Psychic **Poltergeist**, and exactly one Basic Psychic remains in hand, the validated alternative discard set retaining that Energy now wins over a generic payment that would destroy the route. This is a *public printed-cost sufficiency* claim; native Perfection, exact copied attack prompt, damage, and actual KO still require separate acceptance.

Added three committed Perfection-targeted tests: known donor with one-color missing, absent donor/Ability, and visible lock plus spent-GX handling. Local isolated Node 22 reproductions passed all **11 pure behavior cases** (8 initial + 3 new); the new tests' mock used a minimal faithful veto implementation, and **full repository CI/native Store execution remains pending**, so the local pass does not certify the actual integrated native path. Code commits: [v2 donor integration](https://github.com/FlareZ123/experiment-01/commit/442c266263f6005f0754838736beeea1a3ad1549), [three regressions](https://github.com/FlareZ123/experiment-01/commit/ffc89a61cc26ee4ce896611324637137d72f53da).

This v2 change is motivated by an observed and reproducible **Perfection resource tradeoff**, but does **not** claim an observed live discard prompt where the new selection activated. Do not reinterpret Agent36's attached-Energy target choice as a discard-prompt witness; those are distinct decision points. The first required native check remains a real, consented Store prompt with an actual alternative valid two-card payment, followed by a copied-attack and counterplay ledger.

## Special-Energy coverage and locked attachments (v3)

The pure unit selector now reuses `specialEnergyUnits` from the established `agent3-special-energy-frontier.cjs` for a fixed **verified known set**: **Telepath [P] Energy M3**, Speed Lightning, Capture and Spiky (and continues the existing Basic/DCE route). This matters for the actual New Moon candidate, which has three Telepathic Psychic Energy, and can pay Perfection's printed Psychic costs using a held or already attached Telepath. The native public catalog must explicitly identify the printing as Special; unknown Specials still abstain, with no invented type. Temple of Sinnoh changes Telepath to one Colorless, so a one-attachment two-Psychic proposal is suppressed if it no longer meets the printed cost.

A Special-Energy-only attachment lock now filters the legal fuel candidates individually, leaving **Basic Energy** available when Basic attachments remain permitted. Prior blanket rejection of all Basic routes under this narrow lock was unnecessarily conservative. Three additional contract cases cover Telepath funding, unknown Special rejection and special-only versus Basic attachment lock, committed at [v3 known-energy integration](https://github.com/FlareZ123/experiment-01/commit/7890af5ad2e6ad77d9e8f4a2c3ee83cc642a5740), [Tel/Temple tests](https://github.com/FlareZ123/experiment-01/commit/6be8b56c826a2ec0e361d970557143fa124de18d), [Basic-fuel lock correction](https://github.com/FlareZ123/experiment-01/commit/7f240754787182c7c9fa88095e855aa31a290306), and [regression](https://github.com/FlareZ123/experiment-01/commit/b9c81ae3f940f41aa73bdd8adbeb7ddd936bb11b). All new tests are included in the already modified manual-only contract workflow. They are **committed, pending actual GitHub CI execution**. No native activation or competitive-game claim results from this improvement.

## Canonical bilateral runner transport (developmental, default OFF)

The initial narrow `withActorHandDiscard` option was inaccessible from ordinary `runDevelopment` full-game trials because the seven-component `withCanonicalActorOnlyPolicy` wrapper did not forward it. That integration gap is closed in `simulation/evaluation/agent7-canonical-actor-composition.cjs` and `simulation/evaluation/agent41-bilateral-v2-development.cjs`: use **`runDevelopment({actorOnlyComposition:true,actorHandDiscard:true,actorAttackFuelDiscard:true})`**, or the existing environment-driven runner with **`AGENT47_ACTOR_HAND_DISCARD_DEV=true`** and **`AGENT47_ATTACK_FUEL_PAYMENT_DEV=true`** alongside bilateral actor composition. All switches are default off. The new option is deliberately disallowed in the same arm as the source-specific `actorMewUltraBallPaymentGuard`, preserving a controlled comparison rather than silently overwriting the established source-payment intervention.

Canonical per-trial actor audit now carries `actorAttackFuelDiscardOptIn` and `nativeDecisionCounts.attackFuelDiscardAlternatives` / `attackFuelDiscardSelected`, plus the versioned selector identity. Candidate and original source share the identical wrapper when enabled; no rank-conditioned or hidden-zone decision is introduced. The ordinary return rows and `attackDeclarations` fields are unchanged; copied Perfection attacks still require the **separate Agent36/Critic postgame telemetry repair** and must never be estimated solely from `AttackAction` counts.

The existing canonical integration regression gained two new tests for forwarded options, explicitly counted selected payment, and rejecting an opt-in without the actual hand-discard resolver or alongside source-specific Mew payment. These tests and the detailed pure module tests are now included in the existing **manual-only** contract workflow.
