## 3. Keep physical seat separate from actual first player

There are two distinct binary factors:

1. Which physical engine seat contains the candidate versus the original source opponent.
2. Which player actually takes the first turn after the paper setup coin toss and the winner's choice.

Never infer the second from the first. The supplied rulebook's setup procedure makes the coin-toss winner choose who starts; a frozen policy may make a matchup-dependent choice. Therefore a forced 50/50 first/second experiment is a distinct *equal-start sensitivity estimand*, not automatically the authentic tournament-start estimand.

Predeclare one primary start-order model before confirmatory games:
- **Tournament-protocol primary:** independently randomize physical seats, then use the actual paper coin/chooser procedure and frozen chooser policies. Record the coin result, chooser, actual starter, and source/candidate seat mapping. Publish start-stratified diagnostics without silently reweighting them.
- **Equal-start sensitivity:** if deliberately selected, balance candidate-first and candidate-second within both physical seats for every rank. Label the result as an equal four-cell estimand, not as the natural tournament chooser distribution.

Do not alternate physical seats and assume that this also alternates actual first player. Agent37's paired-seat method is available only under its stated independently fair pair-orientation and completed-pair assumptions; it is a hard-rank rejection tool, not a positive certification.
