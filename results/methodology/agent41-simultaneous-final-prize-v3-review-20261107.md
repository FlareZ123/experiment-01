## Physical-seat mapping

The fixture assigns `owner` and `enemy` identities independently of physical seat:

```js
state.players = actorSeat === 0 ? [owner, enemy] : [enemy, owner];
state.activePlayer = actorSeat;
```

The native regression runs both `actorSeat=0` and `actorSeat=1`. The adapter resolves a winner by comparing the winning Player object with `state.players[0]` and `state.players[1]`, then emits `GameWinner.PLAYER_1` or `PLAYER_2`. The tests check the expected owner/opponent winner seat, corrected final state, and corrected log name for owner-only and opponent-only promotion cases. This is the correct mapping for Twinleaf's physical player slots.

The pinned `choosePrizeCards` implementation also confirms the native early-return order: it iterates `state.players` in index order and, when a group exhausts that player's remaining Prizes, calls `endGame` with the corresponding `PLAYER_1` or `PLAYER_2` and returns. This is the defect the adapter is correcting.

- `check-effect.ts:418-443` constructs a real `new KnockOutEffect(owner, pokemonToDiscard.cardList)`, dispatches it through `store.reduceEffect`, then adds its resulting `knockOutEffect.prizeCount` to the actual Prize group.

## Rulebook and scope

The uploaded Advanced Player's Rulebook v3.4 §D says both players take the Prize cards generated by the simultaneous Knock Outs. Section §E's terminal table distinguishes both-final-Prize outcomes by promotion availability: matching availability gives a tie; if only one player can promote, that player wins.

The adapter is deliberately narrower than the whole table. It only arms for exactly two players, exactly two Active KnockOutEffects (one per player), two Prize groups both sufficient to exhaust their respective remaining Prizes, both destinations being the players' hands, no other KO effects, no sudden death, and no prevented Prize effect. Promotion availability is currently the presence of a Pokémon in the player's Bench. Effects that could prevent promotion are outside this fixture.

The current `mixedTerminalRows` cases where only one Prize group exhausts its player's Prizes are diagnostic controls. The adapter declines to arm. The Rulebook §E table includes the relevant one-final-Prize/no-promotion row, and the test's expected tie for that case is consistent with the table. Do not describe those rows as supported by the resolver.
