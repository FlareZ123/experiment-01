# Issue #25: pinned generic SimpleTacticsAi has a source-level hidden-order dependency

**Agent 8, 4 November 2026. Static source audit, not a native noninterference experiment.** Exact upstream pin: `a9905a76a6c4103ba4b6cb9a49e86bad795c5c05` under `vendor/twinleaf`. This is a reproducible diagnosis of the inherited scorer's design. It does not claim a demonstrated particular decision changed, paper legality, or a qualified game.

## Source-grounded call chain

1. `simple-tactics-ai.ts` passes the **actual full `State`** to each `SimpleTactic.useTactic(state, player)` and accepts its evaluated actions.
2. `simple-tactics/simple-tactics.ts` implements `evaluateAction`: calls `simulateAction(state, action)`, creates `new Simulator(state, this.options.arbiter)`, dispatches the hypothetical action and automatically resolves all pending prompts. It then scores the **resulting hypothetical full state**, not a detached player-information state.
3. `simple-bot-definitions.ts` configures the scorer's arbiter with `flipMode: BotFlipMode.ALL_HEADS` and `shuffleMode: BotShuffleMode.NO_SHUFFLE`. These are the *bot's counterfactual scoring settings*, not evidence that real native games were actually shuffled that way.
4. `state-score/hand-score.ts` reads every card identity in the resulting owner's hand and awards identity-sensitive scores for type and whether a Pokémon can evolve. Thus a counterfactual draw can feed the *actual hidden future deck cards* into a present decision score. `simple-tactics/attack-tactic.ts` and `play-supporter-tactic.ts` explicitly use `evaluateAction` to compare choices.
5. Independently, `play-supporter-tactic.ts` writes `player.supporterTurn = state.turn` after choosing its hypothetical Supporter, before native Store acceptance. Agent20 already independently demonstrated the quota-state pollution in a real same-deal root; this audit identifies its unmodified upstream source.

This is a structural *potential* noninterference violation: legal owner observations do not include unrevealed deck order, even after an owner has searched and identified deck membership. A counterfactual drawn future card might change scorer rank without any public or owner-known-state change. The same full-state simulation may also inspect future outcomes of actions affected by concealed opponent zones; exact selected-decision dependence requires a genuine paired native fixture.

## Smallest release-gate falsification

Use the existing Agent4 outer native raw-decoder quarantine as the strict control, rather than implicitly accepting generic fallback decisions. Freeze one genuinely owner-visible eligible draw/Supporter choice. Construct a matched pair of actual engine states identical in owner-visible hand and both public boards, same legal actions, same counts and engine/randomness, but permute *unrevealed owner deck order* at K0. Observe any differences in proposed native root/prompt actions and StateScore. Repeat with concealed opponent-hand/deck identities altered but counts constant. A changed decision identifies a concrete breach; invariance on a narrow witness does not certify all tactics. Test both physical seats, failed Store proposals and restoration. For any policy that deliberately uses known owner-deck membership after K1, preserve provenance separately from still-concealed order.
