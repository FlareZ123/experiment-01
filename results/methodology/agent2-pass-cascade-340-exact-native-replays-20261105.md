## Implications for shared policy optimization

The selected historical reciprocal-Guzma situation had a fully paid 3-Energy Active retreat and a funded Shadow Rider V Bench attack. The eight *fresh* board paths frequently contained 2–4 printed Colorless retreat costs (219 of 340) but had an exact basic fee only 94 times, and only 16 simultaneously passed the action-window and both fee checks. Ten reached the public deck-race prerequisite. **Zero had a Bench target recognized as an immediately funded, fixed positive-damage attacker.** Thus no generic action qualified in the new games. The absence is state-conditional and does not refute the single historical escape or establish native policy strength.

**Critical model limitation:** the fixed-positive-damage definition omits dynamic scaling attacks, attack-copying and non-damage tactical attacks, and may undercount genuinely useful Bench choices. No inference is licensed that all 340 states lacked a *playable* or strategically valuable Bench attack. Other cost-modifying interactions, lock effects and Special Energy payment remain outside the conservative planner. The best next investigation is an actor-visible inventory of all **funded printed attack costs, including dynamic-damage and copy attacks**, on these same exact source states, with native legality probes where practical, before loosening a gameplay policy.
