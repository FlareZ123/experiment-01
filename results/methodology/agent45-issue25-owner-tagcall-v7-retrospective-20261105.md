## Preregistered question and fixed control

The original [v7 registration](../aichi-2026/registrations/agent45-issue25-tagcall-v7-frozen-eight-20261105.json) was committed before the new native work executed. The hypothesis was that a shared first-owner-turn, own-hand `Tag Call CEC` → authentic own-deck `Guzma & Hala CEC` source action could remedy some observed original-source Pass decisions. All original eight `agent1-issue25-mirrored-r6-r11-20261104.json` trial IDs, real printed candidate and original-source 60s, both source seats and the 48-root native cap remained unchanged. Source ranks 6 and 11 are the only source ranks in this retrospective, with `champion-reference` and `new-moon-lockbreaker-v1` as fixed incumbent contenders. The original v5 Prize/Promotion, selected three printed attack frontiers, bilateral Special Energy policy, and Capture owner-deck prompt policy remained inside the same experimental composition.

The single additive treatment invokes the already tested [Agent6 native owner Tag Call handler](../../simulation/twinleaf/agent6-owner-tagcall-hala-native.cjs) at each real `headless.runMatch` boundary. It receives the genuine source-registered physical `deckA` and `deckB` printings as each owner's *own known list*, then makes K0 decision offers using the detached acting owner's current hand, board and public game resources. It accesses the true shuffled owner-deck card objects only at a naturally Store-accepted Tag Call search prompt. The same policy runs on either physical seat. Source-owner card priorities remain a developmental assumption; no dominance or optimal Supporter sequencing is proven.

## Independent accepted-root action reconciliation

After the initial CI result, a read-only, *post hoc* public-action comparator was added with SHA-256-checked complete root ledgers. [Second successful native CI 42076210948](https://github.com/FlareZ123/experiment-01/actions/runs/42076210948) re-executed the same original eight initial physical deals and passed all pure/native comparator tests. The callback Store audit's two accepted Tag Call Items agreed **exactly with two public accepted `PlayCardAction` roots**, establishing separate evidence that the policy did something physically observable. Actual source-owned **Guzma & Hala Supporter plays were 0** across all eight treatment arms, even though two copies had been physically gained in separate games. Neither authentic G&H hand gain is evidence of an executed Supporter or fully paid multi-category search.

- `6:101:B:champion-reference`: first exact public action difference at ordinal **17**, **global turn 2**, source physical seat0, original action `PassTurnAction`, treatment action `PlayCardAction` for **Tag Call**. Source public native Pass count changed from five to four; source native card plays from zero to two; zero source attacks.
- `6:101:B:new-moon-lockbreaker-v1`: first exact public action difference at ordinal **24**, **global turn 2**, source physical seat0, original action `PassTurnAction`, treatment action `PlayCardAction` for **Tag Call**. Source public native Pass count changed from four to three; source native card plays from zero to one; zero source attacks.
- The other six registered arms had **identical accepted public action sequences** (through the original 48-root cap or the retained root14 abort).

Global turn2 is the acting second player's opening turn in the native game, so a Supporter could potentially be used if the normal turn restrictions allow it. The observed bot made no G&H Supporter play after the authentic Tag Call fetch, a concrete downstream policy gap. Future work must distinguish whether G&H was actually eligible and payable in that board/hand state; the public action ledger alone cannot establish the absence of a Supporter lock, available discard cards, or a strategically sound follow-up.
