## What the census can support

The fixed original-source decision policy accepts Passes repeatedly while holding the very same structurally attachable Tools. This is **evidence of a persistent action-enumeration or action-selection shortfall in the specific developmental bot policy**, subject to separate strategic opportunity-cost analysis. The complete Pokémon Tool catalog includes older or outside-format cards; matching a name to a catalog Tool is not itself a legality audit. No claim that every Tool attachment is correct is warranted. In rank6, **Tool Jammer is also a physical discard payment** for Agent45's separately verified Tag Call/Guzma & Hala two-card mode, so an early Tool attachment changes access to a potentially more important setup line. In rank11, early Float Stone on a Trubbish may offer retreat value, but neither retreat execution nor downstream competitive advantage was measured in this census.

The study is constrained to the original eight-arm v5 developer frame, **ranks6 and11 only**; it does not sample the full field, and selections are retrospective. The 33 Pass states are from a post-choice/pre-Store observer: **pre-choice noninterference remains unverified for those 33 as a cohort**, even though two anchor roots were independently pre-choice-reproduced with exact full native action tape and whole physical-deal parity by Agent20. Counts refer to structurally plausible owner actions, never completed paper games.
