## Correct diagnosis and limits

These outcomes establish **native bot-decoder prompt failures** in the actual configured decision policy. They do not establish a Pokémon game-engine semantic crash or illegal Tapu Lele-GX card text, and do not establish that its public play was strategically wrong. It is a hypothesis worth testing that the protected Basic-opening choice changed whether a Tapu Lele-GX Bench entry was possible and thus exposed a formerly unhandled mandatory/optional ability confirmation. To prove this, a next native negative/positive fixture should bind the same owner-visible `ConfirmPrompt` to its exact triggering effect, verify the complete paper option menu and choice/payoff, and independently record physical payment, owner-hand change, subsequent legal Supporter quota and opponent continuation. The `ChoosePrizePrompt` case requires a separate legal hidden-Prize selection policy, with no opponent Prize identity read.

**Most valuable integration target:** The selected symmetric both-seat policy should handle real owner-visible `ConfirmPrompt` and `ChoosePrizePrompt` rather than changing deck evaluation to exclude error-producing turns. A bot decoder cannot legitimately treat those rooted prompts as complete games. Register all error outcomes; use fixed test states plus natural real Store cases to distinguish *missing decoder capability* from owner-legal strategic choice. The initial outcome-class label `engine-error` should retain its specific native `bot-decoder` failure attribution in competitive-admission reports.

The full benchmark remains 29/32 original name/count inventories, **zero qualified paper matches**, no selected fixed 60, and no established 99.5% field win rate.
