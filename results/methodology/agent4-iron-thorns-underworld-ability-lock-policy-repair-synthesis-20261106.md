## Strategic conclusion

The owner decision planner must model **Power availability under current public suppression effects** before treating printed Pokémon Abilities as usable search/acceleration channels. The narrow guard demonstrates one conservative implementation pathway: preserve the existing candidate root, inspect only detached owner-view face-up opponents and active public sources, reject just a provably unavailable Power, and let the original selector evaluate other choices. It avoids hidden-information access and preserves genuine native matching starting deals.

The repair is not sufficient for strong gameplay. Its first replacements use Energy on **Tapu Lele-GX** and **Girafarig**, neither shown to satisfy the urgent attacker setup axis. An optimizer or deck evaluator that scores native action count, graph reachability, or avoids crashes without comparing the **opportunity cost of the next legal action** can still choose poor tactical plays. Further development should rank legal alternates, check the actual physical target, perform independent paper resource auditing, and observe consequential opponent damage/Prize exchanges.
