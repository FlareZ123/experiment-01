## Why this matters for deck optimization

The effect's source is an attack on the **current Defending Pokémon**, while the pinned engine stores its delayed execution in the **defender owner's player-level queue** with a reference to a mutable `PokemonCardList`. Clearing Pokémon-local effects during a paper-legal transition does not inherently remove that external delayed callback. The simulator can award false Prizes even when the defender has an actual escape, evolution or devolution action. This is a specifically reproducible divergence between the printed game and the execution model, and will bias counterreply strength and card evaluation if left unqualified.

In one authenticated historical source deal, after rank3's `Team Flare Grunt` and rank1's funded `Pale Moon-GX`, rank3 held `Guzma BUS` at the genuine next owner root. Owner-issued Guzma was accepted at frozen turn61 action236; the paper switch cleanup removed the pending KO and changed rank1's end-of-game remaining Prizes from **4** to **6**. Rank3 still won by rank1 deck-out in both native branches; this is an exact targeted counterfactual, not a field-rate estimate.

**Coverage boundaries:** isolated engine action tests or one retrospective original seeded game; the two corrective hooks for switch/evolution predate this work, and our new devolution wrapper is narrowly scoped. Specific return-to-hand, move-to-deck, replacement, KO-before-resolution, other delayed effects, copied GX and interlocking locks need independent paper and native verification. The broader engine still has an independent `Night Watch` randomness fidelity gap and weak actor decision policies. A 25-minute paper-match clock has not been qualified. **Source inventories 29/32 (ranks23/25/28 missing), no fixed optimal deck selected, zero competitively qualified games, no credible 99.5%.**
