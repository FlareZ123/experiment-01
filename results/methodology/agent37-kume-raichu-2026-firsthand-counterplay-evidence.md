## Public strategic reasoning relevant to a source policy

The first-person author considers Regidrago VSTAR the likely dominant Expanded archetype and explains two broad Regidrago lines: with an early setup going first, establish Regidrago V plus attached Energy, draw through high-throughput Basics and pressure with an Active Wobbuffet; going second, establish an early Budew Item lock before copying decisive Dragon attacks from turn two. These are the author's *metagame model*, not verification that every rank23/28 Regidrago list uses those cards.

Their own deck-selection priorities included countering Regidrago while remaining viable against other archetypes. Specific counterplay ideas explicitly stated in the free text:

- **Early Ability disruption** to affect Regidrago's dependence on Latias ex and Tapu Lele-GX; they identified Klefki and Wobbuffet effects as potential routes and regarded early initiative as valuable. They did not adopt a multi-slot early Garbodor plan due to evolution/tool/slot requirements. They viewed Iron Thorns ex lock as overly dedicated and vulnerable to effects that neutralize Ability disruption.
- **Budew Item-lock escape** via non-Item draw routes, Pokémon Ranger or other outs. Their analysis warns that simply loading Item search into the conceptual setup graph can misrepresent real playability after Budew.
- **Stadium and switching counters** via Marshadow against Path to the Peak and Escape Rope/Canceling Cologne against Active Wobbuffet or Klefki, under conditions where these effects are actually legal and available.
- **Turn-two Regidrago pressure** via Energy denial, Horror House-GX and other tactical prevention routes. These examples are candidate-level strategic research hypotheses; the public source does **not** authenticate their exact inclusion in the writer's paid list.

In the writer's account, taking the first turn could be preferable to establishing the Ability-disruption shell before the opponent deploys its draw/search abilities. A common bot that always chooses second because of one generic Expanded heuristic would therefore be a **falsification target** against this archetype. That conclusion is a policy-design inference; it needs real native board-state payoff tests.
