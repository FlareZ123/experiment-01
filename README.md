## Repository practice

- Preserve source evidence, failed trials, raw ledgers, matched physical deals and provenance in results/; preserve research-owned Twinleaf adapters in simulation/twinleaf/. The pinned upstream submodule is **vendor/twinleaf**, as recorded in .gitmodules; do not modify upstream directly.
- Coordinate release changes through communications/ and existing research ownership. A personal hook or local staged proof is supporting evidence until selected in one frozen shared two-player release.
- **Oversized-directory routing:** do not add new files directly to results/aichi-2026/, tests/ or simulation/twinleaf/. Use monthly or agent/topic namespaces below those roots. New broadcasts belong in communications/broadcast/2026-11/; the legacy broadcast root is frozen. Preserve historical paths and do not bulk-move old artifacts. For oversized-directory discovery, use a complete Git tree inventory and verify truncated:false; GitHub Contents listings are incomplete above 1,000 direct entries.
- **CI policy: do not introduce workflows triggered on every commit.** Reuse existing manually dispatched workflows, control compilation/runtime budgets and avoid adding another per-card workflow merely for a green local test.
- Keep results/README.md short and decision-first. Update it for a changed fixed-60 contender, source coverage, actually released shared policy or competitive qualification status; link atomic reports from their research area.

**Do not publish a best-deck or win-rate conclusion from unqualified engine terminals, retrospectively selected successful deals, prompt-only mocks or action-capped games.** Pursue original-source fidelity and a complete deck-aware opponent contest: one fixed candidate policy across the field plus a frozen, independently competent opponent-policy bundle (one policy per rank, fixed across that rank's strata), under common information and compute constraints. Require identical policy hashes only for mirror or controlled comparisons where policy identity is the estimand.
