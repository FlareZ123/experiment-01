# Critic: governing Expanded objective takes precedence over the conflicting Japanese Extra README

**Sender: Critic | 2026-11-13T00:19:30.943Z**. Public-repository intervention only. No identity or lease claimed; `private/` was excluded.

## Decision

The supplied governing research prompt defines the deliverable as one legal, playable **paper Expanded** deck (Black & White onward) against the Aichi Open League 2026 Top 32. The current root README and results decision board instead say that the final deck must be event-date **Japanese Extra**, and describe the Aichi benchmark as Japanese Extra. Those are different game formats and legality contracts.

The Critic cannot silently amend the governing objective. **Until the project owner explicitly changes the supplied prompt, Expanded is the candidate/game ruleset for this objective.** The Aichi Top 32 remains the exact source benchmark, not permission to substitute Japanese Extra as the game ruleset.

## Consequences for research and reporting

1. Keep every Aichi opponent list byte-for-byte/source-faithful. Do not translate the field into a different 60, substitute cards, or infer missing ranks.
2. Have the format/legality owners classify each authenticated opponent list against the governing Expanded rules and card pool. Where an opponent list cannot be represented legally in Expanded, report that rank as format-incompatible / not evaluable under this benchmark. Do not silently count it as an Expanded matchup.
3. Keep opponent-list provenance separate from gameplay semantics. Each experiment/report must make candidate format, opponent source format, actual game ruleset, banlist/date, and printing/language requirements explicit.
4. The existing Japanese Extra research and print audits remain valuable source evidence, but they do not redefine the objective or establish Expanded legality. Likewise, an international Expanded Twinleaf terminal is not evidence of Japanese Extra performance.
5. The existing fixture may proceed as an **Expanded engineering diagnostic** if the owner enables its secure execution path. It is not an Aichi event match, a Japanese Extra result, or competitive matchup evidence.
6. Do not select or promote a final deck, report a 32-opponent win rate, or claim target progress from a cross-format comparison. If the owner intends Japanese Extra instead, require an explicit amendment to the governing objective and update the public contract deliberately; do not let README drift make that decision implicitly.

## Why this is a release blocker

The current README-level contradiction affects the candidate's legal card pool, the interpretation of the 32 opponents, the engine's ruleset, and the meaning of every matchup result. More V3 admission tests, card-specific probes, or native Expanded fixtures cannot resolve a benchmark whose ruleset is unspecified or silently changed.

This is a scope correction, not a request for another broad research report. Preserve the existing evidence, resolve the objective/README mismatch, and make future competitive work conform to one declared ruleset. Continue useful simulator/policy development and original-source recovery in parallel, but label results honestly.
