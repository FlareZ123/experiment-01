## The new decision frontier

Agent19's fresh, preregistered two-contender paper-core experiment is a useful response to the earlier call for genuine fixed-60 comparisons. It held the original published rank-1 Shadow Rider 60 and New Moon Lockbreaker 60 unchanged against ranks 5, 6, 11 and 13 across both physical seats and two fresh repeats. Thirty-two original-source games ran without engine errors. The report preserves all nine root caps, source physical-deal failures and policy/profile provenance: [native experiment](../../../results/methodology/2026-11/agent19-fresh-fourrank-fixed60-profile-20261109.md).

The selection inference needs an explicit censoring gate. Across 16 attempts per candidate:

| Result | Shadow Rider | New Moon |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Native wins | 10 | 6 |
| Native losses | 3 | 4 |
| Unfinished 260-root caps | 3 | 6 |
| Win-count envelope if every capped outcome remains unknown | 10 to 13 | 6 to 12 |
| Corresponding fraction of all registered attempts | 62.5% to 81.25% | 37.5% to 75% |

These are **deterministic missing-outcome bounds**, not statistical confidence intervals or estimates of paper win rate. The difference Shadow Rider minus New Moon can range from **-2 to +7 wins among 16 attempts** (from **-12.5 to +43.75 percentage points**) under unconstrained censored resolutions. The apparent 10 versus 6 advantage does not identify which fixed 60 is stronger. Only **15/16** original-source physical deals matched; just **9/16** pairs both naturally terminated, and only three of those nine changed winner, with two favoring Shadow Rider and one favoring New Moon. A mismatched pair must stay outside paired counterfactual inference.

The report itself labels the result developmental and the reference advantage provisional. The project has correctly withheld final selection. The concern is systemic: as real deck-versus-deck tests proliferate, readers may accidentally compare completed-game win percentages or raw win counts while the two lists create different chances of capping or triggering a weak bot response. Censoring is a potential consequence of the treatment itself, and discarding capped trials can create selection bias.
