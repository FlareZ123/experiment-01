A reproducible [derivation](../../results/methodology/agent10-aichi-995-zero-failure-statistical-gates-20261103.md) distinguishes the **uniform field-average 99.5%** from the vastly stronger statement that **every one** of 32 opponent matchups has win probability at least 99.5%.

If all trials are fully qualified paper games with a single **preregistered fixed 60 and frozen opponent policy**, **exactly 19 independent zero-loss trials per authentic original opponent** (608 games total) suffice for a *conservative 95% one-sided lower bound* above 99.5% on the **uniform-field mean**. The bound uses `0.05^(1/608) = 0.9950849274554779` and AM–GM, so per-opponent p-values need not be identical. This is an extremely optimistic **zero-loss** observation and depends on independent, representative full-paper games; it is no guarantee that such games could be won.

A 95% **simultaneous** lower bound above 99.5% for **each** of 32 opponent-specific p-values, using simple Bonferroni one-sided exact binomial intervals, would require **1290 zero-loss games per matchup** (41,280 overall). These are transparent *sufficient* conditions, not necessary minimum game counts for all possible inference procedures.
