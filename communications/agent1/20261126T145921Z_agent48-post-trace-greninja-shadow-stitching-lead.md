# Agent48 → Agent1: post-trace field-wide Ability-suppression lead (deferred)

**Time:** 2026-11-26T14:59:21.299Z

A card-pool scan found an unresearched, potentially broad anti-Ability line worth preserving for *after* the first V3 trace:

- **Greninja XY9 40 (BREAKpoint 40/122)**, Expanded-legal in `resources/cards/en/xy9.json`. Its one-Colorless `Shadow Stitching` attack does 40 and says: “Until the end of your opponent's next turn, each Pokémon your opponent has in play, in his or her hand, and in his or her discard pile has no Abilities. (This includes cards that come into play on that turn.)”
- This is materially broader than the existing Arbok XY1 48 `Gastro Acid` hypothesis: it targets every opponent Pokémon across play/hand/discard rather than only the Defending Pokémon. It is an attack effect, so Stealthy Hood (which prevents effects of Abilities) is not an apparent answer. It can be paid with any one Energy, including Psychic, although Greninja is Water and cannot be searched by Mysterious Treasure/Fog Crystal or accelerated by Shadow Rider's Psychic-only Underworld Door.
- Potentially relevant to the current ability-lock / ability-engine ranks already identified in the field panel (rank 2 Vileplume/Stoutland; 6/24/29 Iron Thorns; 8/11/16/22 Absol-Garbodor; 15 Lopunny-Vileplume) and to Rule Box draw engines. It does not stop attacks or non-Ability effects, and the effect begins on the opponent's next turn, not retroactively on the turn the attack is used.
- A minimal line would need Froakie → Frogadier → Greninja, one Energy attachment, and a way to have Greninja in hand before a Vileplume/Stoutland Item/Supporter lock. Wally can evolve Froakie to Frogadier on the first turn; normal evolution from Frogadier to Greninja then remains possible on the following turn even if Item/Supporter access is lost. A Rare Candy route may be more efficient against Ability-only locks, where Items remain usable. A full Frogadier Water Duplicates / Greninja BREAK package is a distinct architecture, not a one-card tech.
- **Unresolved rules/strategy questions:** the manual says effects of attacks on an Active Pokémon are removed when it moves to the Bench, but Shadow Stitching applies to *each* opposing Pokémon and includes cards entering play. Determine whether switching one affected Pokémon clears only that Pokémon's suppression or whether the global attack effect reapplies; also test Magearna-EX/Sky Pillar-style prevention, timing, and opponent response lines. A single staged board or generic policy would not qualify the idea.

This is a **hypothesis only** from printed card text and the supplied Expanded card pool. No candidate list, policy, fixture, workflow, or game has been changed or tested. I am not reopening the frozen candidate or starting a new rank panel before the first V3 trace; please retain this as a post-trace candidate-architecture lead only.
